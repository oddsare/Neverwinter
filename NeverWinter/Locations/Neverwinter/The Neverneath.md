# The Neverneath — "The Endless Maze"

**Location:** Magically protected catacombs beneath [[Locations/Neverwinter/Districts/Protectors Enclave|Castle Never]]
**Also known as:** The Endless Maze
**Status (1525 DR):** Active, sealed, untouched since the 1502 disappearance of [[NPCs/Dagult Neverember|Lord Dagult Neverember]]
**Threat level:** Lethal to anyone except a true Alagondar heir (and even they should bring backup)

---

## Canon overview

The Neverneath is the **magically protected substructure** of Castle Never, originally built to serve two purposes:

1. **Maintain the structural integrity** of the castle above (the maze is also the foundation).
2. **Protect the buried corpse of Lord [[Lore/Ancient History|Halueth Never]]** — the sun elf founder of Eigersstor / Neverwinter, c. -10 DR.

After the **Spellplague of 1385 DR**, wild magic altered the protective ward. The ward gained **malevolent sentience and the ability to reshape corridors at will.**

It now **bides its time** — visitors may proceed apparently undisturbed at first — before trapping them in an **infinite magical corridor loop** full of **deadly traps and animated statues** that attack anything that moves. Explorers can wander for days without finding an exit.

> **The antagonist is the ward itself, not a ghost.** Halueth Never's corpse remains undisturbed, protected by the corrupted guardian it predated. Sources that frame the Neverneath as "the corrupted spirit of Halueth Never" are wrong; canon treats it as a **sapient magical effect bonded to the castle's stones.**

**Sources:** *Neverwinter Campaign Setting* (4e, 2011), ~pp. 134-138; *Lost Crown of Neverwinter* (D&D Encounters Season 6, 2011); FR Wiki "Castle Never" and "Halueth Never."

## Named features (canon)

### The Crypt of the Neverwinter Nine

A **circular chamber roughly 200 ft across**, lit by **everburning torches.** Houses the entombed remains of the **[[NPCs/Renaer Neverember#The_Neverwinter_Nine|Neverwinter Nine]]** — the elite bodyguards of King Nasher Alagondar — in nine marble coffins surrounding a central throne.

At the chamber's heart: **the Inner Vault.**

### The Inner Vault

Sealed behind a **great stone door bearing the Sigil of Neverwinter**, warded with potent magic. Contents and exact mechanism not specified in canon, but the Sigil is Alagondar dynastic regalia and the Vault is widely speculated to be the resting place of the **[[Lore/The Lost Crown and Hidden Alagondar Vaults|Lost Crown of Neverwinter]].**

Canonical bloodline-locked vault candidate. (Crown of Neverwinter is canonically bloodline-locked; the Inner Vault sharing that property is canon-extrapolated.)

### The Tomb of Halueth Never

Deep within the labyrinth. **Halueth Never's body lies undecayed on a great stone slab**, ringed by **twelve unsheathed magical swords of differing types, points outward.**

- The swords **never rust or decay.**
- They **animate to attack any trespasser** unless cryptic instructions inscribed on the surrounding flagstones are followed precisely.
- The corpse itself does not animate or interact (in published 4e/5e tabletop canon).

*Note:* the *Neverwinter Nights 2* video game features a variant version of this tomb — **eight statues of the Neverwinter Nine, one absent, and nine floating swords.** Treat as secondary/video-game canon if conflicts arise.

### Halueth Never's corpse

- A sun elf, dead since the founding-era (~ -10 DR).
- Undecayed despite ~1500+ years.
- Why: protected by the Neverneath's wards (originally) and now by the corrupted-but-functional ward.
- **Significance for the Alagondar bloodline question:** Halueth predates the Alagondars by over a millennium. If the Inner Vault's bloodline check uses Halueth's line, even a "true Alagondar" might not pass it.

## Inhabitants

- **Gargoyles** — canonical denizens, patrol the corridors.
- **Animated statues** — generic, scattered throughout the maze.
- **The twelve animated swords** at Halueth's tomb.
- **Ghosts of those who died during the 1451 Ruining** — bleed down from the upper castle into the Neverneath. Canonical.
- **No canon-confirmed survivors of unauthorized entry** in published 4e/5e tabletop material. The maze's reputation precedes itself for good reason.

## The ward — how it actually works

Canon language frames the ward as **a sapient magical effect**, not a creature or a trapped spirit. Implications for play:

- **It has intent.** It chooses when to spring traps, when to reshape, when to let alone.
- **It is bonded to the stones.** Cannot be killed by killing a creature — would require dismantling or recasting the original magic.
- **Destroying it compromises the castle.** The maze IS the foundation. Bringing down the Neverneath brings down Castle Never.
- **It is paranoid.** Spellplague wild-magic corruption turned a defensive ward into an aggressive one. It treats everyone as a potential threat until proven otherwise.
- **Possibly reasonable.** A sentient ward might respond to language, ritual, or proof of Alagondar lineage. The party could potentially *talk to it* — though it would likely test them first.

## Campaign hooks

### Dagult Neverember IS in here, alive (LOCKED 2026-05-15)

[[NPCs/Dagult Neverember|Lord Dagult Neverember]] entered the Neverneath alone in **1502 DR**, intending to claim the Lost Crown to lock in his rule against a rising tide of opposition — Sons of Alagondar agitation, a Luskan assassination plot, and Indrina Lamsensettle's exposé all closing in at once. **The Crown was about keeping his power, not protecting citizens.** He **didn't believe the bloodline-lock legend was real.**

The corrupted ward caught him before he reached the Inner Vault. **23 years later he is still inside**, alive, aged but functional, lucid but changed. Halueth Never's residual protective magic has incidentally kept him fed, watered, and sane.

He has not become a better man in the dark. Twenty-three years alone with his thoughts has only deepened his bitterness. He still believes he is owed the throne. **He is a captive villain, not a victim to be rescued.**

**He knows things the party needs:**
- The 1502 Luskan assassination plot — names, dates, what was at stake (may still operate in 1525 with successors)
- Where [[NPCs/Indrina Lamsensettle|Indrina Lamsensettle]]'s dossier is
- The identities of rival Alagondar claimants he was hunting (some may still be alive)
- What the ward told him on the way down

**He will not share any of it freely.** He'll bargain — for restoration to power, for safety, for his freedom. He'll lie about anything he can. The party will have to **interrogate**, **coerce**, or **outmaneuver** him.

Recovering him is a campaign-defining choice, **not a triumph.** Bringing him back into the city is its own political crisis. He cannot be brought out by force; the ward must be persuaded, or a true Alagondar heir must escort him out. Once out, the party must decide whether to turn him over to [[NPCs/Renaer Neverember|Renaer]] (who doesn't want him), to Waterdeep, to the Sons of Alagondar, or to a quieter fate.

### Finding the Crown

If the Lost Crown is in the Inner Vault (canon-strong inference), reaching it requires:

1. Surviving the Neverneath's corridors.
2. Bypassing or defeating the gargoyles and animated statues.
3. Opening the Inner Vault's bloodline-locked seal.
4. Wearing the Crown without being a fraudulent claimant (or it kills you with cold/heat).

Each step is a multi-session arc.

### The twelve swords

The flagstones around Halueth Never's tomb bear cryptic instructions. **Solving the puzzle disarms the swords; failing animates them.** Twelve magical swords (varying types) are a serious encounter — but if disarmed, they're also a major treasure.

### Talking to the ward

If the party can determine the ward is sentient and convince it they are not threats (or are authorized), they could pass through unmolested. Mechanisms might include:

- Speaking the True Name of Halueth Never (if it can be discovered).
- Demonstrating Alagondar bloodline.
- Bringing a relic the ward recognizes.
- Recasting the original protective magic, restoring the ward to its original purpose.
