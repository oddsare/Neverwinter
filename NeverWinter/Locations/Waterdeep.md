# Waterdeep: The City of Splendors

**Also Known As:** The City of Splendors, the Crown of the North
**Region:** Sword Coast North
**Size:** Metropolis. Population 1,347,680 in 1372 DR, up to ~2,000,000 in the 15th century DR
**Government:** Oligarchic city-state
**Ruler (c. 1489 DR):** Open Lord Laeral Silverhand
**Real power:** The secret council of Masked Lords, with the guilds and the nobility close behind
**Lords' Alliance influence:** Highest. It leads the Alliance.

> Canon reference only, as of about 1489–1492 DR. The campaign is set in 1505 DR, so who rules here now is Brodie's call. See [[Factions/Lords' Alliance]].

---

## Exposition

Waterdeep is the most important city in the North and perhaps in all of Faerûn. It sits on the Sword Coast, sheltered on the seaward side by Mount Waterdeep, whose watchtowers are patrolled by the Griffon Cavalry. Strong walls guard its landward side. Paved, patrolled roads run north (the Long Road and the High Road) and south (the Trade Way). Its claim extends about 100 miles beyond its walls. Goods from the mineral-rich North, the merchant kingdoms of Amn and Calimshan, the Inner Sea and the sea kingdoms to the west all pass through its markets and Deepwater Harbor. It is even the largest spelljamming port in Faerûn.

The city draws the Realms' best artisans, artists and scholars. Waterdhavians are social, stalwart and outspoken, with a worldly outlook. They chase wealth and status but will not stand for military conquest. They respect the law, especially laws about trade and property, and their courts are famously accurate.

Waterdeep is ruled by masks. Only one Lord shows a face in public: the Open Lord. The rest sit hidden behind enchanted helms, and guessing their identities is a favourite game among the nobility, made harder because the Lords spread their own rumours. Meanwhile the guilds control much of the city's economy and 76 noble families scheme over old bloodlines. People say the Lords rule Waterdeep but don't always run it.

## Who Rules

### The Lords of Waterdeep
- A mostly anonymous council that has governed since **1032 DR** (Ahghairon's coup). A full council is about **20 Lords**, but the number has ranged from 16 to 29 (16 in 1368 DR, 21 in 1372 DR). Some Lords are kept secret even from the city's records.
- **Masked Lords** appear in public only when sitting in the Lord's Court, or every four years at the **Shieldmeet Council**, where the Lords' Rule is reaffirmed. They know each other's identities. Anonymity is meant to protect them from coercion and to let Lords come from any class.
- **The Open Lord** is the only Lord whose identity is public. Titles: Warden of Waterdeep, Overmaster of the Guilds, Commander of the Watch. They are the city's chief diplomat. Powers reserved to the Open Lord:
  - vetting and choosing new Lords
  - naming the next Open Lord
  - setting how many Lords there are
- **Becoming a Lord:** the Masked Lords draw up a shortlist and the Open Lord meets each candidate privately. Joining is voluntary. If the Masked Lords are all unable to act, certain others can choose Lords instead: the head of the Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors, some guildmasters, the Open Lord, the Blackstaff, and the heads of twelve noble families (Argent, Belabranta, Brokengulf, Estelmer, Helmfast, Jardeth, Melshimber, Piiradost, Ralnarth, Silvertor, Tesper, Thongolir).
- **Open Lords up to 1489 DR:** Ahghairon the Mage, Baeron Silmaeril, Lhestyn the Masked Lady, Piergeiron the Paladinson, Caladorn Cassalanter, Dagult Neverember, Laeral Silverhand.

### The Ruler: Laeral Silverhand
- **Age:** born 765 DR, so about 724 in 1489 DR and 740 in 1505 DR. She is ageless, one of Mystra's Chosen.
- **Known for:**
  - She is the fifth of Mystra's **Seven Sisters**.
  - She was once the **Witch-Queen of the North**, enslaved by the Crown of Horns.
  - She was married to **Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun**.
  - In 1371 DR she led the Alliance army to relieve Evereska.
  - She became Open Lord reluctantly in 1488–1489 DR, at the Masked Lords' request, after Dagult was exiled.
  - She is famously organized and friendly. She mediates with rogues and monsters and keeps a cover identity in Skullport.
  - Her magic has weakened since Mystra's death and rebirth, so she relies on deputies.
  - 5e stats: chaotic good, 19th-level wizard.

### Other powers in the city
- **The guilds:** they once ruled the city and nearly destroyed it in the Guildwars of 1256–1262 DR. No one wants that again. Guild Law runs alongside city law (see below).
- **The nobility:** 76 families, most tracing their lines back before the city's founding. Examples include the Cassalanters (moneylenders), the Wands (wizards) and the Amcathras.
- **Crime:** no thieves' guild has openly operated since the early 14th century DR, because criminals can never be sure their partners aren't secret Lords. By 1492 DR the **Xanathar Guild** led by a beholder was the biggest criminal group, competing with Zhentarim factions.

## Laws & Justice

Waterdeep is called a city of laws "molded by Tyr's spirit of justice." The law comes from three places:
- **The Code Legal**, available in many languages at the Palace and in Common from Magisters at every gate and at Deepwater Harbor.
- **The Edicts of the Masked Lords.**
- **Many unwritten laws.**

### The Code Legal
Crimes are split into four **Plaints**:
1. **Crimes Against the Lords.** Renamed *Crimes Against Lords, Officials, and Nobles* by 1492 DR, when nobles gained more protection.
2. **Crimes Against the City.**
3. **Crimes Against the Gods.**
4. **Crimes Against Citizens.**

**Punishments, harshest first (older code):** instant death, death on conviction, exile, mutilation (loss of the offending body part, or branding), flogging, hard labour, imprisonment in Castle Waterdeep's dungeons, imprisonment with light work in the Castle compound, a fine to the city, damages to the victim, and an **edict** (a public order forbidding the convicted from doing something).

**Changes by 1492 DR:** flogging replaced mutilation, the two kinds of death sentence merged into one, and crimes were no longer graded by severity.

**Exile into Undermountain:** a person sentenced to death could sometimes choose permanent exile into Undermountain instead. About 10% survived (1368 DR), and anyone caught back in the city was executed.

### Sample sentences
**First Plaint (Lords, officials and nobles):**
- **Assaulting a Lord:** treason, punished by death.
- **Murdering a Lord, official or noble:** death (made a separate crime by 1492 DR).
- **Impersonating a Lord:** death.
- **Bribing an official:** exile up to 20 years, plus a fine of up to double the bribe (1492 DR). Bribery is rare because it ruins reputations, and the rich usually bribe with property rather than coin.
- **Blackmailing an official:** flogging and exile up to 10 years.
- **Using magic to influence a Lord without consent:** prison up to a year, plus up to 1,000 gp (added by 1492 DR).
- **Disobeying an edict:** exile up to 5 years or a fine of up to 1,000 gp.
- **Blasphemy against a Lord, Magister or city officer:** 4 days of light work and 120 gp. Magisters have ruled that printing *misquotes* of officials in broadsheets counts as blasphemy, though other lies and libel are allowed.

**Other offences:**
- **Theft, vandalism or arson against the Palace or the City Walls:** exile, plus the cost of repairs and 2,000 gp.
- **Impersonating a Guardsman or Watch officer:** dungeon, a 5,000 gp fine and flogging.

### Other laws
- **Smokepowder is banned.** Only the City Guard and a few mages of the Watchful Order may have any, and only in small amounts. (Compare [[Lore/The Powder]].)
- **Drugs:** a Lords' Edict bans making and selling drugs, but using or owning them isn't illegal. In practice only commoners get charged, on the assumption that they're dealing, unless they can show they work for the Guild of Apothecaries & Physicians.
- **Guild Law:** rules made by the guilds. It can't override city law, but Magisters and the Watch enforce it, so many treat it as equal.
- **Heraldry:** an edict forbids copying or mocking the arms of the city, its Lords or its nobles. Only those with written rights can order garments bearing them.
- **Taxes:** a monthly tax of **1 shard (1 sp) per person over age ten**, collected by the City Guard on the last day of each month. It began in the first year of **Dagult Neverember's** time as Open Lord. Paying 1 gold dragon buys a year's exemption, shown by a writ. Dodging the tax on collection day is so common that the streets fill with "taxing traffic." Guilds pay a per-head tax for their members.
- **Bounty hunting** has been legal since the **Tarnsmoke Proclamation** of 1400 DR.
- **Debts:** debtors usually have 20 days to pay, often working them off for the city on sewers, roads or walls. In extreme cases they can serve as temporary soldiers to clear the debt. Seizing property needs a Lord's approval. Debts must be proven in writing, so nearly every merchant keeps careful records, even of illegal trade, using false item names.
- **Spelljammers** must land on the ocean several miles out and approach by sea. Breaking this rule brings heavy fines and imprisonment.

### How justice works
- **City Watch:** the police. Crimes can be reported to any Watch officer.
- **City Guard:** the army and gate guard.
- **Above them:** Force Grey (an elite dozen spellcasters and warriors) and sometimes the Red Sashes.
- **Charges:** only city officers may charge someone. Temples may not bring charges under the Third Plaint.
- **Trials:** the accused is held at a courthouse until a **Magister** (called a "Black Robe") hears the case in the Common Courts. **There is no bail.** Each Magister travels with at least six City Guards. Magisters can judge on the spot except for Severe crimes. **Detect lie** spells make verdicts very accurate.
- **No lawyers:** they are banned. Instead you hire a "professional witness" (a *lawspeaker*), usually a skilled speaker or actor rather than a legal expert.
- **Appeals:** the convicted can appeal to the Lords' Court, but a frivolous appeal brings a harsher sentence. A Lord can dismiss charges, though this is very rare.
- **Public support:** support for a strong police force is high, because the city pays for any innocent deaths the Watch or Guard cause.

> Vault note: [[Lore/Code Legal of Neverwinter|Neverwinter's Code Legal]] uses the same four-Plaint layout as Waterdeep's 1492 DR code, with a few different fines. For example, using magic to influence a Lord carries up to 2,000 gp in Neverwinter against 1,000 gp in Waterdeep.

## People & Society
- **Races:** about 64% human, 10% dwarf, 10% elf (mostly moon elves), 5% halfling, 5% half-elf, 3% gnome, 2% half-orc.
- **Faiths:** Deneir, Mystra, Oghma and Tempus lead.
- **Customs:** cooking contests are common on market and festival days. Savoury pies and sausages are the favourites.
- **Adventurers:** a large native population of adventurers helps defend the city.

## Economy
- **Imports:** grain, livestock, leather, ore, timber and exotic goods.
- **Exports:** ale, weapons, cloth, fish, furniture, leather goods, pottery, refined metals and every kind of finished goods.
- **Currency:** platinum *sun*, gold *dragon*, electrum *sambar*, silver *shard*, copper *nib*.
- **Construction stone:** comes from Mirabar, shipped through Luskan by an expensive magical process.

## Defense
- **City Guard and City Watch:** see "How justice works" above.
- **Force Grey:** the elite troubleshooters.
- **Griffon Cavalry:** patrols Mount Waterdeep and the docks.
- **Walls and terrain:** strong landward walls, with Mount Waterdeep's fortified heights on the sea side.

## Relations
- **Alliances:** leader of the [[Factions/Lords' Alliance|Lords' Alliance]]. The Lords are allied with [[Factions/The Harpers|the Harpers]].
- **Enemies:** the Knights of the Shield, the Shadow Thieves of Amn and the Confluence.
- **Rivals:** Baldur's Gate outcompeted Waterdeep in trade in the 15th century. [[Lore/Sword Coast Reference|Mirabar and Tarmalune]] are commercial rivals.
- **Neverwinter:** Dagult was ousted as Open Lord in 1489 DR for spending Waterdeep's treasury on rebuilding Neverwinter. See [[NPCs/Dagult Neverember]].
- **This campaign (1492 DR):** *Dragon Heist* went Manshoon's way. A party with Renaer foiled Manshoon's scheme by beating his [[NPCs/Manshoon's Simulacrum|simulacrum]] (they never faced [[NPCs/Manshoon|Manshoon]] himself), recovered Dagult's hidden hoard of 500,000 gold dragons from the Vault of Dragons, and returned it to Waterdeep. See [[Campaign Decisions]].
