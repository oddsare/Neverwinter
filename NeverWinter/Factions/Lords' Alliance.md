# The Lords' Alliance

**Type:** Political and trade coalition of city-states
**Also called:** The Council of Lords
**Emblem:** A golden crown on a field of red
**Founded:** 1325 DR
**Leader (canon):** Open Lord Laeral Silverhand of Waterdeep, 1488 DR to the end of published canon
**Scope:** The Sword Coast, the North and the Western Heartlands
**Status:** Active (canon). Its state in 1505 DR is Brodie's call.

> Canon reference only. Everything here comes from published Forgotten Realms material, mostly as summarized by the Forgotten Realms Wiki, and reflects **c. 1489 DR**. Most published canon covers the 1480s–1490s DR, and a few recent books reach about 1501–1505 DR. 1525 DR is past all of it. Who sits on the council in 1505 DR is not decided yet (see the open questions at the end).

---

## Overview

The Lords' Alliance is a partnership of free cities and towns. Waterdeep, Silverymoon, Baldur's Gate and Neverwinter are the big names, and smaller towns make up most of the membership. It is **not a government**: each member rules itself. Members often disagree and chase their own interests, but they set rivalries aside when a real threat endangers them all.

**Why it was formed:** to oppose the merchant houses of Amn. Later it also stood against the Zhentarim (the Black Network), the Shadow Thieves of Amn, orc hordes and Northlander raiders.

---

## How It Works

- **Council of Lords:** each member city sends its ruler or a spokesperson. Bigger cities carry more weight.
- **Leader:** the Open Lord of Waterdeep usually acts as head of the Alliance. Leaders on record: Lord Piergeiron (as of 1358 DR), Dagult Neverember (about 1467–1488 DR), Laeral Silverhand (1488 DR onward).
- **Treaties and pledges:** members bind themselves with **trade treaties** and **pledges to keep each other safe**, especially along the main trade routes.
- **One voice abroad:** the Alliance bargains with foreign trading partners as a group. It also sets members' shared policy toward groups the North has long feared, such as goblinoids and orc tribes.
- **Military:** the Alliance takes charge when a campaign across the North or the Heartlands is needed.
- **Revel's End:** the Alliance runs this isolated prison in the Frozenfar, which holds some of the worst criminals of the North and the Sword Coast.
- **Adventurers:** it hires them for scouting, information gathering and raids on Zhentarim outposts.
- **Agent ranks (lowest to highest):** Cloak, Redknife, Stingblade, Warduke, Lioncrown.
- **Guards:** circa 1372 DR, the Order of Tye, a knightly order, guarded ranking members when they travelled outside allied cities.

## Member Cities

### Waterdeep
**The City of Splendors, the Crown of the North**

- **Stands for:** trade, culture and order. It is the most important city in the North and perhaps in all of Faerûn, and draws the best artisans, artists and scholars in the Realms. It is the financial hub of the coast.
- **People:** about 64% human, 10% dwarf, 10% elf, 5% halfling, 5% half-elf, 3% gnome and 2% half-orc. Population was 1,347,680 in 1372 DR and up to about 2,000,000 in the 15th century DR. Main faiths are Deneir, Mystra, Oghma and Tempus.
- **Government:** an oligarchic city-state. The public Open Lord rules with the anonymous Masked Lords, and the Magisters act as judges.
- **Economy:** imports grain, livestock, ore and timber. Exports finished goods of every kind.
- **Influence: Highest.** It is the most powerful member and its Open Lord leads the Alliance.

**Ruler: Laeral Silverhand, Open Lord of Waterdeep**
- **Age:** born 765 DR, which makes her 724 in 1489 DR and 740 in 1505 DR. As one of Mystra's Chosen, she doesn't age.
- **Known for:**
  - She is the fifth of the **Seven Sisters**, daughters of Mystra.
  - She was once the **Witch-Queen of the North**. While enslaved by the Crown of Horns she was forced to do terrible things.
  - She was married to **Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun**.
  - In 1371 DR she led an Alliance relief army to Evereska.
  - She took the Open Lord post reluctantly, at the Masked Lords' request, after Dagult was ousted.
  - She is famously organized and friendly, and she often mediates with rogues and monsters, keeping a cover identity in Skullport.
  - Her magic has weakened since Mystra's death and rebirth.
  - 5e stats: chaotic good, 19th-level wizard.
- Vault link: [[NPCs/Arveiaturace - The White Wyrm|Arveiaturace]] already asks whether Laeral is still Open Lord in 1505.

### Neverwinter
**The City of Skilled Hands, the Jewel of the North**

- **Stands for:** craftsmanship and gardens, and rebuilding after the Mount Hotenow disaster. Volo called it the most cosmopolitan and civilized city in Faerûn. It is a member in good standing that **always sides with Waterdeep** and stands against [[Locations/Luskan|Luskan]] and the orcs.
- **People:** humans and half-elves with a dwarf minority (c. 1372 DR). By c. 1479 DR there were also dragonborn, eladrin, tieflings, orcs and half-orcs. Population was 23,192 in 1372 DR (the vault sets ~50,000 for 1505 DR). Faiths include Asmodeus, Bahamut, Helm, Kelemvor, Oghma, Selûne, Torm, Tyr and Waukeen.
- **Government:** a lordship. In earlier eras it was a monarchy under the Alagondars.
- **Economy:** exports crafts (notably water clocks and coloured-glass lamps), fish, garden produce, timber and magical innovations. It imports crafters and mercenaries.
- **Influence: High.** It was a major member historically, but its power was cut by the 1451 DR eruption.

**Ruler (c. 1489): Dagult Neverember, Lord Protector**
- **Age:** born 1429 DR, so 60 in 1489 DR and 76 in 1505 DR. This matches [[NPCs/Dagult Neverember|his vault note]].
- **Known for:**
  - His nicknames are "Dagger" Neverember and "the Lord Pretender."
  - He claimed Alagondar descent and garrisoned Neverwinter with Mintarn mercenaries.
  - He led the Alliance from about 1467 to 1488 DR.
  - He was ousted as Open Lord of Waterdeep for using its treasury to rebuild Neverwinter.
  - He is Renaer's father.
  - 5e stats: lawful neutral fighter.
- **In this campaign:** Dagult is missing and [[NPCs/Renaer Neverember|Renaer]] rules. Neverwinter's seat in 1505 DR is not decided yet.
- **Date check:** the wiki says Laeral took over the Alliance in **1488 DR**, while the vault has Dagult ousted as Open Lord in **1489 DR**. Canon sources split the same way.

### Silverymoon
**The Gem of the North, the Welcoming City**

- **Stands for:** learning, the arts (especially music) and magic. It is known for **unity between humans and the other races**, which it owes to Alustriel's long rule, and it is seen as an echo of Myth Drannor.
- **People:** 41% human, 29% elf, 12% half-elf, 10% shield dwarf, 5% lightfoot halfling and 2% gnome. It is a metropolis. Faiths include Lathander, Oghma, Milil, Selûne, Helm, Mielikki, Silvanus, Tymora and Mystra. **Worship of Cyric is outlawed.**
- **Government:** a constitutional magocratic republic, meaning wizards rule under a constitution.
- **Economy:** exports Silverymoon Blue wine, books, paper, ink, herbs, glass, furs, timber and ore. It imports food, armour, weapons and cloth.
- **Influence: High.** It is the cultural and magical heart of the North. Its regional power base weakened when **Luruar** (the Silver Marches confederation) collapsed in 1488 DR.

**Ruler (c. 1489): Taern "Thunderspell" Hornblade, High Mage**
- **Age:** born 1204 DR, making him about 285 in 1489 DR and 301 in 1505 DR. The wiki chose 1204 as a compromise because sources conflict. He has stayed healthy with **potions of longevity**.
- **Known for:**
  - He succeeded Alustriel as High Mage.
  - In 3e he is an 18th-level evoker and lawful good.
  - He is a longtime **Harper**.
  - He founded the **Spellguard** of Silverymoon in 1255 DR to protect Alustriel.
  - He fought a dracolich on the city walls in 1361 DR.
  - He made cloaks of protection against chaos for the city's defenders against an orc army.
  - Luruar sent him to the Council of Waterdeep in the 1480s DR because of his diplomatic skill.
  - He generally follows Alustriel's wishes.
- Vault link: [[NPCs/Ophala Cheldarstorn|Ophala]]'s Many-Starred Cloak is based here.

### Baldur's Gate
**The Gate, the City of Blood, "halfway to everywhere"**

- **Stands for:** profit and opportunity. It has long been a neutral trading power, tolerant of outsiders from rich merchants to refugees to smugglers and pirates.
- **People:** mostly human, about 125,000 in 1492 DR. Faiths include Gond, Tymora, Umberlee, Helm, Lathander, Ilmater, and cults of the Dead Three.
- **Government:** a plutocracy, meaning the wealthy rule. Power sits with the Council of Four (the dukes) and the Parliament of Peers. The Watch and the **Flaming Fist** mercenary company keep order.
- **Economy:** exports dyes, fish, lamp oil and trade coins. It imports stone.
- **Influence: High.** It is the Alliance's southern anchor and one of the richest merchant cities on the coast.

**Ruler (c. 1489): Ulder Ravengard, Grand Duke and Marshal of the Flaming Fist**
- **Age:** canon gives no birth year. A fan wiki says 1440 DR, which would make him about 49 in 1489 DR and 65 in 1505 DR. This is **unconfirmed**.
- **Known for:**
  - He was born poor in the Lower City and joined the Flaming Fist young.
  - He became Marshal in 1482 DR after Abdel Adrian died, and was elected Grand Duke before 1488 DR.
  - He kept command of the Fist, which made him the most powerful person in the city.
  - He worships Torm. His wife is Francesca and his son is **Wyll**, the companion from *Baldur's Gate 3*.
  - He was Baldur's Gate's lead delegate at the Council of Waterdeep against the Cult of the Dragon.
  - In 1492 DR he was in Elturel when it was dragged into Avernus. Later he was imprisoned beneath the Gray Harbor, and **Enver Gortash** succeeded him (*Baldur's Gate 3*).
  - 5e stats: lawful neutral, 11th-level fighter.

### Mirabar
**City of the Mines**

- **Stands for:** wealth from metal. As of 1368 DR it was **the richest city north of Waterdeep**. Its rulers will **threaten clients with reduced output** to get their way. The vault notes it as a commercial rival of Waterdeep.
- **People:** humans and shield dwarves. The dwarves live underground beside their workshops. The humans above move the ore to market and defend the city against magical threats. Population was about 23,000 through 1370 DR (4,100 of them dwarves in 1358) and 10,307 in 1372 DR. Faiths are Chauntea, Tymora and the dwarven gods.
- **Government:** the hereditary **Marchion** handles foreign policy only. Real power sits with the elected **Council of Sparkling Stones**, a mixed dwarf and human assembly that meets once a year to set production quotas.
- **Influence: Moderate.** It is small in people but strong in leverage, because the North depends on its ore.

**Ruler (after 1485): Selin Ramur, Marchion**
- **Age:** not given in canon.
- **Known for:**
  - He is the city's figurehead and a **key member** of the Alliance, but he rules only Mirabar's foreign policy.
  - *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide* spells his name **"Selin Raurym,"** which would make him a descendant of the earlier Marchion Elastul Raurym.
  - He is Damaran, lawful neutral, and has noble stats in 5e.

### Mithral Hall
**Stronghold of Clan Battlehammer**

- **Stands for:** the dwarven kingdom and the legacy of Clan Battlehammer and Bruenor. It is the dwarven shield of the North and the source of its mithral.
- **People:** 81% shield dwarf, 7% svirfneblin (deep gnome), 5% gold dwarf, 4% human and 3% urdunnir dwarf. Population was 4,991 in 1372 DR. It worships the dwarven gods.
- **Government:** a monarchy. Its rulers, in order: Bruenor, Gandalug, Bruenor again (1370–1409 DR), Banak, Connerad (1445–1486 DR), then Dagnabbet (1486 DR onward).
- **Economy:** mithral, mithral goods and iron.
- **Influence: Moderate.** It has military weight and mithral, but the dwarves pulled out of Luruar in 1488 DR.

**Ruler (from 1486): Queen Dagnabbet Waybeard**
- **Age:** not given. Dwarves live for centuries, so she could plausibly still rule in 1505 DR.
- **Known for:**
  - She is the **first queen** of Mithral Hall.
  - As King Connerad's general during the Darkening (1484 DR), she **turned back the first attack by the orcs of Many-Arrows** through sharp tactics.
  - She is the daughter of Dagnabbit and granddaughter of **Dagna**, Bruenor's general.

### Yartar
**River-trade city of the Dessarin**

- **Stands for:** trade around the clock. Caravans and river barges pass through its walls day and night. It has allies across the North.
- **People:** population about 6,000 in 1366 DR, and more crowded since. A **criminal element**, the **Hand of Yartar**, holds the city back from becoming truly influential. It worships Tymora.
- **Government:** a barony ruled by a **Waterbaron**.
- **Economy:** exports barges and depends on trade. It imports food.
- **Influence: Moderate to low.** It has wealth and connections but crime eats at it.

**Ruler (c. 1491): Nestra Ruthiol, Waterbaron**
- **Age:** in her fifties around 1491 DR (*Princes of the Apocalypse*), so roughly 65–75 in 1505 DR.
- **Known for:**
  - She is **lawful evil**, cunning and vindictive.
  - She sees Alliance membership as vital to Yartar's survival.
  - She lets both the Harpers and the Zhentarim into the city but deals with them only when she must.
  - She was accused of **murdering a former lover**, Kaidrod Palyr, after a public argument with his wife. She was cleared, and some suspect the Hand of Yartar was involved.
  - She ruled during the Elemental Evil crisis of 1491 DR (*Princes of the Apocalypse*).
  - She is Tethyrian, with noble stats in 5e.

### Daggerford
**Small town of the Delimbiyr Vale**

- **Stands for:** stubborn independence. It is a relic of the old kingdom of Delimbiyr that has never fallen despite repeated wars. It wants to grow and be seen as an alternative to Waterdeep, a goal that has given it an inflated sense of its own importance.
- **People:** humans, dwarves and halflings, about 1,200 in 1485 DR, most of them farmers. Faiths are Amaunator, Chauntea, Lathander, Tempus and Tymora.
- **Government:** an oligarchy. A Duke or Duchess rules alongside a Council of Guilds.
- **Economy:** exports furs and clothing. It imports green wood.
- **Influence: Low.** Its value is as a voice on the council and a stop on the Trade Way.

**Ruler (from c. 1486): Duchess Morwen Daggerford**
- **Age:** about 40 c. 1485 DR, so roughly 44 in 1489 DR and 60 in 1505 DR.
- **Known for:**
  - She was Master of Arms and military commander of the castle, and she trains with the militia and visits Tempus's shrine.
  - Many thought her **a better ruler than her brother Duke Maldwyn**. She inherited the title when Daggerford suspended inheritance by the eldest son.
  - Gruff on the surface, she has a noble heart.
  - Early in her reign Baroness Wynne Cromm captured her, and a **succubus named Pencheska ruled Daggerford in her place**.
  - 5e stats: lawful good fighter.
- **Warning:** around 1485 DR the succubus **Pencheska**, working for Tarul Var of Thay, imprisoned Morwen in Cromm's Hold and ruled in her likeness. As of 1489 DR, canon leaves open whether the duchess is the real Morwen. See [[Locations/Daggerford]].

### Longsaddle
**Hamlet of the Harpells**

- **Stands for:** nothing grand. It is a quiet ranching hamlet on the Long Road, a member of the Alliance **only because the powerful, eccentric Harpell family of wizards** lives there, in the Ivy Mansion.
- **People:** humans, about 130 in the hamlet and 1,100 in the surrounding area (1358–1366 DR).
- **Government:** a council of elders, with a Harpell as spokesman.
- **Economy:** beef and mutton.
- **Influence: Low** politically, but its magical punch is far beyond its size.

**Ruler (c. 1489): Dowell Harpell, Spokesman**
- **Age:** not given.
- **Known for:**
  - He took the Harpell name when he married **Penelope Harpell**. They met when she saved him from being eaten during a fight with a hill giant king.
  - He is a skilled enchanter but a notably poor evoker.
  - He helped Penelope teach magic to the reborn **Catti-brie** (*Legend of Drizzt* novels).

### Amphail
**Waterdeep's horse country**

- **Stands for:** feeding Waterdeep and breeding fine horses, including Amphail grays, black chargers and Amphail fancys. It is named after Amphail the Just, one of Waterdeep's early warlords. In summer it smells of horse manure.
- **People:** about 850 (600 in the village and 250 on outlying farms), mostly human with a few half-elves, dwarves and halflings. It worships Chauntea.
- **Government:** a **Lord Warder**. One source says the office rotates among three leading families (Amcathra, Ilzimmer and Roaringhorn), changing every Shieldmeet.
- **Influence: Low.** It sits in Waterdeep's orbit.

**Ruler (from c. 1485): Dauner Ilzimmer, Lord Warder**
- **Age:** not given.
- **Known for:**
  - He is a **bombastic family man who loves horses more than people**.
  - He used to live in Waterdeep.
  - He is Chondathan, lawful neutral, with noble stats in 5e.

## Allies & Enemies (canon)

**Allies (as of 1488 DR):** [[Factions/The Harpers|The Harpers]], the Emerald Enclave, the Order of the Gauntlet. The Zhentarim are also listed, even though they were the Alliance's enemy in the 14th century. The Alliance had a mutual-aid pledge with a spellcasting order called the Guardians of the Weave.

**Enemies:** [[Locations/Luskan|Luskan]], the Shadow Thieves of Amn, Calimshan and the church of Bane. It deeply mistrusts the Red Wizards of Thay but does not treat them as enemies.

## History (canon)

- **1325 DR:** founded by towns of the Sword Coast North to oppose Amn's merchant houses. Membership later spread south.
- **1358 DR:** used military and diplomatic pressure to expel Luskan's troops from the island of Ruathym.
- **1361 DR:** open war after Luskan conquered Ruathym.
- **1363 DR:** fought in the Second Dragonspear War, then worked to make the Trade Way safe.
- **1368 DR:** gathered evidence against Amn over Waterdhavian ships sunk on the route to Maztica.
- **1371 DR:** Laeral led an Alliance army to relieve Evereska. It arrived months too late.
- **1372 DR:** Treaty of Garumn's Gorge between Mithral Hall and the orc kingdom of Many-Arrows. This was a member's treaty, not an Alliance one.
- **c. 1467–1488 DR:** Dagult Neverember led the Alliance.
- **1480s DR:** the Alliance sent **seven members** to the Council of Waterdeep against the Cult of the Dragon's plan to free Tiamat. During the giant crisis (*Storm King's Thunder*), rumours blamed the Alliance for storm giant King Hekaton's disappearance.
- **1484–1485 DR:** the War of the Silver Marches.
- **1488 DR:** Luruar (the Silver Marches) collapsed. The Alliance **turned inward and stopped admitting members from outside the North**. Laeral became leader.
- **c. 1491 DR:** Alliance agents worked in Mulmaster, hoping to bring it into the Alliance or at least get it to follow Alliance rules.
