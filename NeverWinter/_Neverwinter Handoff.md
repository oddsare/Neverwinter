## Canon research (2026-10-18)
Looked up on the Forgotten Realms Wiki, **not taken from the vault**. This is canon reference only and doesn't decide anything for the campaign.

### Noble families of Neverwinter
Canon names very few, and most come from the video games.
- **Alagondar** — royal house, founded after Nasher died (1385 DR); Bann was the second king. Ruled from Castle Never and all died in the 1451 Ruining. Their ghosts wait for a rightful heir. *(Neverwinter Campaign Setting)*
- **Neverember** — Waterdeep and Neverwinter. Supposedly descends from Vers Never, said to be Nasher's illegitimate son; the link is disputed and may have been made up. Dagult **taxed Neverwinter's nobles heavily to keep them weak** and limited the guilds.
- **Cragmire** — early 1300s. Allied with the royal family but secretly a necromancer cult. Destroyed in 1328 DR (Year of the Adder). Their keep was on the High Road near Raven Cliff Beach; the barrow seals broke in the late 1470s (Red Wizard Kallos Tam). *(Neverwinter MMO)*
- **Cyl** — Neverwinter shipping house, noble since about 1346 DR and **secretly in thrall to mind flayers**. Anali Cyl refused an arranged marriage in 1372, took the heirloom falchion *Decision* and became one of the three "Neverblades" against Luskan. Ed Greenwood has the house **still thriving in Neverwinter in the 1490s**; that replaces the older NWN2-based line that none were left. See [[Factions/House Cyl]].
- **Tolbert** — mentioned only as the family Ellandra Tolbert was born into.
- Individual nobles: **Lord Dalren** (NWN2, killed by Ammon Jerro), **Formosa Laitannen** (NWN1, Blacklake), **Mordai Vell** (tiefling, Ashmadai leader, Vellgard Manor), **Hugo Babris** (lord in 1451, put in place by Waterdeep).
- Before 1451, **Blacklake** was the nobles' quarter.
- These are *not* Neverwinter houses: Margaster, Nesher, Hawkwinter, Maernos and Piiradost are Waterdeep. Exeltis is Sword Coast North; Pemberton is Highcastle.

### How other settings do intrigue
Discussed because Brodie was stuck on "noble houses or what?":
- Waterdeep: Masked Lords, noble houses, guilds.
- Baldur's Gate: Council of Four, patriars, Flaming Fist.
- Cormyr: dynasty and noble houses.
- Eberron: Dragonmarked Houses as corporations.
- Greyhawk: Directing Oligarchy.
- Sigil: factions run the city.
- Ravnica: guilds are the government.
- Dark Sun: templars and merchant houses.
- Wildemount: Cerberus Assembly behind the throne.

The kinds of power these settings mix: bloodline, money, force, faith, magic, crime, outside powers, secret societies. Intrigue needs three things: something everyone wants, rules for how power changes hands, and leverage.

### Ashmadai
- Secret cult of Asmodeus in Neverwinter, also called the Messengers of the Raging Fiend. Served Thay to get back the **Ruby Rod of Asmodeus**.
- By 1479 split in two: **Favria** (dwarf, zealots, still loyal to Thay) and **Mordai Vell** (schemers inside the new government's inner circle).
- Vell's side used isolated cells ("pods"), so captured cultists can't give much away. They mark buildings and bodies with Asmodeus's symbol and bind summoned devils into kidnapped people.
- Vell died around 1484; Favria probably leads after that. Canon is silent later.

### Thay
- A country of living people ruled by undead: Red Wizards over Mulan bureaucrats, with a huge slave population.
- After **Szass Tam** (lich) took power in 1385, the rulers became vampires and liches, and the living became second-class. By 1479 Thay had the greatest concentration of undead in Faerûn.
- Ties to Neverwinter:
  - **Sylora Salm** set off the 1451 Ruining by freeing **Maegera** through Dahlia; the dwarf Athrogate pulled the lever.
  - She built the **Dread Ring** in Neverwinter Wood for Szass Tam's godhood ritual. Drizzt, Dahlia and Entreri beat her in 1463, and her champion Jestry killed her.
  - **Valindra Shadowmantle** (lich) took over, worked to restore the Dread Ring, and led the **1479 Siege of Neverwinter**.
  - Earlier, a Thayan trade enclave operated in Neverwinter after the Shadow War.
- **Szass Tam's current schemes** (Ed Greenwood, late 1490s): eastern expansion, a personal spy network in place of the trade enclaves, and a plot to control Gilgeam of Unther. See [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Szass Tam's Schemes]].
- **Thay around 1500 DR** (Ed Greenwood): about 7 million people, **most slaves now replaced by undead**, Bane as near state religion, and Szass Tam's hidden heirs. See [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Thay Today]].
- **D&D movie lore is ignored** in this campaign (Brodie, 2026-10-18).
