# Lord Dagult Neverember

**Title:** Lord Protector of Neverwinter (former); Open Lord of Waterdeep (former)
**Born:** 1429 DR
**Status:** Missing since 1502 DR. Whereabouts unknown.
**Family:**
- Father of [[NPCs/Renaer Neverember|Renaer Neverember]] (current Lord of Neverwinter)
- Husband of **Alethea Brandarth** (deceased; she left her wealth to Renaer, not Dagult — source of the father-son rift)
- House Neverember; seat at **Brandarth Hall** in Waterdeep
- Publicly claims descent from **Vers Never**, alleged bastard son of [[Lore/The Alagondar Dynasty|Nasher Alagondar]]
- **Privately: the claim is canonically fraudulent. He is not actually an Alagondar.**

---

## The fraudulent claim — canon

> **Canon (Forgotten Realms Wiki, Neverwinter Campaign Setting 4e):**
> *"The actress/aristocrat [[NPCs/Indrina Lamsensettle|Indrina Lamsensettle]] commissioned genealogical research and found enough genealogical data left intact to prove that Dagult's claim of lineage was completely fabricated. She found no blood ties to Lord Nasher Alagondar. She feared Dagult would silence her over it."*

**Dagult's public story:**
- Descends from **Vers Never**, alleged bastard son of Nasher Alagondar.
- Vers (per the story) left Neverwinter for Waterdeep in the 14th century, married **Mirtria Ember**, and founded the Neverember line.
- Therefore Dagult has a legitimate (if cadet-branch) claim to Neverwinter's throne.

**What the canon actually says:**
- Vers Never's existence is "conflicting records / unconfirmed rumors" even in-universe.
- Whether Vers actually existed or not, Indrina's research found **no blood tie** between Dagult and Nasher.
- Dagult **knows his claim is suspect** (at minimum) — he has actively suppressed dissenting evidence (see below).

**What he does about it:**
- **Pays scribes to find or forge books that legitimize his lineage** (canon).
- **Burns books that cast doubt** on his claim (canon).
- **Seeks the death of other Alagondar claimants** to clear potential rivals (canon — "silently disposes of rivals," from NCS 4e / FR Wiki).
- Surrounded himself with loyalists and Mintarn mercenaries who would not investigate the matter independently.

**Bottom line:** Dagult is a fraud. He is not Alagondar. Canon settles this. He **cannot open Alagondar bloodline-locked vaults**, **cannot wear the Crown of Neverwinter** (or it kills him with cold/heat — see [[Lore/The Lost Crown and Hidden Alagondar Vaults]]). His entire claim to legitimacy is forged paperwork and a body count of rival claimants.

## Background — Waterdhavian noble

- **House Neverember** is a long-established Waterdhavian noble line, with their seat at **Brandarth Hall**.
- Dagult was **Open Lord of Waterdeep** before becoming Lord Protector of Neverwinter — for years he held both titles simultaneously.
- Married **Alethea Brandarth** for her family money. Their son was [[NPCs/Renaer Neverember|Renaer]].
- Alethea died young. **Her will left her wealth to Renaer**, not Dagult — a public humiliation and the canonical source of the father-son rift. Renaer grew up wealthy in his own right and **estranged** from his father.
- Used Waterdeep's treasury to fund Neverwinter's rebuilding ("the Neverwinter Reconstruction Fund") — the canonical scandal that got him **ousted as Open Lord of Waterdeep in 1489 DR.**

---

## Lord Protector of Neverwinter (1467-1502 DR)

- **1467 DR:** Proclaimed himself an Alagondar heir. Hired Mintarn mercenaries to garrison Neverwinter. Called his movement "**New Neverwinter**."
- Used embezzled Waterdhavian wealth to rebuild the city — earning popular support among commoners.
- **Centralized power** through:
  - **High taxes on the nobility** (suppressing rival noble houses)
  - **Heavy regulation on the guilds** (suppressing the urban political class)
  - **Mintarn mercenaries replacing local guard** (suppressing the militia that might oppose him)
- Was **deeply unpopular with**: nobles, guilds, the Sons of Alagondar (who saw him as a usurper).
- **Ousted as Open Lord of Waterdeep in 1489 DR** following the embezzlement scandal. Retreated to Neverwinter as his sole power base.
- Continued ruling Neverwinter until his disappearance in **1502 DR.**

## Character — clinging villain

**Dagult is a bad person.** Not a desperate hero in over his head. Not a misunderstood reformer. A petty, vain, power-hungry man whose entire identity is wrapped around ruling Neverwinter. The city is all he has left — his father's name is dust, his Waterdeep position is gone, his wife is dead, his son hates him. Without the throne he is nothing, and he knows it. **He will do anything to keep it.**

His public framing — that he restored Neverwinter, that the rebuilding was for the people, that he was a benevolent Lord Protector — is **self-justification.** Citizens benefitted from the Neverwinter Reconstruction Fund as a side effect of his power-consolidation, not because he cared about them. He cared about being the man who restored Neverwinter and was therefore owed its throne.

His murders of rival Alagondar claimants are not the acts of a man trying to save a city. They are the acts of a man eliminating threats to his rule.

## Disappearance — 1502 DR (LOCKED)

**What actually happened (locked 2026-05-15):**

By 1502 DR, Dagult was a man cornered on three fronts:

1. **Luskan was plotting to assassinate him.** He had specific intelligence on a planned hit. (The plot may still operate in 1525 with successors.)
2. **Neverwinter itself was turning against him.** Two decades of high noble taxes, heavy guild regulation, and Mintarn-mercenary garrison had eroded his support. The Sons of Alagondar were organized opposition; the nobility quietly funded them; the commoners were running out of patience.
3. **His fraudulent Alagondar claim was decaying.** [[NPCs/Indrina Lamsensettle|Indrina Lamsensettle]] had likely already begun her genealogical research; Dagult's book-burning was failing to keep ahead.

**He could have stepped down.** He could have brokered a peaceful transition, named a successor, negotiated with the Sons of Alagondar, retreated to Waterdeep with what wealth he still had. He could have *let go.*

He did not. **He clung.**

Dagult's plan: **claim the Lost Crown of Neverwinter** to silence every challenge at once. Crown-bearer rules unchallenged. The mythic legitimacy would override Indrina's evidence, neutralize the Sons of Alagondar, and deter the Luskan plot — and most importantly to him, it would *guarantee he kept the throne.* The Crown was about keeping power, not protecting citizens.

He **didn't believe the bloodline-lock legend was real.** He thought it was folk-myth woven by old loyalists to discourage usurpers — exactly the kind of story a fraudulent claimant of any era would dismiss. **A better man might have questioned his own claim when faced with that legend. Dagult did not.**

He entered the [[Locations/Neverwinter/The Neverneath|Neverneath]] alone (or with a small trusted retinue, since lost) in 1502 DR.

**Outcome (LOCKED):** **He is alive and trapped.** The Neverneath's corrupted ward caught him before he reached the Inner Vault. He has been wandering its infinite reshaping corridors for **23 in-game years.** He is:

- **Alive** — the ward did not kill him, only contained him. Halueth Never's protective magic still feeds the corpse-chamber's life-sustenance to anyone inside the maze, by ancient design.
- **Aged but functional** — 96 by 1525 (born 1429 DR). The ward's sustenance has kept him fit but he is *old.*
- **Sane, mostly** — and still bitter. Twenty-three years alone with his thoughts has not made him a better man. He believes he is still owed the throne. He may even still believe his Alagondar claim is real — that the ward was *wrong about him*, not that he was a fraud.
- **Still in possession of his memories** — Luskan plot intel, names of suppressed evidence, archive locations, the rivals he killed.

**Recovering Dagult is a campaign-defining choice, not a triumph.** He is the most valuable witness in Neverwinter. He is also a vindictive old usurper who will immediately attempt to reclaim his throne the moment he is free. The party will have to decide what to do with him:

- Turn him over to [[NPCs/Renaer Neverember|Renaer]] (who doesn't want him back)
- Hand him to Waterdeep for the embezzlement trial that was decades overdue
- Extract his testimony then exile him
- Kill him quietly
- Let him try to claim power again and watch the city tear itself apart

He may know:
- Names of the 1502 Luskan plot conspirators (and their possible successors operating in 1525)
- Where [[NPCs/Indrina Lamsensettle|Indrina Lamsensettle]]'s dossier is
- The identities of rival Alagondar heirs he was hunting (some may still be alive)
- What the ward told him about the bloodline test

**He will share none of it freely.** He'll bargain. He'll lie. He'll demand restoration to power as the price. He is not a sympathetic prisoner — he is a captive villain.

## What he knows / knew

If recovered alive (e.g., from the Neverneath):

- The **real location of the Lost Crown** and other Alagondar vaults.
- The **identity of one or more surviving true Alagondar heirs** (he was hunting them for 33 years — he likely identified at least one before he vanished).
- The exact mechanism of the Inner Vault's seal.
- **Where his suppressed evidence went** — the books he burned, the scribes he killed, the trail he buried.
- Possibly: the **truth about his own lineage** — does he know he's a fraud, or did he genuinely believe the Vers Never story?

## Status by 1525 DR

**MISSING.** No body. No confirmed sighting. Renaer rules in his absence (since 1520 DR after the Dance of the Revolving Lords).

The **mystery of Dagult's whereabouts** is one of the campaign's potentially major open threads. Resolving it answers:

- Where the Lost Crown is.
- Whether the Alagondar line is *actually* dead.
- Whether Renaer has the option to abdicate to a true heir.
- Whether 23 years of "the Dance" had a hidden author all along.
