# Campaign Decisions

Brodie's decisions that don't have their own note yet. Recorded 2026-10-12. Details to come.

## 1. Politics
- **Renaer and his council are the weaker faction.** Some council seats are owned by houses.
- **4 major noble houses**, each with vassal houses:
  - **3 bad**: out for personal gain.
  - **1 good**: stands with the people, currently weaker than the other 3.
- **Major crafting guilds** in the city.
- **One of the largest houses** is known for **crafting and using Dawnfire**.
  - **Its name is House Handhagr** (Brodie, 2026-10-22), said **HAND-hah-ger**. Old Norse *hönd* (hand) + *hagr* (skillful): "skillful of hand." It's the major crafters' house, standing for **all the skilled hands of the city** and how good they are at crafting. Still open: bad or good.
- **Another house** handles the **mining**.
- **House Cyl is one of the four major houses** (Brodie, 2026-10-22): merchants, mainly by ship, who handle **most of the city's storage and shipment of goods**. Canon secret: in thrall to mind flayers (see [[Factions/House Cyl]]). Still open: bad or good.
- **No single family controls Dawnfire.**

See [[Lore/Dawnfire]].

## 2. Shar and Selûne
- **Shar and Selûne are a major part** of the campaign.
- **A group that thinks the gods are overstepping.** It uses Shar's tactics openly and blames her for what it does.
- Still to decide (Brodie): the group's goals, how far along they are, what people have noticed, where the story goes.
- **Shar's arc in the [[Lore/3 Faction Rule|Three-Faction Rule]]** (Brodie, 2026-10-19): **she starts as the Ugly, then turns Bad.**
  - **Now, as the Ugly:** she's the neutral swing side who can **help or hurt the heroes' party**. She's unpredictable ("crazy") and may back either side. She is still evil; she just isn't the enemy yet.
  - **Later:** once the **first evil threat is defeated**, she **turns Bad**.
- **There is a fane of Shar in the Chasm** (Brodie, 2026-10-19): a place where Shar has strengthened the Shadow Weave (Ed Greenwood's term; see [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Fanes of Shar]]). What it's like is not decided.

Ties to [[Lore/Current Era]]: the gods have become more active in mortal affairs.

## 3. Magic
- **Magic is abundant.** Reference: Critical Role campaign 4 (Aramán), where magic runs through institutions and the noble houses are a threat you can't just fight.
- **Heads of houses are around level 10–15.**
- **There may be no magic shops** as such.
- **10th-level spells (2026-10-20):** canon says **Mystra forbade spells of 10th level and higher** after Netheril fell (-339 DR). The [[Lore/Current Era]] rumor of enclaves close to 10th-level magic means they're **defying the goddess of magic and risking the Weave**. Brodie: "that works perfectly."
- **REMINDER, to decide later (Brodie):** **what Mystra and her Chosen will do** about it.

## 5. Waterdeep: Dragon Heist (1492 DR)
Recorded 2026-10-17.
- In this world, *Waterdeep: Dragon Heist* (1492 DR, the Year of Three Ships Sailing) **went Manshoon's way**. That is the winter branch. In canon, the Manshoon in Waterdeep that year is a clone based in Kolat Towers.
- **The party succeeded.** They foiled Manshoon's scheme and recovered Dagult's hidden hoard (500,000 gold dragons) from the Vault of Dragons.
- **They fought Manshoon's simulacrum, not Manshoon.** That matches the book: the simulacrum handles the field encounters, and the real Manshoon stays in his sanctum. Manshoon himself came through 1492 untouched.
- **The simulacrum survived 1492.** It can never regain spell slots. My best guess is that it has 4 of its 18 left (see [[NPCs/Manshoon's Simulacrum]]). Because it survived, Manshoon has not cast *simulacrum* again since, since that would destroy it.
- **They returned the money to Waterdeep.** Per the book, Laeral lets the party keep a 50,000 gp finder's fee, and the rest goes back to the city.
- **Renaer was there.** In the winter branch, the vault is entered through the Brandath mausoleum, and only Brandath blood gets past its guardian, so Renaer went into the vault with the party.
- This covers only the hidden hoard. The money Dagult spent rebuilding Neverwinter is a separate matter.
- Still to decide (Brodie):
  - Where is the real Manshoon in 1505, and why hasn't he recast *simulacrum*?
  - Where is the simulacrum in 1505, and who does it obey?
  - How did Dagult react? This was 1 year before his 1493 disappearance.
  - Who was the party?
  - Did they take Manshoon's ledger, or get Jelenn Urmbrusk's favour?

See [[Locations/Waterdeep]], [[NPCs/Manshoon]], [[NPCs/Manshoon's Simulacrum]], [[NPCs/Dagult Neverember]], [[NPCs/Renaer Neverember]], [[Lore/Calendar of Harptos]].

## 6. Timeline rework
Recorded 2026-10-18.
- **Canon's "present" is 1492 DR** (*Dragon Heist*). Everything after that is campaign territory.
- **The campaign starts in 1505 DR** (moved from 1525).
- **Renaer:** born **1461**. Age 18 in 1479 (met the *Blackstaff Tower* companions and founded his own group), 31 in 1492, **took power in 1503 at 42** (young for a ruler), **44** at campaign start.
- **1486:** Dagult seals the Chasm, and **Dawnfire is discovered** the same year. Dagult and his mercenaries see it could give them **military might**.
- **1489:** Dagult ousted from Waterdeep.
- **1492:** the Waterdeep incident (§5). Dagult **barely holds onto** Neverwinter afterward.
- **1493:** Dagult disappears. Dawnfire weapons weren't coming fast enough, so he gambled on the **Lost Crown**, which he'd known about for a long time. He went into the Neverneath and got trapped, just barely before he could mass-produce the weapons.
- **1493–1503:** the Dance of the Revolving Lords, **10 years, 6 rulers** (names later).
- **1503–1505:** Renaer's rule is **stable for 2 years**, then the story picks up. Not much time to get everything done, and he faces **pushback from influential people in the city and in the neighboring places the city protects**.

## 7. Ed Greenwood's Neverwinter lore
Recorded 2026-10-19. Source: [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Secrets of Neverwinter]].
- **Dagult uses Ed's version.** He's a con man who believes in what he does and does good things. The end justifies the means, so he may trample anyone in his way. His flaw is that he sees nothing wrong with getting rich by doing good. This replaces the "clinging villain" write-up.
- **The campaign takes place after the period Ed describes, so the vault's own lore stays.** Dagult has been trapped in the Neverneath since 1493.
- **The Chasm District stays as Brodie wrote it, not Ed's version.** It has a wall, a magical seal that stories say weakens every year, Thieves Cove and Gutter's End. Ed says no trace of the Chasm remains; the vault doesn't follow that.
- **Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun is dead**, as in canon (Brodie, 2026-10-19). He died in 1374 DR (see [[Lore/The Blackstaff]]) and had no part in Dagult's fall. Vajra Safahr is the Blackstaff. Notes that treated him as alive were updated.
- **Both disguised dragons are in the campaign, as different characters:** [[NPCs/Zundaerazylym - The Laughing Wyrm|Zundaerazylym]] (steel dragon, seamstress) and Ed's Murthra "Manytongues" Alaerin (brass dragon, House of Tongues). The party may meet one, both or neither.
- **What caused the Chasm is disputed in the world.** Different people say different things: the Spellplague, the 1451 eruption, devils or Asmodeus, the Abolethic Sovereignty, an evil wizard, or angry gods. The DM truth is not decided.
- **Ed's hidden kin are probably Alagondar, not Neverember** (Brodie, 2026-10-22). The video's captions said Neverember, but they hide from Dagult, who hunted Alagondar heirs for the Lost Crown. Wylandra Blaythloak, Unthra Rorivan and Rundrigo Blackserpent are three of them. Still open: whether that's final, and how they tie to Renaer and the Lost Crown.

See [[NPCs/Dagult Neverember]], [[Locations/Neverwinter/Districts/Chasm District]].

## 8. Population of Neverwinter
Recorded 2026-10-19.
- **Neverwinter has about 50,000 people in 1505 DR**, roughly twice its old size. That puts it in the range of Mulmaster, Suzail and Saerloon.
- **How it got there:**
  - About 23,000 lived there before 1451.
  - About half died in the eruption (~11,500 survivors). Fewer than 5,000 stayed in the ruins; the rest fled.
  - Refugees started coming back after Dagult arrived in 1467.
- **Who lives there now:** about **15,000** come from pre-1451 families (survivors and their descendants). About **35,000** are newcomers or their children, so roughly 1 in 3 people is "old Neverwinter."
- That number fits inside the walls on the city map at a busy-city density.
- Still to decide (Brodie): **why so many newcomers came.**

See [[Locations/Neverwinter/Neverwinter]], [[Lore/The Three Cataclysms]], [[TIMELINE]].

## 9. Szass Tam's hidden heirs (Brantarra's sons)
Recorded 2026-10-19. Source: [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Thay Today]].
- **The campaign uses Ed Greenwood's hidden-heirs plot.**
  - **Brantarra**, the Red Wizardess of Murbant, slept with **King Azoun IV of Cormyr** in 1352 DR, as part of a Thayan plot to put Azoun's son on the Dragon Throne under her mind control, and hers under Szass Tam's.
  - She fled Thay. **Several Chosen of Mystra** helped her hide herself and her sons from **Szass Tam** and from Cormyr's royal magician **Vangerdahast**.
  - She has **two sons**, now grown men living under other names and guises:
    - **a bastard heir to Cormyr's Dragon Throne** (Azoun IV's son);
    - **Szass Tam's only blood heir.** Tam destroyed every other member of his family and doesn't know this son exists.
- **Age problem to solve:** **Azoun's son** was conceived in 1352 DR, so he was born **about 1353** and is **about 152 in 1505**. **Szass Tam's son's birth year isn't given**, so he could be younger. Ed calls them both "adult men" in his present but doesn't say how Azoun's son has lived so long.
- **Maybe (Brodie, 2026-10-19): one of the sons could be a lich.** That would explain the age. Which son is not decided. Points to weigh:
  - A lich is a powerful spellcaster who chose undeath by a dark ritual, with a soul-holding phylactery (in 5e, fed with souls).
  - The Chosen of Mystra who protected him might turn against him if he became one.
  - A non-evil version exists in the Realms: an archlich (the elven form is a baelnorn).
  - Szass Tam's son as a lich would mirror his father. Azoun's son as a lich would be an undead claimant to Cormyr's throne.
- **Idea, probably not (Brodie, 2026-10-19): the claimant could be Azoun's son's son** instead of the son himself.
  - **For:** he's Azoun IV's grandson (Queen Raedra is a great-great-granddaughter), has the Obarskyr blood the law requires, could be a normal age (no 150-year problem), and might not know who he is.
  - **Against:** the line is still illegitimate, it began as a Thayan plot, and Cormyr already has a working queen. Unseating Raedra would risk civil war.
- Still to decide (Brodie):
  - Where are the two sons in 1505, under what names, and do they know who their fathers are?
  - How has Azoun's son lived so long (and Tam's son, if he's that old too)?
  - Is Brantarra alive?
  - Who is hunting them now (Thay, Cormyr's War Wizards, others)?
  - How does this reach Neverwinter, and the party?

See [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Thay Today]], [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Szass Tam's Schemes]].

## 12. Races and alignment
Recorded 2026-10-20. Full detail and edition tables: [[Lore/Races and Alignment]].
- **Races are viewed the way 3.5 and early 5e did.** Some races **lean evil by nature**. They do evil things, but **they don't have to**. They lean that way unless something changes them or puts them on a different path. (Brodie's reference: the demons in *Frieren*, but changeable for players.)
- **Which races lean evil:** every race 3.5 lists as evil, including monsters that are now playable races.
- **What causes the lean:** their **god**, their **culture** and their **nature**. **God and nature come first.**
- **What can change them:** many things: **a god, a choice, awful events.** Whatever causes trauma can change a person.
- **Player characters can be drow or orcs.** The world is **cautious and expects the worst, but is willing to open up if given the chance.** Good deeds and reputation matter, the same as for everyone, but people can be scared.
- **Half-orcs and tieflings are treated differently** (see the lore note).
- **Half-orcs and tieflings are not rare anymore.** They're fully integrated into most societies, just fewer in number: **about 1 in 100 people each**, similar to dragonborn. In Neverwinter that's roughly 500 of each.

## 13. The Thayan war (background thread)
Recorded 2026-10-20. Built on Ed Greenwood's Thay notes ([[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Thay Today]], [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Szass Tam's Schemes]]); the war breaking out is Brodie's, not canon.
- **Background, not the main story.** It runs whether or not the party engages. It starts with **Thay, Aglarond and Cormyr**, maybe others (from the notes: Rashemen, Mulhorand, Unther, Murghôm).
- **Thay strikes first** through its **cells of Red Wizards**. **Every enemy killed becomes a soldier for Thay.**
- **Szass Tam** micromanages and wants control of everything (canon, Ed). His **undead generals** obey without question. He believes the Red Wizards will "be on his side," but their loyalty is uncertain, so **he raises undead to be loyal** ("he will make his loyalties").
- **Unther is already Tam's puppet**, maybe not 100%.
  - Untherans already distrust Mulhorand (canon: Mulhorand's 1371 occupation, and Untherans enslaved by Mulhorandi in Abeir).
  - Tam's agents **whisper in the right ears** and stage **false "border attacks,"** so **Unther is ready to attack Mulhorand**. Thay then invades Mulhorand from the other side (Ed's plan).
  - Gilgeam's control goes **read his mind → influence his thinking → fully control him**. **Full control needs a Dread Ring ritual.**
- **The active Dread Ring is in the east**, near **Undumor (Aglarond)** or **the Thesk-border forts** (Nethjet, Nethentir, Surcross), both canon Dread Ring-like fortresses by 1479. Which one is undecided. It's fed by the war's dead (canon: a Dread Ring needs a city-sized death toll).
- **Valindra Shadowmantle** works for Tam. **Dawnfire is a main reason she's in Neverwinter**; what exactly it boosts is undecided. **Thay smuggles Dawnfire east** to the ring. Canon tie: she was already hunting the primordial's energy to "supercharge" her magic, and Dawnfire is Maegera's fire. Thay's failed 1451 eruption accidentally created it.
- **The Neverwinter Wood ring is old lore**: broken and thought dead, but with **hints of recent activity** that lead the party to information about the Dread Ring and to Valindra.
- **The party can act for and against each country.** Helping Thay stays possible but **rare**: they're slavers and undead.
- **Open (Brodie):**
  - Which eastern ring?
  - What exactly does Dawnfire boost?
  - How does Dawnfire travel east?
  - When does the war break out?
  - How does Cormyr enter (the hidden heir, §9)?
  - Is the dragon-rage ritual against Murghôm kept or dropped?
  - Is Arauthator's grave a possible future ring site?

See [[Lore/Campaign_Guide/Chapter_3_Factions_and_Foes]] (Valindra, the Dread Ring) and §9 above.

It fits Brodie's original hook (2026-05-14): "Claugiyliamatar moved into Neverwinter because of all the chaos."
- **Replaces** the May suggestion that she "commutes" from Deeping Cave.
- **Open (Brodie):**
  - Under the city, or in the Neverwinter Wood? Where exactly?
  - When did she move: before or after she found Aldric in 1486?
  - What happened to Deeping Cave and her hoard?
