# The Lost Crown and the Hidden Alagondar Vaults

> **The Canon:**
> After the destruction of Neverwinter in **1451 DR** (Mount Hotenow eruption / "the Ruining"), survivors of the catastrophe **hid the treasures of the [[Lore/The Alagondar Dynasty|Alagondar family]] in secret rooms and vaults** scattered through the city. Each cache was sealed with **magical protection that opens only to a true heir of the Alagondar bloodline.** Among the hidden treasures is the **fabled Lost Crown of Neverwinter.**

This is one of the campaign's most load-bearing plot elements. Resolving it is **the endgame political question**: who actually deserves to rule Neverwinter.

---

## What was hidden

The survivors didn't have time to catalog everything they cached. Best surviving accounts list:

- **The Lost Crown of Neverwinter** — the royal crown of the Alagondar line. Headline treasure. Its bearer is the legitimate ruler.
  - **Canon physical description:** Mithral warrior-king's crown inlaid with **four blue sapphires.**
  - **Canon magical properties:** Protects the wearer from cold and heat. **Enhances majesty** — grants advantage on Persuasion and Intimidation checks while worn.
  - **Bloodline lock (canon-confirmed):** If a non-Alagondar attempts to wear it, they are **destroyed by a burst of cold and heat.** No save, no negotiation. This is **why Dagult Neverember never wore it publicly** despite his claim — and probably why he disappeared trying.
  - The Crown is widely speculated to rest in the **Inner Vault** at the heart of the [[Locations/Neverwinter/The Neverneath|Neverneath]] beneath Castle Never (canon-strong, not definitive).
- **The Seal of Neverwinter** — royal seal for authenticating decrees and documents.
- **Royal genealogical records** — could prove (or disprove) specific descent claims. Politically explosive in either direction.
- **Regalia and ceremonial weapons** — signet rings, the throne sword, court vestments.
- **Private royal correspondence** — diplomatic, personal, possibly compromising to surviving noble houses.
- **State treasury reserves** — coin, gems, art objects intended for the city's rebuilding.

There may be more. The hiders left no master inventory.

---

## How the vaults work

- **Magically protected** — sealed against ordinary access. No lockpick, no battering ram, no fireball opens them.
- **Bloodline-locked** — they respond to **true Alagondar bloodline only.** Mechanism is canonical but the exact magical method is open: divination ward, blood-touch ritual, hereditary attunement, or all three layered.
- **Distributed across the city** — *not all in one place.* Some likely beneath Castle Never (possibly via the [[Lore/Neverwinter Sub-Locations|Neverneath]]); others in Neverdeath crypts, sealed temple chambers, hidden basements of pre-Cataclysm noble houses, or buried beneath Bluelake.
- **Untouchable to imposters** — pretenders cannot force them open. **This is the key plot mechanic.** Trying and failing may be lethal (the wards likely defend themselves).

---

## Who can open them

**Verified canon facts (and their implications):**

- The Alagondar royal family was **killed during the 1451 eruption.**
- "Many believed the line has ended."
- But canonically: *"no legitimate heirs to the old Alagondar royal line are known to exist"* — that's "**not known to exist**," not "do not exist."
- [[NPCs/Dagult Neverember|Dagult Neverember]] claims descent from **Nasher Alagondar's bastard son** — a *cadet branch*, not the legitimate royal line.
- Dagult canonically **"silently disposes of anyone claiming connection to the rulers of old Neverwinter."** Why? Because every legitimate claimant is a potential vault-opener — and a threat to his own claim.

**Therefore:**
- Dagult **cannot open the vaults himself** (bastard line ≠ true bloodline).
- Renaer is in the same position (he is Dagult's son; same lineage problem).
- **There are surviving true heirs.** They are hiding — by choice (knowing Dagult would kill them), by ignorance (not knowing their own bloodline), or by force (Dagult has captured them and is keeping them alive but suppressed).

## Where the vaults likely are

Untested possibilities — each is a plot location waiting to be designed:

| Location | Argument for | Argument against |
|---|---|---|
| **The Neverneath beneath Castle Never** | Built to protect Halueth Never's corpse + the castle's foundation; would logically have other royal protections layered in. | The Neverneath dates from -10 DR (Halueth's era), pre-Alagondar; the survivors of 1451 may not have been able to access it. |
| **The Vault of the Nine** | Sealed crypt of the royal bodyguards — exactly the kind of place loyalists would hide royal treasure. | Already a known location; survivors would have picked somewhere unknown to imposters. |
| **A Neverdeath District crypt** | Ancient graveyard, full of sealed family mausoleums — hiding a vault inside one is plausible. | Mausoleums are vulnerable to grave robbers. |
| **Beneath the Bluelake** | Lake-water naturally conceals; some lakebed structures predate the Cataclysm. | Engineering challenges. |
| **Inside a still-standing pre-Cataclysm noble house** | Hide-in-plain-sight; daily routine of an occupied house is camouflage. | The current occupants may not know what's beneath their floor. |
| **The House of Knowledge** | Oghman temples are repositories of records — survivors might have entrusted records there. | Library is heavily curated; hard to hide a vault. |
| **Helm's Hold** | Outside the city, defensible, religious sanctity. | Outside city = outside the Alagondar magical wardscape. |

Recommend: **at least 3 vaults, in different districts.** One contains the Crown (climax). Others contain Seal, records, regalia.

## Campaign integration

### What this means for Dagult Neverember's disappearance (1502 DR)

The single most useful inference: **Dagult vanished trying to open a vault.**

- He spent 33 years (1467 onward) consolidating power as Lord Protector with the explicit goal of *eventually* claiming Alagondar legitimacy.
- Bastard-line claim got him political power but not vault access.
- He must have tried at least one vault. The vault rejected him.
- Possible outcomes for 1502:
  - **He's dead** — the ward killed him.
  - **He's trapped** — the [[Lore/Neverwinter Sub-Locations|Neverneath]] caught him in an infinite loop; he may be **still alive** in there, 23 years later, slowly going mad.
  - **He was abducted** — someone realized what he was doing and took him. Could be [[NPCs/Claugiyliamatar - Old Gnawbone|Claugiyliamatar]] (long-game leverage), the [[Factions/Arcane Brotherhood|Arcane Brotherhood]] (wanted to study the wards), or a true Alagondar heir who beat him to it.

### What this means for Renaer Neverember

He is Dagult's son. **Same bloodline problem.** Renaer cannot open the vaults either.

He might:
- **Not know** what his father was doing (he was estranged; this was Dagult's project).
- **Know** but be honest enough to let it lie.
- **Know** and quietly look for a true heir to *legitimize* his rule by alliance (marriage, regency, public endorsement).
- **Know** and be hunting for the Crown himself, hoping to find a way around the bloodline lock.

The Tier-3 campaign question for Renaer is: *what does he do when a true heir is found?* Abdicate? Marry? Murder? All three are valid Renaer arcs.

### What this means for the party

A campaign-ending arc:

1. **Tier 1**: hints. Old graffiti, suppressed rumors, a sealed door that won't open.
2. **Tier 2**: the party realizes the vaults exist. They find one. It rejects them. They start hunting for an heir.
3. **Tier 3**: a true heir is identified (possibly a PC, possibly an NPC the party rescues from Dagult's "disposal" pipeline). The party fights to get them to the Crown vault. Final confrontation: the heir opens the vault, claims the Crown — and the party decides whether to put them on the throne or let Renaer keep it.

### Cross-thread interactions

- **[[NPCs/Claugiyliamatar - Old Gnawbone|Claugiyliamatar]]:** Old Gnawbone wants to *be* a Neverwinter noble. The Crown is the keystone of Neverwinter nobility. **Does she want the Crown for herself?** She can't open the vaults (not Alagondar). But she can *find a true heir, manipulate them, and use them as a key.* This is the most political-intrigue version of her arc.
- **[[NPCs/Voaraghamanthar - Two Black Brothers|The Two Black Brothers / Iniarbor]]:** the brothers' kidnapping operation might *include* abducted true heirs. Iniarbor the lich could be experimenting with bloodline-magic on Alagondar descendants. **Buried alive in the Mere** = a survival-grade prison for someone he wants to keep usable.
- **The [[NPCs/Selune and Shar - Sister Goddesses|Sharran cult]]:** Shar specializes in unmaking and remaking identity. A Sharran ritual might *forge* a true Alagondar heir from a willing subject — or *erase* a real one to clear the field for Dagult's line. Plot-grade ambiguity.
- **The [[Factions/The Harpers|Harpers]]:** they almost certainly know about the vaults. They almost certainly know who the surviving heirs are. **They've been protecting them.** This is what the secret basement of the [[Lore/Neverwinter Sub-Locations|House of a Thousand Faces]] is actually for.

## Open design decisions

1. **How many vaults?** Recommend 3-5. Each one a discrete tier of reveal.
2. **Where is the Crown specifically?** The hardest to find, last to open. Recommend somewhere thematically charged — beneath Castle Never (politically loaded), inside the Vault of the Nine (the bodyguards literally still guard it), or in a Neverdeath crypt (the past holds the future).
3. **Who is the true heir?** Player character? Recurring NPC? Hidden NPC the party rescues? Multiple competing claimants?
4. **What does the bloodline check do to imposters?** Killed, trapped, transformed, marked? Recommend something gradient — a small failure marks you, a major one kills you. Dagult is alive somewhere because his attempt only marked him (and the markers are why he had to vanish).
5. **Does Renaer know?** Pick one answer before tier 1.
