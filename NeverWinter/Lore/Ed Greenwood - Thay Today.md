# Ed Greenwood — Thay Today (c. 1500 DR)

> Source: **Realms Lore** (YouTube), Ed Greenwood. Transcript added 2026-10-19. Summarized in my own words.
> A companion to [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Szass Tam's Schemes]]. Ed's "present" is **about 1500 DR**; the campaign is in 1505.
> Ed mentions that he co-wrote a recent **Thay sourcebook** with **Alex Kammer** and **Alan Patrick**.

## The people
- **A magical land:** the presence of the **Athora** makes Thay magical. Whoever lives there, trained or not, **many locals are born with the Gift**, and **wild magic lurks**.
  - **"The Gift"** is Ed's term for the **inborn ability to work arcane magic**: to cast, wield and craft it (see the Crownrood daughters in [[Factions/House Crownrood]]). Without it, no amount of study makes someone a wizard.
  - **The Athora** isn't explained in this video. In Ed's Volo video, he names it as one of the **immovable Weave anchors** Mystra uses to keep the Weave from collapsing (see [[NPCs/Volothamp Geddarm (Volo)]]). That's likely why Thay is so steeped in magic.
- **About 7 million Thayans** around 1500 DR: merchants, crafters, shopkeepers and soldiers. Many are descended from former slaves brought from **Mulhorand, Unther and all over the Realms**.
- **Slaves have mostly been replaced by undead.**
  - Most slaves, and most of the **serving military**, are now undead. They don't need feeding and can't be worked to death.
  - The **few slaves left** are mostly **talented people kept as status symbols** by Thayan nobles and by rich merchant "would-be nobles."
- **Just under 4,000 Red Wizards**, counting apprentices and novices. Those are **kept out of public view**, because the Red Wizards never want anyone in Thay to meet a weak Red Wizard and get ideas.

### The social ladder
1. **Red Wizards**.
2. **Nobles**, who come in two kinds:
   - **"Mercantile-might" nobles**, who could bankroll armies back in the days of slave-fodder troops led by veteran Thayan officers;
   - **a handful of "military commanders of real competence."**
   
   Eight or nine generations on, these origins are mostly forgotten.
3. **Minor merchants**.
4. **Crafters**.
5. **Shopkeepers**.
6. **Laborers**.

### Non-humans
- Thay is **far more human-dominated** than most of Faerûn.
- **In the cities**, life is easy enough for **dwarves, halflings, gnomes, elves and other non-humans** in the **poorer districts**, if they don't mind being working drudges.
- **In the countryside** they're much more accepted as "one of us," especially along Thay's **southwestern, eastern and southeastern fringes**.

## Arcane spellcasters in Thay

### Children with the Gift
- **Children are tested for the Gift.** Only parents who can stay **on the move** can hide a gifted child.
- **The authorities want anyone with the Gift to be a Red Wizard, or dead and gone from Thay.**
  - Gifted children are **scooped up for Red Wizard training**.
  - Those who show **rebellious or deceitful tendencies** may be **deliberately killed off during training**.

### Inside the Red Wizards
- **"Drudge" Red Wizards:** some unambitious, loyal Red Wizards choose bad treatment in exchange for relative safety, rather than trying to advance.
- **Non-human Red Wizards** face something like a modern glass ceiling.
  - They're watched, gently harassed, and usually have to be **very good** to rise.
  - But they're **accepted as full Red Wizards**, and even **trusted a little more**: superiors assume they have nowhere else to go, so they'll be diligent and reliable.
- **Training:** young Red Wizards are usually trained by **older, often embittered and disabled Red Wizards** all over Thay.
- **The Arcane College in Eltabbar** is where they go to be **tested**. They're trained there too if the tests show gaps in their mastery.
  - If the **Probity Corps** suspects a trainee's loyalty, or how they're being trained, it can **whisper to a senior Red Wizard** to send them to the College.
  - Some Red Wizards only ever see the College when being tested.

### Other casters
- **Sorcerers:** Red Wizards **look down on** them for relying on the Gift rather than strict Red Wizard teaching.
  - Immigrant or visiting sorcerers are **watched constantly** for any sign they're a foreign agent or plotting against Szass Tam.
  - **Junior Red Wizards** do the watching, and **Probity Corps** agents tail them on the ground.
- **Warlocks** are considered **walking security risks**, because of their patrons' influence. They're **not welcome as allies or residents**. Visiting warlocks are expected to **cast no magic at all** inside Thay.

## Weather
- **The land:** parts of Thay are mountainous and parts still desert. The desert is improving thanks to **waste used as mulch** and **Red Wizard weather control**.
- **Snow:**
  - **most often in the high country**: the **Thaymount** and the slopes of the **Sunset Mountains**;
  - **uncommon on the plateau ("High Thay")**, where hoarfrost and brief sleet storms are more common than snow that stays (except in places the sun doesn't reach);
  - **rare elsewhere**.
- **Rain and fog:**
  - **Night ground fogs**, from the day-to-night temperature swing, are more common than real precipitation.
  - Real snow or summer rain tends to be **brief**, sometimes heavy but fast-moving. That keeps humidity low.
  - Thayans are used to watching a storm's **front** (a wall or plume of falling rain) approach or recede in the distance.
- **Weather control:**
  - **Red Wizards cast weather magic regularly and widely for agriculture.** They favor **steady overnight downpours**, which soak the crops and also **hamper monsters, brigands, rebels**, and anyone else trying to move about unseen by the Red Wizards, the Probity Corps or government spies.
  - It's **coordinated through the local tharchions**, who receive the "reports from the fields" through their courtiers, but **commanded by the zulkirs**. That stops Red Wizards **freelancing for under-the-table coin**: the moment you have freelancers, you get weather chaos.
  - **"Weather teams"** of Red Wizards are sent to specific places to cast specific spells.

## Worship
- **Bane is close to a state religion** around 1500 DR. For Red Wizards, nobles and social climbers, being **seen as devout followers of Bane is socially necessary**. (In Ed's Thay sourcebook, the merchant tour guide calls the temple of Bane *the* temple to be seen at.)
- **In the south and southwest** (above all **Bezantur**, but also **Nethjet** and **Nethentir**), temples to **other gods** may be **larger, wealthier and more openly popular** than the local temples of Bane.
- **In private, everyone believes in all the gods.** People murmur prayers to whichever seem most useful, at home before bed and just after rising.

### Why Szass Tam opened the temples
- **The Second Sundering shook Szass Tam's confidence.** He walked the **Paths of the Doomed** alone, wondering if he was going about things the right way.
  - He had suffered reverse after reverse, like the **Dread Ring** affair, and found he **couldn't even wipe out his Red Wizard rivals outside Thay**.
  - Meanwhile he kept replacing slaves and soldiers with undead, and zulkirs with undead he controlled.
- **The visions:** during his wanderings he received visions telling him to **let all the temples breathe and stay open**. When a tyrant's grip is too tight, everything looks like Bane's way, and one misses the other plans and troubles brewing.
- **So now he allows the worship of all gods.** He has **forbidden Red Wizards, governors and soldiers to interfere** with worship, or with other temples' activities such as fundraising.
- **But it only seems like freedom, for now.** Tam is always watching for any threat to his rule or to Thay's supremacy.

## The High Autharch
- **What the post does:**
  - **settles disputes over policy**;
  - rules on **how officials should interpret** Tam's decrees or court rulings;
  - **settles disputes between officials**.
- **Rarely held more than about six years**, because holders **burn out or are assassinated**.
- **Why it exists:** Tam wants a **strong enforcer to keep the daily peace** while he pursues grander schemes of world domination.
- **Clerics of Bane often serve** as High Autharch.

## Ed's verdict on Szass Tam
- **"A failure, who keeps trying":** an overreacher whose plans fail again and again.
- **His yes-men:** to quell dissent he replaced almost all the **zulkirs with undead beholden to him** (the ultimate yes-men), and Thay's **armies with undead**.
  - He got total obedience, but nothing can **think beyond its controller's brain**: Tam's own.
  - So Thay keeps failing at conquest, and Tam has taken to **redefining his goals** to cover his failures.
- **The trade enclaves** started because Tam wanted **spies and assassins everywhere**, and a way to take over the world by trade. He **abandoned them because he changed his mind**.
- **Ordinary Thayans** have learned not to cross him, to keep their heads down, and to **enjoy the prosperity**.
- **The yes-undead zulkirs** are only the latest of many blunders driven by his **need to micromanage**.
- **How dangerous is he?** If his performance ever matched his ambitions, **Toril would stand on the brink of doom**. So far it has been, at best, **mildly threatened**.
- **What he misses:** his fierce focus means he often overlooks **activities, and even social changes, inside Thay**.
  - **Merchants** quietly rejoice, because it gives them room to operate.
  - **Nobles** enjoy the breathing space.
  - **Intruding adventurers** like it because it gives them a slender chance of survival.

## Where Thay may go to war
- **Aglarond:** he's gnawing at it, and **war is likely**.
- **Mulhorand and Unther** are stirring.
- **The Dragon Princes of Murghôm** are intercepting **more and more Thayan spies**.
  - If Tam moves their way, the dragons will **pull others in** through pacts, alliances, promises of treasure, and warnings that it's high time Thay, with its frightening undead armies and slaving ways, was wiped out.
- **The danger:** Tam will have to finesse things to **avoid a war on several fronts**: his traditional foes **Rashemen, Aglarond and Mulhorand**, plus Red Wizards scattered across Faerûn. Given his **record of lacking finesse**, things **could get big, and fast**.
- **His paranoia** is high and growing.

## Szass Tam's family, and his hidden heirs
- **Tam destroyed his few fellow Tam family members.** He wanted no rivals, and he wanted to be able to play Thayans off against each other by hinting that each was his favorite successor, without everyone assuming he'd simply name a relative.
  - **He is now the last Tam.** The others were **destroyed so thoroughly** that no resurrection or reanimation is possible.
  - **But there may be a "lost heir" he doesn't know about.**

### Secret: Brantarra, Red Wizardess of Murbant
- **A zulkir's private scheme:**
  - Tam had ordered Thay's spies in cities it had its eye on to be reinforced with wizards loyal to Thay, to prepare for peaceful takeovers by installing Thay-friendly rulers.
  - One zulkir "creatively interpreted" that. He recruited **gifted young locals** of cities like **Murbant** as **his own apprentices**: loyal to him first, Thay second, and Tam a distant third.
  - **Brantarra** was his triumph. He presented her to Tam, who approved; hence her title, **"Red Wizardess of Murbant."** Murbant was still independent then; it only officially became part of Thay in **1357 DR** (the *Spellbound* boxed set).
  - **Tam saw through the zulkir's deceptions and soon destroyed him.**
- **The plot against Cormyr:**
  - In **1352 DR**, Brantarra **slept with King Azoun IV of Cormyr**, then escaped (see *Cormyr: A Novel*).
  - The plan was for Thay to covertly take **Cormyr** by putting **Azoun's son on the Dragon Throne**, under Brantarra's mental control, while she was under Szass Tam's.
- **Her escape:** after the zulkir's death, Brantarra **fled**. **Several Chosen of Mystra secretly helped** her magically hide herself and her son from **Szass Tam** and from **Vangerdahast** of Cormyr.
- **A second son:** she had also conceived a son **by Szass Tam himself**.
- **The secret:** **two hidden grown men are out there** in the Realms under other names and guises:
  - **one a bastard heir to Cormyr's throne**;
  - **one Szass Tam's only blood heir**.

## How this fits the vault (Brodie decides)
- **Thay in Neverwinter, 1505:** by Ed's account Thay works abroad through **spies**, its trade enclaves are **abandoned**, and Tam's attention is on the east. That supports the idea that Thay's presence in Neverwinter would be **spies, not armies** (see [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Szass Tam's Schemes]]). The vault's earlier Thayan enclave in Neverwinter (after the Shadow War) would be long abandoned.
- **Slavery:** the handoff's Thay summary mentions a **huge slave population** (wiki, c. 1479). Ed says that by about 1500 **most slaves have been replaced by undead** (see [[_Neverwinter Handoff]]).
- **Hidden heirs** are a theme the vault already uses: the **lost Alagondar heirs** Dagult hunted (see [[Lore/The Lost Crown and Hidden Alagondar Vaults]], [[NPCs/Dagult Neverember]]).
- **Chosen of Mystra hiding people:** the same quiet network that guards Volo ([[NPCs/Volothamp Geddarm (Volo)]]), and Laeral is a Chosen ([[NPCs/Laeral Silverhand]]).
- **Cormyr:** a hidden bastard heir to the Dragon Throne connects to the vault's Cormyr notes, including [[Factions/House Crownrood]] and the War Wizards.
- **Gods:** Bane as Thay's state religion, and Tam opening the temples, sit alongside the vault's Shar and Selûne threads ([[NPCs/Selune and Shar - Sister Goddesses]]).
- **No movie lore**, and no clashes beyond the slavery note.
