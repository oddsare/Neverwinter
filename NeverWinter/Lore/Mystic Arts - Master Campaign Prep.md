# Mystic Arts: Master Campaign Prep

> Source: **Mystic Arts** (YouTube), host **Di**, the same channel as [[Lore/3 Faction Rule]]. Transcript added 2026-10-25.
> A campaign diary introducing the master prep document for **Kingmaker Everlasting**, Di's long-running home campaign (the third Kingmaker campaign, set in his own world, Atroxia). Summarized in my own words. This is DM advice, not Realms canon. The Patreon thanks and the fan-art shout-out are left out.

## How he uses a master prep document
- It's **a guiding plan, not a script.** He has no idea how much of it will come true and makes no effort to force it.
- He writes it so he knows **what to seed from session to session.**
- Most of the time he just runs one session at a time and makes sure everyone has fun. The document sits **far in the back of his mind.**

## A thesis for the whole campaign
- One line opens every session: **every new age is defined by the one that came before.**
- His world just came out of a great war. It left a dozen scars: a dead overlord, fractured nations, new dynasties, recessions and a changed magic system.
- Any one of those could cause a crisis alone. **The present is the moment when all the aftermaths boil over at once**, heading toward "the next war."
- His analogy: the years between the First and Second World Wars.

## The players: ordinary people first
- The campaign opened with a **prologue of about 38–39 solo and pair sessions**, one character or two at a time, before the group met.
- He told players **not to make people who were already adventurers.** He wanted to watch the change. A few could, as long as not everyone did.
- Most made people with jobs: a politician, a cop, a journalist, a business owner, a criminal, a construction worker, a lawyer.
- They meet in an untamed frontier county and become explorers, then adventurers, then rulers of that territory. **What they do there will decide the fate of the whole Republic.**

## Legacy characters
- Since the theme is legacy, several PCs are tied to characters from earlier campaigns:
  - a distant heir of an empire's founder (a PC the same player made years ago)
  - the daughter of an old PC
  - a warforged built by past PCs
- **Reverse-engineered legacy:** one player first played a father in a side adventure, deliberately as the parent of the character he'd play next.
- The rest of the party mixes this up: a fugitive noble turned thief, a journalist against the regime, a cop who believes in the system (but secretly does forbidden magic), a lawyer torn between law and the wild.

## House rules he handed out at the start
- Rules for **resting on excursions**, a **domain system**, **downtime jobs** and **permanent injuries**.
- **Players give each other Inspiration** for in-character moments or good drama, instead of it coming from rests.
- **Historic Inspiration:** when a character does something that would make the history books, they get a **d20 with a 20 on every face** to replace one roll later.
- His own **exploration rules.**

## No single villain: a rogues' gallery
- He didn't want **one obvious bad guy**, because that means one obvious plot. He wanted to find out what the *players* care about.
- So he made **a big list of possible villains to pull from**, like a comic-book rogues' gallery:
  - a corrupted church of a good goddess, led by a supremacist
  - a totalitarian political party founded by a secret devil-worshipper and fronted by a former president
  - two elemental titans left over from old adventures
  - **Asmodeus**, his likeliest main villain, who controls that party
  - **Orcus**, run as a prince of undeath, starting with one tribe in the frontier county
  - **Lolth** and a drow lieutenant
- **How he picked them:**
  - things he'd **never used before** (drow, Orcus, Asmodeus showing up in person)
  - things that come out of **his world's current situation**
  - **unresolved hooks from earlier adventures**, so they get an ending
- The biggest names are saved for the endgame, with **lieutenants** below them.

## The "deep state"
- A group of powerful people in the government who all work together behind the scenes: a former president, the party chairman, a spymaster who runs a private espionage firm, a corporate head, a mercenary-guild boss.
- He **cut one member** because that person was more interesting as an unpleasant ally the players could win.
- **Their goals are written down:**
  - stoke tensions by funding terrorism, cults and unrest
  - use fear to win the presidency and parliament
  - build up the military, special forces and new weapons
  - chase fringe theories (planar crossings) to build a new world order

## Pace and the big picture
- He told the players up front: **this is the main game for years.** Make characters for the long haul and don't expect level-ups often. They're at session 90-something and level 4.
- **He doesn't recommend this for new DMs**, only for a group that has already run long campaigns together. And if a player moves away, it could all fall apart.
- **The big arc:** explorers in the frontier, then the county's fate tied to the Republic's, then leading the Republic (or whatever replaces it), then the next war across the world. He wants **a big political campaign with factions and armies.**
