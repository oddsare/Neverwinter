# Sword Coast Reference — Neverwinter and Its Neighbors

> Curated reference of Neverwinter's geographic and political neighbors. Canon descriptions pulled from the AideDD Faerûn Interactive Map (Mike Schley). Campaign-relevance notes are specific to this campaign's threads (Claugiyliamatar, the Two Black Brothers, Shar/Selûne, the Kurtulmak's Chosen undercity, Luskan-as-Bad-faction, Dagult's missing treasury, etc.).

**Source:** [AideDD Faerûn Interactive Map](https://www.aidedd.org/atlas/faerun) by Mike Schley. Note that the map's lore text reflects an earlier era (pre-1502 DR in some places). Use the map for **geography**; use the vault's other lore docs for **current 1525 DR politics**.

---
## Immediate Region (Neverwinter's doorstep)

### Neverwinter

**Map coords:** x:1982, y:665

**Campaign relevance:** The campaign's home city. Renaer Neverember rules in 1525 DR. Map text below reflects an earlier era under Dagult — use map for geography, not current politics.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> The City of Skilled Hands was once known for its master crafters, fine goods, and lavish gardens heated year-round by the Neverwinter River, flowing from Mount Hotenow. When the volcano erupted and a great chasm to the Underdark opened, Neverwinter was nearly destroyed. Now, with the volcano's wrath spent and the chasm sealed, the city is rebuilding itself under the rule of Lord Dagult Neverember.
> 
>  MAP OF NEVERWINTER

### Neverwinter Wood

**Map coords:** x:2133, y:620

**Campaign relevance:** Directly east of the city. Canon 4e: contains the Dread Ring (Thay) and dark fey enclaves. A Thayan / cult of the Dragon presence.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> A dense forest with a sense of magic within it. The western region of the Neverwinter Wood is known as the Watcher's Forest, so named for its sacredness amongst the Helmite priests of the region. The Neverwinter River flows down from Mount Hotenow through the Wood. Abundant whitethistle shrubbery grows throughout the Wood. If consumed, the plant is known to give someone the runs. Shederran are a highly valuable species of trees found only within the Wood. They are highly sought after for their use in crafting the highest quality bows and crossbows.


---

### Helm's Hold

**Map coords:** x:2007, y:683

**Campaign relevance:** Walled abbey-fortress south of Neverwinter. Canon 4e: Abolethic Sovereignty plot via Rohini the Prophet operates here. Politically tied to Neverwinter through Heirs of Azure.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Helm's Hold is a fortified city and cathedral dedicated to Helm. A nearby stretch of Neverwinter Wood known as the Watcher's Forest is named for the fact it is tended by some residents of Helm's Hold. In the years following the cataclysm of the Spellplague, Helm's Hold became renowned as a place of safety and healing that catered to all those in need. As of the late 15th century DR, the town is controlled by the Order of the Gilded Eye, an over-zealous military organization dedicated to the eradication of extra-planar evil throughout the Realms.


---

### Mere of Dead Men

**Map coords:** x:2096, y:974

**Campaign relevance:** Cursed swamp south of Neverwinter on the High Road. Home of Voaraghamanthar + Waervaerendor (Two Black Brothers) and the lich Iniarbor. The flooding-spell that created the swamp is still active.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> A cold saltwater swamp. It is a desolate place full of monsters and few members of the civilized races. The swamp constantly grew over time, swallowing homes and property located close to its boundaries. The Mere itself is full of trees, vines, quicksand, and hidden islands, and it is generally covered in fog, making visibility very poor. The bones of fallen creatures are clearly visible throughout the Mere. The water is deep enough that it could be navigated on a flat-bottomed boat, but the dark water and hidden obstructions made that choice dangerous. Inside the Mere are the flooded ruins of a number of castles and settlements. Many of the castles had strange wards that would either strengthen certain magic or negate it.


---

### Sword Mountains

**Map coords:** x:2216, y:879

**Campaign relevance:** East of Neverwinter. Home to orc hordes that periodically raid. Useful as backdrop threat or location for the Kurtulmak's Chosen undercity.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> The western slopes of these mountains lead to the Trackless Sea in rolling foothills. The mountains themselves are steep and craggy, with snow-capped peaks. Gold and platinum are found in the foothills of the Sword Mountains. They are known to be home to several tribes of orcs and goblinoids.


---

### The Crags

**Map coords:** x:2116, y:429

**Campaign relevance:** Foothill region south-southeast of Neverwinter, between Neverwinter and the High Forest.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> As of 1451 DR, the discovery of the lost city of Gauntlgrym and the activation of the legendary forges within awakened the primordial Maegera within her prison beneath Mount Hotenow. In a fit of rage and fury, the once-slumbering primordial released a burst of energy that caused a massive volcanic eruption and subsequent earthquake. Nearby cities such as Neverwinter were left in a near-ruined state. Among the races that can be found throughout the crags are humans, dwarves, goblins, bugbears, hobgoblins, as well as gnolls, orcs, and ogres.


---

### Kryptgarden Forest

**Map coords:** x:2301, y:898

**Campaign relevance:** Claugiyliamatar's lair (Deeping Cave). North of Waterdeep, well within her claimed dominion. Tier 3 finale destination.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> A small but ancient and foreboding forest and one of the so-called the Moonwoods. Kryptgarden is a hilly woodland covered in a thick layer of foul-smelling fog, and contains many ruins of bygone dwarven civilizations. A portion of Kryptgarden south of Phandalin is known as the Crooked Forest because some of the trees there had been bent by ancient creatures that dwelled there to indicate paths through the forest. Small game animals live in the forest, but few larger creatures dwell within, as they were readily eaten by Kryptgarden's most fearsome predator, the dragon Claugiyliamatar. Kryptgarden Forest is reputed to be one of the sources of the evil that lurk in the ruined dwarfhold of Southkrypt, and only the second most powerful of them after the Sword Mountains.

## North Along the Coast

### Port Llast

**Map coords:** x:1941, y:590

**Campaign relevance:** Small port between Neverwinter and Luskan. Useful as a roadside encounter or small-investigation location.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Port Llast, formerly Grath's Hold and Last Port, is a small town of low stone houses and narrow streets, mainly known for its skilled stonecutters and excellent harbor—a small bay sheltered by a high, rocky spit, with a fine beach and inlet. The town is closely allied to Neverwinter, but Luskan covets Port Llast, wishing a southern base for its warships. Most of the humans of Port Llast are fishers, but Port Llast's primary land-bound industry is cutting stone, with quarries situated on the coastal headlands just to the south. Port Llast has a violent history, having been assaulted and/or occupied by sahuagin, pirates, a drow raiding party, and frost giants, all in the second half of the 15th century DR. It was always retaken and rebuilt.

### Mirabar

**Map coords:** x:2195, y:272

**Campaign relevance:** Major mining city further inland-north. Source of metal and trade. Commercially rival to Waterdeep.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> A mining city of great wealth, occupied by shield dwarves who live underground near to their workshops, which produce metal goods for export in the region and beyond. The humans above cooperate with the dwarves to handle the mining, move the ore to market, and defend the city against magical threats. Mirabar is ruled by a hereditary marchion, but the true power is in an elected assembly called the Council of Sparkling Stones, a mixed dwarven and human group that meets once a year to determine production quotas. On the surface, Mirabar is populated with squat stone buildings and a few stone towers. The area around the city is dotted with open mines and heaps of rock. Roads lead to its major mines in the Spine of the World mountains. Making maps of the city is illegal for security purposes, because the city is concerned about intelligence getting into the hands of Mirabar's enemies.

### Beorunna's Well

**Map coords:** x:3045, y:209

**Campaign relevance:** Gray Wolf Uthgardt tribe site. Relevant to the Netherese / Uthgardt alliance plot from the Campaign Guide.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> The Black Lion tribe of Uthgardt barbarians forsook their nomadic habits and settled in a small, roughly built town around their ancestral mound. They trade timber, furs, and smoked meat, notably with the dwarves of Citadel Felbarr. The town is surrounded by a stockade, and the modest houses are built around the deep pit that once contained the ancestral mound. At the bottom of the pit dwell Underdark monsters that the barbarians fear. Legend says that Beorunna, the father of Uthgar, defeated the demon Zukothoth at the ancestral mound, causing it to sink into the earth.

## South Along the High Road / Sword Coast

### Leilon

**Map coords:** x:2073, y:917

**Campaign relevance:** Small High Road town between Neverwinter and Waterdeep, on the edge of the Mere of Dead Men. The brothers may strike near here.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Leilon is a small mining settlement that also serves as a stop for travelers on the High Road. Its sits on the edge of a shallow mudflat that extends to the Sea of Swords, with the crags of the Sword Mountains looming over the town. Leilon was abandoned following the Spellplague but was rebuilt and resettled in the late 15th century DR with a wooden palisade and surrounding ditch. The original town contained stone cottages with slate or thatched roofs. As the town was rebuilt, much of the initial buildings were simple wooden constructions. Leilon's most defining landmark, the High Tower of Thalivar, was erected in the center of town by the wizard Thalivar sometime in the late 13th century DR, and was abandoned not long after when the wizard disappeared.


---

### Phandalin

**Map coords:** x:2136, y:865

**Campaign relevance:** Small mining town further south. Lost Mine of Phandelver canon hook — useful side adventure.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Originally a farming community, Phandalin was raided by orcs in 951 DR and subsequently abandoned. It was eventually resettled in the late 15th century as a frontier settlement of farmers and prospectors. As of the early 1490s, it is a modest town of hard-working frontiersfolk built atop the ruins of the old Phandalin, with about fifty well-maintained structures built of logs and flagstones. The town is dominated by an apple orchard on the west side and a ruined manor atop a hill to the east. Phandalin is adjacent to land which is good for farming and ranching, and is near enough to Neverwinter Wood to support a woodcutting and logging industry. Most of the wealth, however, is from mining in the nearby Sword Mountains for gold, platinum, ore, timber, skins, and cold iron.

### Conyberry

**Map coords:** x:2258, y:710

**Campaign relevance:** Small town near Neverwinter Wood. Map says: ruined/abandoned per latest canon.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Conyberry was a small village consisting of around a dozen houses standing close to the eastern side of Neverwinter Wood, sheltering within the forest edge. In the late 14th century DR, an unusual defender of Conyberry was the elven banshee named Agatha, whom the villagers looked on almost in affection as a protector and friend. However, in the early 15th century DR, members of the Uthgardt Gray Wolf Tribe attacked and destroyed the town. While the Gray Wolfs maintained their alliance with the Netherese, they used Conyberry as a place to store supplies and as training grounds, but after the Netherese were destroyed, the barbarians abandoned the town and left it laying in ruins.

### Westbridge

**Map coords:** x:2375, y:869

**Campaign relevance:** Crossroads town on the High Road south.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Westbridge earned its name for its location on the west side of the Stone Bridge. It is located at the intersection where the Long Road intersected with the Stone Trail. It is a farming village, but the residents often hunt game in the nearby Kryptgarden Forest.

### Goldenfields

**Map coords:** x:2406, y:1183

**Campaign relevance:** Massive Chauntean agricultural breadbasket south of Waterdeep. Claugiyliamatar canonically raids its fields.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Goldenfields, nicknamed the Granary of the North, is a walled abbey to Chauntea of spectacular scope and size. It is the most prolific provider of crops, grains and fruits to the people of the North. The walled temple-complex features a small town of several dozen longhouses, an abbey, an inn, a brewery and the vast farms, orchards and gardens for which the place is famous. Goldenfields provides much of the food consumed in Waterdeep and the neighboring settlements, but does not seek payment in return. They donate this food to others in honor of Chauntea.


---

### Amphail

**Map coords:** x:2304, y:1181

**Campaign relevance:** Small horse-country town in Waterdeep's hinterland.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Amphail was named after one of Waterdeep's early warlords, Amphail the Just, who had estates here. Amphail's main trade is growing food for Waterdeep, but it is also known for its outstanding horses—including Amphail grays, glossy black chargers, and Amphail fancys. Due to this, the village suffers from the stench of horse manure during the warmer months. The wealthier inhabitants or landowners of Amphail are generally the ones who breed horses.

### Red Larch

**Map coords:** x:2362, y:1011

**Campaign relevance:** Small town in Waterdeep's hinterland — Princes of the Apocalypse canon hook.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> A waystop on the Long Road seven days north of Waterdeep, located at the intersection of three trails. One trail leads to the Bargewright Inn, another to Kheldell, and the third runs into the hills to derelict, monster-infested keeps. The town was named for a stand of red larch trees that were chopped down about the time of the town's founding. Red Larch is known for its nourishing, though otherwise non-noteworthy food, called crumblecake.

### Waterdeep

**Map coords:** x:2299, y:1301

**Campaign relevance:** City of Splendors. Khelben Blackstaff's seat. Holder of the missing embezzled treasury. Canonically Claugiyliamatar's primary target city.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Also known as the City of Splendors, the bustling city of Waterdeep is ruled by a lordly council. The current Open Lord, Laeral Silverhand, keeps her identity known to the public. The Masked Lords rule alongside her anonymously, thus preventing any of them from being bribed or threatened. Waterdeep boasts a prodigious magical academy at Blackstaff Tower, which is run by a wizard holding the office of the Blackstaff—currently Vajra Safahr.
> 
>  MAP OF WATERDEEP


---

### Daggerford

**Map coords:** x:2480, y:1442

**Campaign relevance:** Small town south of Waterdeep.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> While it is a small town, primarily a farming community, and considered by some to be a mere stopover, Daggerford has great ambitions to grow and be seen as an alternative to their northern neighbor of Waterdeep. Due to its key location, Daggerford is a vital mercantile hub for its region of the Sword Coast. It is the site where goods moving on ships traversing the Delimbiyr River can be transferred to caravans journeying along the Trade Way or vice versa. It is a walled settlement with a population that by and large lives in the outlying hamlets, farms, and estates, rather than within the town proper. Surrounding the town wall is a modest moat, and sitting atop a hill in the center of the town is the grand Ducal Castle, that has served the dukes of Daggerford since the age of their founder, Tyndal.


---

### Baldur's Gate

**Map coords:** x:2722, y:2347

**Campaign relevance:** Far south metropolis. Possible source of foreign characters.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> The grim, crowded city of Baldur's Gate sits alongside the River Chionthar. Its nobles, called patriars, reside in the Upper City while most of the rest of the populace lives in the Lower City. Refugees and the destitute make what homes they can in the Outer City. A short way to the east sits the walled district of Little Calimshan, a thriving neighborhood of immigrants from that southern realm. The Council of Four governs Baldur's Gate, led by Grand Duke Ulder Ravengard.
> 
>  MAP OF BALDUR'S GATE

## Inland — The Silver Marches and Beyond

### Silverymoon

**Map coords:** x:2901, y:491

**Campaign relevance:** Alustriel Silverhand's city. Many-Starred Cloak base — Ophala Cheldarstorn's mage fraternity. The decoy-soap-opera scrying op against Claugiyliamatar is run from here.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Silverymoon, sometimes translated as Aerasumé in ancient Elven, is a sturdy, vibrant city, and it thrives as the pinnacle of human culture. Education and the arts, particularly musical ones, are valued highly in the Moon, but it is most famous for its pervading, magical tradition, and its being home to many notable wizards, sorcerers, and other mages. Revered as the Gem of the North, Silverymoon is also the prime signatory state of the League of the Silver Marches and, therefore, viewed by many as the capital. For over a century, it was ruled by High Lady Alustriel, a powerful wizard and one of the Seven Sisters, as well as a Chosen of Mystra.


---

### Sundabar

**Map coords:** x:3195, y:451

**Campaign relevance:** Trade city in the Silver Marches.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Sundabar, previously known as Citadel Sundbarr, was one of the wealthiest and most militaristic cities within the nation of Luruar, or all of Faerûn for that matter. While it was originally a dwarven citadel, it evolved as its demographics, exports and overall culture changed. One thing remained however, Sundabar continued to serve as the rampart of the north, keeping the realm's dangers beyond civilized lands. Sundabar is a fortress. Its harsh and treeless surface is surrounded by two ringed walls, surrounded by Tarnar's Moat, a water-filled trench that is rumored to be filled with man-eating eels. Within its walls, Sundabar's massive stone buildings are encircled by cobblestone streets, with no flora to be found. Beneath Sundabar is a volcano rift known as the Everfire. In 1484 DR, during the War of the Silver Marches, Sundabar was conquered by an orc army and renamed Hartusk Keep, and the surface population was completely wiped out.


---

### Mithral Hall

**Map coords:** x:2692, y:367

**Campaign relevance:** Dwarven city. King Bruenor Battlehammer canon — possible high-tier ally.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> One of the best known dwarven strongholds in Faerûn, originally a mithral mine belonging to Clan Battlehammer. Sunken into the valley known as Keeper's Dale, the main entrance to Mithral Hall is kept by two huge, well guarded granite portals. Once inside the city, one is faced with a maze of tunnels and numerous caves and rooms. The tunnels and chambers of Mithral Hall are a self-contained city complete with taverns, forges, workshops, homes, and shrines to Moradin, Clangeddin, and Dumathoin. After being built by Gandalug Battlehammer and his kin, Mithral Hall accumulated a long and fascinating history filled with all manner of heroes, villains, victory and defeat. As of 1479 DR, the peace treaty signed in 1372 by Bruenor Battlehammer and the orc king Obould still holds true.


---

### Citadel Adbar

**Map coords:** x:3346, y:200

**Campaign relevance:** Dwarven mountain fortress.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> This dwarven citadel consists of a massive fortified fortress that provides surface access to miles and miles of defensive granite corridors, tunnels, and hallways that wind their way under the Ice Mountains. The dwarves dwelling here make their living by mining, smelting and forging their native ores. Their constant work causes a permanent cloud of smoke to hang over the city and creates such a din and clangor that it is just as well that the city does not entertain many guests. Like other dwarven communities in Faerûn, the number of dwarves in Citadel Adbar had been steadily dwindling over the last several generations.


---

### Gauntlgrym

**Map coords:** x:2046, y:524

**Campaign relevance:** Ancient dwarven city. Houses Maegera the Inferno (primordial), the Hosttower-Maegera connection (4e Neverwinter Campaign Setting). Politically: dwarves + drow Bregan D'aerthe overlap.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Gauntlgrym was an ancient dwarven city and the capital of the Delzoun. The city was, at first, occupied only by dwarves, but during its rise to power, it gained in notoriety and developed populations of humans, elves, gnomes, and other races, until its fall in −111 DR. After that, it was resettled briefly by humans until it was overtaken by illithids. After its location was lost for centuries, it was reclaimed and resettled by dwarves under the leadership of King Bruenor Battlehammer in the late 15th century DR. The city is built on a massive scale, with many doorways large enough that even dragons can pass through them. The city is adorned with icons to the dwarven pantheon and other dwarven heroes.


---

### Loudwater

**Map coords:** x:3013, y:1202

**Campaign relevance:** South of the Silver Marches. Edge of the High Forest.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Loudwater, called the City of Grottos, sits on the confluence of the Delimbiyr and Greyflow rivers in the Delimbiyr Vale. In this stretch, the Delimbiyr is especially wide, giving the settlement enough room for a modest harbor. It is a picturesque garden city home to a pleasant and thriving cosmopolitan community of humans, half-elves, elves, and many other species. It is also the home of Mielikki's chosen, the Green Regents and their Scions, and thanks to their efforts the city serves as the very image of her faith. The city enjoys both fertile farmland and a key location for commerce, allowing it to both flourish and prosper. The local people make a living through farming and fishing, as well as by providing services to caravans. Loudwater is famed for its many grottos. These small caves and hollows between the hills are believed to be sacred to the city.

### Longsaddle

**Map coords:** x:2301, y:561

**Campaign relevance:** Harpell wizard family seat. Eccentric mage faction.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> A sleepy little hamlet which, despite its small size, is a member of the Lords' Alliance thanks to the presence of the powerful Harpell family of wizards. This community is famous for the presence of peculiar wizards who are eccentric and often dangerous in their spell-casting manners. The area surrounding Longsaddle is a vast expanse of grassland that caters to the town's ranchers, and the forest around the town is inhabited by werewolf descendants of Bidderdoo Harpell. These werewolves are called 'the Bidderdoos' and although the townsfolk fear them, they provide some measure of protection for the town.


---

### Triboar

**Map coords:** x:2388, y:743

**Campaign relevance:** Fortress trade town. Renaer's mercenary supply chain.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Triboar is a lively crossroads frontier town full of the hustle and bustle of roaming merchants, caravanners, and other travelers. At the heart of the town, where the roads cross, is a vast marketplace where local farmers, ranchers, and roadside salespeople offer their wares. The Triboar economy revolves around trade, by selling wagons and caravan services, as well as by farming and dealing in horses, cattle, and forged ironware.


---

### Yartar

**Map coords:** x:2498, y:736

**Campaign relevance:** Crossroads town on the Dessarin River.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> Yartar is a prosperous and increasingly crowded fortified city, alive and full of trade, day and night. It is a member city of the Lords' Alliance, and holds a number of allies throughout the North. As a crossroads trading town, it is always bustling with caravans entering and leaving its walls. It is renown as a place for merchants, smugglers, mercenaries, and spies from all over the North to meet and conduct business. Due to its unique location, on a major road near the crossing of two rivers, Yartar also enjoys a thriving fishing community.


---

### High Forest

**Map coords:** x:2742, y:942

**Campaign relevance:** Massive ancient forest. Iliyanbruen successor / fey presence, dryads, treants. Many-Starred Cloak interests here.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> The High Forest, also known as the Great Everwood, is a remnant of the days of old, when all of Faerûn was covered in green and elves, giants and dragons ruled the world. It is recorded as being over 500 miles (800 km) in length from north to south and covers nearly 20% of the lands of what called the 'Savage Frontier', so it is considered the greatest forest in Faerûn. The region of forest within the wide bend of the Heartblood River is called the Dire Wood, where a great massacre once took place. Strange supernatural phenomena originate from this place. At the northern end of the High Forest is an area called Turlang's Wood, a place where the forest is continuing to expand thanks to the tending of a band of treants. The forest is not ruled by any one group, but instead contains many forces and groups, including Fey'ri, nature-worshiping druids, Uthgardt barbarians, and the elven Caerilcarn, often in competition with one another. The most powerful of these are the treants led by the elderly Turlang.

## Far North — Frontier

### Bryn Shander

**Map coords:** x:1777, y:166

**Campaign relevance:** Capital of the Ten Towns of Icewind Dale. Far north frontier town.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> The biggest of the Ten Towns, a trading center in Icewind Dale, the gateway to its fellow towns of the dale, as well as their lifeline to the rest of civilization to the south. Folks from all around the surrounding settlements gather in 'the Tenth Town' to sell their goods, primarily consisting of scrimshaw made from the bones of knucklehead trout. Merchants from the lands south of the Wall bring in goods that cannot be found in the Frozenfar, such as spices, fruits, wines and hardwood lumber, from as far away as the Heartlands. The circular walled town itself is placed upon a hill, with snowy plains stretching in all directions. Because of this defensive location, residents of cities east of Bryn Shander often take refuge within the city walls during wartime.


---

### Spine of the World

**Map coords:** x:2446, y:75

**Campaign relevance:** Mountain barrier separating Icewind Dale from the rest of Faerûn.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> The Spine of the World is a mountain range that stretched from the Cold Run near the Sea of Moving Ice, to the Ice Spires north of the Silver Marches. Folks all across the lands of the Savage Frontier and the Silver Marches, particularly those in Scornubel, refer to the Spine as 'the Wall'. On the winter days when the white snow cover faces of the peaks met the iron-gray of the sunless winter sky, it appeared as though the peaks were holding up sky. To others, it simply marked the end of the world as they see it. It is home to frost giants, white dragons, and yetis.

### The Evermoors

**Map coords:** x:2709, y:539

### Lurkwood

**Map coords:** x:2374, y:327

**Campaign relevance:** Forest east of Neverwinter. Orcs, trolls.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> A great, temperate hardwood forest. It is one of the woodlands that are collectively called the Moonwoods by some. In ancient times, the Lurkwood was part of the High Forest, but it and many other woodlands were split off by the conflicts of the Crown Wars. The southern area of Lurkwood is safe and attracts loggers, hunters, and woodsmen from the neighboring communities, but its shadowy depths are largely mysterious, plagued with legends of bottomless bogs and deadly quicksand, and overrun with evil creatures, such as orcs. Several Uthgardt barbarian tribes live within the forest as well.

### Trielta Hills

**Map coords:** x:3314, y:2103

**Campaign relevance:** Hill region west of the Silver Marches, north of Mirabar.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> The Trielta Hills is an area of pastoral and idyllic rolling hills, spotted with rocky outcroppings, tiny farming settlements, shallow close-to-the-surface 'scratch mines', and an occasional fortified hold. They are home to many communities of rock gnomes and a few halflings, as well as three communities of orcs by 1492 DR. The entire Trielta Hills region is not menaced by raiders or warlords, dragons, or more sinister villains. There are not much to rob, pillage, or conquer. Despite their cruel culture, the orcs are seemingly uninvolved in any warfare, occupying at least one fortress in the region. Those who live in the hills prefere calm and seclusion. Because of the small volume of mining discoveries, no nation or trade consortium cared to claim the land or set up large-scale mining operations. Sometimes dangerous creatures from the nearby Forest of Wyrms are on the prowl in the hills.

### Star Mounts

**Map coords:** x:2899, y:881

**Campaign relevance:** Mountain range west of the High Forest. Ancient elf and dragon sites.

**Canon (from AideDD Faerûn Map):**

> A massive, ancient mountain range located in the very heart of the High Forest. These peaks are often shrouded in perpetual mist and clouds. They are home to powerful creatures like ancient dragons and giant eagles. The mountains also serve as the headwaters for several major rivers, including the Unicorn Run. Because they are so difficult to reach, they remain one of the most mysterious and unexplored parts of Faerûn.

## Notes on what is NOT on this map

- **Tarmalune** is canonically located on the southeastern shores of **Laerakond** (the continent that returned during the Spellplague). It is NOT on the Sword Coast — but considers itself **Faerûn-Waterdeep's commercial rival**. The Tarmalune Trade House in Neverwinter's Docks District is its outpost in our region.
- **Returned Abeir / Laerakond**, **Akanûl**, **Tymanther**: distant eastern continents, not on this map.
