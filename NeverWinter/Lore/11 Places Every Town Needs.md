# 11 Places Every Town Needs

A town-building checklist from a YouTube video, summarized in my own words. Transcript added 2026-10-23. This is worldbuilding advice, not Realms canon.

## The 11 places
1. **Tavern.** Where most things start: beds, rumors and quests. Taverns go back about 4,000 years to Babylon. The video calls them the social media of their day: hubs for trade, news and even revolutions.
2. **Seat of power.** A town hall or castle where lords or council members argue, decide and uphold the law. It handles everything from settling disputes to planning festivals.
3. **Healers.** A cleric, herbalist or barber. Historical barbers did everything from haircuts to amputations.
4. **Marketplace.** It grows where paths cross, starting with a few traders. The town's location shapes what's sold there (timber and herbs by a forest, fish and salt by the sea). It's also a place for gossip, alliances and secret deals.
5. **Mage's tower or arcane school.** Modeled on Plato's Academy, Aristotle's Lyceum and the medieval universities. It's where magic meets philosophy and science, and it shapes how the town understands magic.
6. **Smithy and workshops.** Tools, weapons, armor and horseshoes. A town's prosperity was often measured by its smiths' skill.
7. **Religious center.** A temple, church or shrine. It offers worship, but also community, learning, healing and sanctuary. Monasteries kept literacy alive until books spread after the 1500s.
8. **Guard barracks and town watch.** Where the defenders live and train, and where plans to protect the town are made.
9. **Water source.** Cities grew by rivers, lakes and springs (Rome on the Tiber). In fantasy it can be a plain well, or tunnels, magical currents or a healing spring.
10. **Food source.** Farms and pastures around the town, livestock, fishing, hunting and trade routes. In fantasy, the land, animals and trade goods can all be magical.
11. **Jail.** Historically it held people until their guilt was decided. It can hold magical beings or enemies of the state, have warded or secret cells, or hold innocent people. How a town uses its jail shows how far it goes to uphold justice, or not.
