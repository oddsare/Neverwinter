# Bestsellers of the Realms (Ed Greenwood)

Canon reference, summarized in my own words. Useful for filling bookshelves, libraries and props instead of "the pages are burned." Names come from auto-captions; spellings may be off. **Campaign connections are open threads.**

## Setting: Suzail, Cormyr, winter of 1357 DR
- Reading was popular across **all classes**, not just nobles and wealthy merchants.
- A busy trade in **chapbooks** (cheap booklets) and **broadsheets**, plus busy playhouses. Nobles hire actors to perform **at home** rather than attend public shows.

## What sold, from most to least popular
1. **Racy romances**, especially thinly disguised tell-alls about who slept with which lord or lady.
2. **Adventure memoirs:** daring deeds, swindles and skulduggery, travels through vividly described far-off places.
3. **Family sagas:** rags-to-riches fiction about plucky country heroes who outwit villains and rise into wealth or nobility.
4. **"True" family histories** of nobles, gentry, rising merchant families and longtime royal servants. Sales depended on how entertaining and **how revealing** they were.
5. **How-to chapbooks:** making wine or clothes, bestiaries, dealing with pests.

## Notable examples
**Romances**
- A cheerful, naive, improbably lucky heroine stumbling through steamy misadventures (readers see the trouble coming before she does).
- A lurid book-length account of a debauched revel thrown by mischievous young nobles at a **fictional** Suzail club, meant to catch their elders compromised. It ends with the club wrecked and burned. Probably written under a pen name by a **courtier**.
- **"Calathe's Crossed Blades"** *(spelling uncertain)*: a cross-dressing, sword-skilled country lass duels and romances her way through fictional noble houses, ending as consort to a high-ranking widow. **It caused a scandal by claiming two highborn women in Suzail were secretly drow using magic to look human.** That rumor never died and spawned folk beliefs across the Realms about **hidden drow women in cities**.

**Memoirs**
- A thinly fictionalized account of real scandals, swindles and smuggling in **Marsember**, ending with the "author" enchanted into an evil sorceress's shapeshifting octopus servant, dragging rich, careless people to watery deaths. Probably written by a bored Watch officer or War Wizard.
- A retired dungeon-delving, dragon-slaying adventurer's memoir under a **pen name**, full of catty remarks about fashions, rulers and nobles. **Banned and burned** in places, which only made it rarer and more valuable, sold under the counter. Rumor: the author married or became mistress to a prominent noble under a new name, and **bought copies of her own book** for her parlors.
- A guidebook of dubious accuracy to places far from Cormyr, with useful bits on inns and currencies. Its author vanished; rumor says a monster ate him while he relieved himself beside a caravan road.

**Family sagas**
- A handsome, penniless commoner becomes a successful maker of hats and coaches for the rich, loses three noble heiresses, and finally weds a passionate (fictional) noble widow.
- **"Black Blades Hall":** a **fictional** evil, decadent noble family brought to justice at sword-point by a swordmaker's daughter, who falls for the one good son and becomes the new lady when the rest are dead. **Its author, a mild shop assistant, was run down by a fast coach**, widely believed to be no accident but revenge by a noble who saw their own family in the book.
- **"The Ghost Baron":** a fictional villainous baron who keeps tormenting the living **after becoming undead**, thanks to his pacts with liches. Written under a pen name by someone who clearly knows magic and undead. **Still selling a century and a half later**, with endless speculation about **which real family** it's about, and whether undead ancestors behind closed doors still direct its schemes.

**Family histories**
- A farming family's true history: Purple Dragon soldiers, adventurers, a pirate, a daughter who wed a Waterdeep guildmaster and took three noble lovers at once. Close to the truth; the family was colorful enough not to need embellishing.
- A retired palace maid's account of her family's service under **16 kings of Cormyr**: decades of palace gossip, habits and private deeds. Elminster wished he'd known her better; a royal official wished he'd burned the diaries first.

**How-to**
- A flamboyant retired rogue's **bestiary**: from stinging insects to dragons, how to cook them, what smells they hate, how to beat them. Elminster: mostly nonsense, but a good read. Rumor says a jealous husband had him killed; Elminster believes he escaped to **Mintarn** under a new name.
- An accurate angling guide to Cormyr's rivers, with maps. Out of date now, but still useful in places.

## Around 1500 DR
- There's **no copyright**, only laws against counterfeiting **government** documents, so popular books are reprinted endlessly.
- **Best-known writers:** **Volo** (notorious); **Uldro Angast** of Baldur's Gate (sage and historian; botany, geology, gardening and farming); **Tamara Tasildar** (historical romance epics); **Torren Murmur** (short pop-science and history chapbooks).
- Waterdeep, Silverymoon and the cities of Cormyr and Sembia have many popular fiction writers.
- **Current fashion:** light-hearted adventure books mixing **bloody fights, passionate love scenes and slapstick** in one volume. Readers who want just one of those buy street chapbooks.

## Campaign connections (open threads)
- **Books as weapons in intrigue:** sagas that thinly disguise real noble families, and authors who die "by accident," suit Neverwinter's **four noble houses** ([[Campaign Decisions]] §1). An easy low-level hook.
- **Banned books** become valuable. A banned book about Neverwinter's nobles would be a political object.
- **Hidden drow posing as humans:** a folk belief that fits Lolth's Dark Queen (maybe) and the **masks** theme.
- **The Ghost Baron:** an undead ancestor secretly running a noble family echoes **Szass Tam's hidden heirs** ([[Campaign Decisions]] §9).
- **Volo:** see [[NPCs/Volothamp Geddarm (Volo)]].
- **Candlekeep:** the kinds of books on its shelves.
