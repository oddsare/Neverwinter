# Races and Alignment

Brodie's rules for how races and alignment work in this campaign (decided 2026-10-20). Summary also in [[Campaign Decisions]] §12.

## The rule
- Races are viewed the way **3.5 and early 5e** did. Some races **lean evil by nature**.
- They do evil things, but **they don't have to**. They lean that way **unless something changes them** or puts them on a different path.
- Brodie's reference: the demons in *Frieren*, but **changeable for players**.

## Which races lean evil
**Every race 3.5 lists as evil, including monsters that are now playable races.**

## What causes the lean
- Their **god**, their **culture** and their **nature**.
- **God and nature come first.**

## What can change them
- **Many things:** a **god**, a **choice**, **awful events**.
- **Whatever causes trauma can change a person.**

## Player characters
- **Players can be drow or orcs.**
- **How the world reacts:** cautious, **expecting the worst**, but **willing to open up if given the chance**.
- **Good deeds and reputation matter**, the same as for everyone. But people can be scared.

## How common are half-orcs and tieflings
- **Not rare anymore.** Both are **fully integrated into most societies**.
- **Fewer in number** than humans, dwarves, elves, halflings and gnomes, and **about as common as dragonborn**.
- **About 1 in 100 people each.** In Neverwinter (~50,000 people, [[Campaign Decisions]] §8), that's roughly **500 half-orcs, 500 tieflings and 500 dragonborn**.

## Half-orcs (treated differently)
- **Lineage plays a part.** Many are children of orc raids, born of pillage and rape. Some have happy families, but **turbulence is the norm**.
- They are **misunderstood**. Mostly they're **brash like their fathers**: strong, different, and willing and able to pick a fight.
- **That comes from culture.** Raised well and properly educated, they can be **the best of leaders and people**, strong enough to pick up a sword and protect others.
- **Their lean comes from culture and how people view them**, not from a god or their nature.

## Tieflings (treated differently)
- They come from **curses, or bloodlines that made pacts with demons long ago**, and are now **born as a race**. (Brodie, 2026-10-20: **demon pacts**, not devils. Canon example: the demon-worshipping kingdom of Narfell, see [[Lore/MrRhexx - History of the Realms]].)
- **Some stories still say two humans can have a tiefling child** when someone in the family **made a pact with a demon**.
- Those children are seen as **signs of their father's sins**: proof of the weakness that led to the pact.

### Notes
- **Orcs** were **lawful evil** in 1e and 2e (organized conquerors) and became **chaotic evil** in 3e (wild raiders).
- **Drow** went from **chaotic evil** (1e/2e) to **neutral evil** (3.5/5e), from wild cruelty to cold scheming.
