# Ed Greenwood — Szass Tam's Schemes

> Source: **Realms Lore** (YouTube), with **Ed Greenwood**. Transcript added 2026-10-19.
> Ed's "present" is the **late 1490s DR**; the campaign is later (1505).
> **Movie lore left out** (Brodie's rule). The video mentions the movie's attempted takeover of Neverwinter. The mass-killing spell Ed gives Tam is called by a name that comes from the movie, so it's described here without that name.

## Who he is
- **The lich who rules Thay.** He's known for ambitious schemes to **conquer neighboring realms, all of the Realms, or even other worlds and planes**, and to **become a god** in the process.
- **His schemes fail, time and again.** Examples:
  - The **Dread Ring**: a ritual meant to draw on the souls of the living, drained in the fortresses that formed the Ring, to power his bid for godhood. It would have **unmade Toril** along the way.
  - Repeated **invasions of Aglarond and Mulhorand**.
  - Attempted **takeovers of Neverwinter** and other places. (In canon these include the Dread Ring in Neverwinter Wood and Valindra's 1479 siege; see [[_Neverwinter Handoff]].)
- **Failure never stops him from trying again.** He's convinced of his own brilliance and "rightful" greatness, and sees every setback as **proof that his goal is so hard that only he could achieve it**.

## Thay's expansion now
- He's still working to **expand Thay into its neighbors and traditional foes**: **Rashemen, Aglarond and Mulhorand**, plus nearby **Threskel**, the **Wizards' Reach**, and the dragon-ruled realm of **Murghôm**.
- **He has stopped supporting the Thayan trade enclaves** he once set up all over Faerûn.
- Instead his **zulkirs and senior Red Wizards** are busy **expanding and strengthening Thay's spy network**. It's really **his personal network**, since Tam thinks of Thay as his.
- In the **Thaymount**, he keeps his most magically gifted **apprentices** and resident Red Wizards working on **new spells and rituals** to give Thay more fearsome battle-might. The goal is to easily defeat the **Dragon Princes of Murghôm**, the **liches who served Larloch**, the **Chosen of Mystra**, or anyone else.
  - **He has new apprentices.** He's still trying to *develop* a consort, if he can't find one.

## His latest private plot: Unther
Alongside all this, he is always hatching **private plans** to increase his personal power faster. The newest:

1. **Control Gilgeam.** He'll develop and covertly cast spells to bring the demigod **Gilgeam, Son of Victory**, the god-king of Unther, **under his mental control**, so as to conquer **Unthalass** and with it all of **Unther**.
2. **Rule Unther through paired Red Wizards.** All across the realm he'll install them in pairs: **one an open agent of his authority**, the other **a spy on the first** and on anyone who acts against them.
3. **Make Unther the invader.** He'll use Unther's might, and its wealth to hire mercenaries, to raise armies so that **Unther, not Thay, invades Mulhorand**.
4. **Deny everything.** Publicly, he'll claim his Red Wizards are in Unther because he has seen a **"great evil" secretly rising** there.
5. **Open a second front.** He'll mass Thay's **largely undead armies along the Mulhorand border**, supposedly for defense. Once Mulhorand is busy fighting Unther, **Thay invades as well**.
6. **Avoid the Dragon Princes of Murghôm.** He hopes to take Mulhorand without provoking them. If they do respond, he plans:
   - a **mass-killing spell** to wipe out any **mercenaries they hire** in their camps;
   - to develop, before he moves against Gilgeam at all, a new **dracorage spell or ritual** with an effect like the infamous **Rage of Dragons**, with or without the **King-Killer Star**;
   - to perfect **ghost dragons under his complete control**. He imagines them **entering the Dragon Princes' bodies as incorporeal beings, then solidifying and bursting the dragons apart from within**.

## The magic it needs
- **For Gilgeam**, three kinds of spells in order: spells to **slide into his mind and read it**, then spells to **influence his thinking**, then spells to **control him**.
  - From the reading spells, Tam hopes to **understand this entity's divine power**, aiming eventually to **take that godhood for himself**.
  - Past moves against other gods have taught him, **correctly**, that **gods vary in how much "divine fire" they have**, how it shows up as powers they can reach out with, and how it maintains and usually even **regenerates their bodies**.
- **For the Dragon Princes and their mercenaries:** he must create the spells he needs, still without moving against anyone and so giving away his goals.
  - He can already easily make **dracoliches, dragonbone golems** and the like, but won't until everything is ready. He may not make them at all, if the mass-killing spell can be used in the right places and enough to avoid tipping anyone off.
  - **Right now he makes dragonbone golems** in **scry-warded caverns inside the Thaymount**, then immediately "puts them to rest" in **waiting graves** to reinforce his undead troops later.
  - **He avoids making new dracoliches**, because they keep enough **free will** to endanger his secrecy.

## His strengths and weaknesses
- **Strength:** he repeatedly **succeeds at creating big, bold, "impossible" spells**. His new magic ends up as powerful and far-reaching as his dreams.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - he **misses flaws** in his own schemes;
  - he **relies on allies and underlings who fail him**;
  - he keeps **provoking many enemies at once**, out of hatred and fear, so he can't handle everything coming at him.
- **The Chosen can't kill him:** Elminster and the other Chosen don't like to admit it, but **Mystra and Azuth have forbidden them to destroy Szass Tam**. They may **thwart his schemes and undo the worst of his cruelties**, but not destroy him, or with him his tireless magical creations.
- **If you meet him and he seems absent-minded,** he's probably thinking about his latest plan to reach godhood, conquer the Realms, destroy his foes, or control gods and mighty dragons.

## How this fits the vault (Brodie decides)
- **Thay and Neverwinter:** canon already has Thay attacking Neverwinter without the movie: Sylora's Dread Ring in Neverwinter Wood and Valindra's 1479 siege (see [[_Neverwinter Handoff]]).
- **Thay in 1505:** with Tam focused on the east and the trade enclaves no longer funded, Thay's presence in Neverwinter would more likely be **spies** than armies. That's Brodie's call.
- **The Dragon Princes of Murghôm** are the dragons the bound red dragon's adventurers defend against Tam (see [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Seven Stars of Selune|Seven Stars of Selûne]]).
- **Other ties:**
  - **Lurs Thult** ([[NPCs/Unsung Heroes of the Realms]]) and **Murthra** ([[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Secrets of Neverwinter]]) both oppose the Red Wizards.
  - Elminster ranks House Cyl's mind flayer **below Szass Tam** in troublesomeness (see [[Factions/House Cyl]]).
