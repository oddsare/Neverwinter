# Ed Greenwood — Netheril

> Source: **Realms Lore** (YouTube), Ed Greenwood with host Ivan. Full transcript added 2026-10-19; only the end of the closing Realms Speak segment is cut off.
> Summarized in my own words.

## What Netheril was
- **An ancient human empire of powerful magic**, and the source of all sorts of spells, magic items and mighty artifacts.
- **For DMs:** anything ancient lying in a king's tomb or down a dungeon **can be "from Netheril."** When Ed created it, Netheril was meant as exactly that: a catch-all source of cool magic items.
- **What common folklore says:**
  - The empire **fell because of its arrogance and overreach**.
  - It had **flying cities** that could move about, so they didn't stay where they started.
  - Beyond that, most people know very little. It's a magical place of long ago, when magic was really powerful.

## Where their magic came from
- The humans who became the Netherese **started as workers for the elves**. The elves gave them a little magic to help with this job, and a little more for that one.
- Over time they realized **they could do it themselves**: magic wasn't only elven, and humans could wield it.

## High and low Netheril
- **Everyone started as "low" Netherese:** expert farmers, foragers and harvesters of the land.
- **When the flying cities began**, those who stayed on the ground became the **low Netherese**. They cut wood, drew water and farmed for the **high Netherese** in the flying cities.
- **Slavery varied:** in some cities the high Netherese literally enslaved the low, but **not in most**. Some cities had a **severe class system**, others were **quite egalitarian**. Ed warns that it's **dangerous to generalize**.
- **"High" and "low" were usually more about geography than class**, but it really varied from place to place.
- **"Arcanist"** was originally what the Netherese called themselves, rather than "archwizard." It wasn't a character class at first.

## Their magic and how it shaped rule
- **Everything was done with magic.** Ed's comparison is **Halruaa**, a surviving enclave of the scattered Netherese, which he calls "the land where the toasters are magical." Elsewhere in the Realms that's unthinkable, because people pay dearly for any spell.
- **Netheril was experimental:**
  - **Ioulaum** created the **first flying city** and the **mythallars**, the **forerunners of today's wards and mantles**.
  - **Ioulaum survives today**, but no longer human: he is **undead, and an elder brain**.
- **Magic varied from place to place, but there was a lot of it.** New discoveries **couldn't be kept secret for long**; whether the inventor wanted to share or not, everyone was soon using them. So magic **advanced by leaps and bounds**.
- **Magic came to decide who ruled, in practice rather than formally.**
  - Whoever could cast the spell could say yes or no. If you control the weather, you control whose crops fail.
  - With flying cities came **outright tyrants**, and a magical empire where people with magic ruled. Even that **varied from city to city**.

## What the Netherese looked like
- Many were **tall, slender and snotty**, wearing **all sorts of wild, experimental fashions**.
- You wouldn't see **lasting deformities or skin conditions**. They fixed anything they didn't like with magic, simply to avoid pain or disadvantage. It **wasn't a cult of physical perfection**.

## The fall
- **Ed deliberately left the fall as vague as possible.** Later designers have filled in details.
- **The story that has come down:**
  - the Netherese grew so strong they **challenged the gods**, and the gods **cast them down**;
  - some great spell or disaster happened;
  - **all the floating cities crashed and everyone died**.
  - That is **not true**: it's an oversimplification, usually wrapped in a moral lesson ("don't do what the Netherese did").
- **Some crashed cities can still be found today.** What happened to others, nobody knows. Wandering adventurers can stumble on one.

## Ruins
- **Low Netherese ruins** are **underground**: food storage cellars, and refuges from bad weather, including magically spun storms.
- **High Netherese ruins** look like **a crashed city on a mountaintop**.
- Ed left them mysterious so **every DM can do something different with each city**.

## Netheril as a campaign setting
- The easy route is to **leave Netheril in the past**. It works as a setting if it fits the stories you want to tell.
- **Ed's suggested era: right after the fall.**
  - **Misinformation** races around about why the cities are crashing.
  - **High Netherese** are scrambling off their cities.
  - **Low Netherese** are moving away so the city folk can no longer come back to **plunder their granaries**, and must meet them on their terms.
  - The result: exploring the unknown, **racing into a wilderness full of monsters** they'd mostly avoided until now. Ed's comparison is *Gilligan's Island*.
  - **Rising non-human peoples**, like hobgoblins and gnolls, see their chance now that the humans' power is collapsing. Characters may find they're being hunted for food. It's a fine time for a tarrasque.
- **His advice:** take players out of their comfort zone, but **don't be cruel**. They come to the table for adventure, not to have their souls crushed after a hard day at work.

## Secrets: two forgotten Netherese arcanists
Ed is more interested in the **people** of Netheril than its magic items, which have become a bucket you can put anything into.

### Hammardratha, "She Who Crafts Tyrants" *(spelled)*
- A **very powerful arcanist** who **magically modified many beholders**.
- She created **less-than-brilliant, obedient hunting beholders** with **tentacles as well as eyestalks**, and **custom eye powers for specific jobs** like **guarding and mining**.
- **She may have created** the **gauth**, the **death kiss** (bleeder), the **death tyrant**, or other beholder look-alikes.
  - Real-world reason: in early D&D, anything round with eyestalks was either a gas spore or a beholder. Players would shoot it from range: if it exploded, it was a gas spore; if not, run. Ed's look-alikes, introduced in *Dragon* magazine, broke that meta-gaming.

### Sardra *(spelled)* and Sardra's sequences
- An influential arcanist of her time, now forgotten.
- **Sardra's sequences** are **chains of spells cast in advance** that **hang invisible in mid-air, roiling, until triggered**. That frees the caster to prepare other spells.
- **The drawback:** **wild magic and overkill effects** when the spells collide or their areas overlap. That kept chain magic from becoming popular in Netheril.
  - It also explains why **chain magic isn't a common wizard specialty** today. Otherwise a 1st-level wizard could spend a week stringing together 20 battle spells.

## Realms Speak
- **Zhentarim** can be pronounced either **"ZHEN-"** or **"ZEHN-"**. Members are **Zhents**, and the adjective is **Zhentish**.
- The **Zhentilar** are the soldiers (the military); the **Zhentarim** are the evil organization itself.

## How this fits the vault (Brodie decides)
- **Xinlenal, near Neverwinter:**
  - The 4e Campaign Guide in this vault has **Ioulaum's Xinlenal, Netheril's first flying city**, lying **crashed in Neverwinter Wood**.
  - **Prince Clariburnus Tanthul** of the Shadovar is trying to **rebuild its shattered mythallar** and raise it again.
  - The Shadovar there also **hope to find Ioulaum**, who they believe fled north before Xinlenal fell. **Ed says Ioulaum is now an undead elder brain.**
  - See [[Lore/Campaign_Guide/Chapter_1_Jewel_of_the_North]] and [[Lore/Campaign_Guide/Chapter_3_Factions_and_Foes]].
- **Halruaa:** Ed's "surviving enclave" matches the vault.
  - [[Factions/House Cyl]] came from Halruaa.
  - [[NPCs/Zundaerazylym - The Laughing Wyrm|Zundaerazylym]] guarded a Halruaan noble family's tokens.
- **Elder brains and mind flayers:** Ioulaum adds another to the vault's list:
  - House Cyl's **Iscahill**;
  - **Ixruu Thalarkul** in Athkatla ([[NPCs/Volothamp Geddarm (Volo)]]);
  - the **Gauntlgrym elder brain** under the Abolethic Sovereignty (4e).
- **Mantles:** the **Lady Ghost** wears Netherese mantle spells ([[NPCs/Unsung Heroes of the Realms]]), and Ed traces mantles back to Ioulaum's mythallars. **Lurs Thult** has killed Netherese arcanists.
- **The Shadovar** tore a tower from Moonshadow Hall, and it floats in Neverwinter's Tower District ([[Lore/Neverwinter Sub-Locations]]).
- **No clashes** with the vault, and no movie lore.
