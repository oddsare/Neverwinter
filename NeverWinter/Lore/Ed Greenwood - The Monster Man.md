# Ed Greenwood — The Monster Man

> Source: **Realms Lore** (YouTube), Ed Greenwood with host Ivan. Transcript added 2026-10-19; it appears complete.
> Summarized in my own words.

## Who's talking
- Ed created the Forgotten Realms as a **child in 1965**, and has been making monsters even longer.
- He was **never officially on staff at TSR or Wizards of the Coast**. Still, through his work with them and with hobby magazines, he invented, or helped invent, many monsters still used today.
- **He has created more than a hundred D&D monsters**, among them the **curst, deepspawn, crawling claw, dracolich, dark naga and alhoon (illithilich)**.
- He also wrote **"ecologies"** for many others: their off-stage lives, breeding cycles and so on.

## Why he made monsters
- **To make players think, not recite.** Look-alike monsters, like the gas spore and his **beholder look-alikes**, meant players couldn't defeat something just by remembering its Monster Manual entry. (See Hammardratha in [[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Netheril]].)
- **To use familiar shapes with new threats.** He wanted his players to face things shaped like **ghosts, beholders and dragons** without the official damage those monsters deal.
- **To answer what happens after the fight.** Players bring back a creature in a cage, or its egg, scales, organs or blood. Then they:
  - try to **cook and eat** it, because they're stuck in the Underdark;
  - try to **sell** it, and want to know what they'll get;
  - use it as **spell components**, in **alchemical experiments**, or to make **spell inks**.

### The spell-ink mini-campaign
- In **2nd edition**, spell-ink formulas were a necessary part of writing spells down: one more rule to slow wizards' rise.
- Ed ran a **mini-campaign in Scornubel** where everyone tried to **invent spell-ink formulas and get rich** making and selling them.
- **The cast:** thieves; oily merchant traders; rogue wizards you just knew would resell the inks for triple the price in Waterdeep; and **spies for the Zhentarim and for Cormyr's War Wizards**, who wanted to seize the formulas.
- A **ready-made template for a trade-secret intrigue**.

### An aside on editing
Ed's digressions gave editors fits. **Jeff Grubb** used to prune long passages from his early manuscripts into files titled **"Ed's Digressions for Re-use"** in some later product.

## Making a monster make sense
- **To use monster parts, you have to know how it reproduces.**
  - Does it lay eggs?
  - If you snatch them, do they die soon after?
  - Do they need warmth or special handling?
- **To dress a lair, you have to know how it lives, where it prefers to dwell, and how it thinks.** For example:
  - Does it set **traps**?
  - Does it keep **multiple escape routes and back doors**?
  - Does it hide, or scare visitors off with **charnel pits, scattered bones, warning messages or corpse trophies**?
- **The ecologies:** as a contributing editor of *Dragon* (later "creative editor"), Ed's **first assignment from editor Kim Mohan** was writing "The Ecology of…" articles. They banked content for future issues and brought the game's monsters to life. Sometimes that meant **making a monster make sense**.

### The medusa problem, and the maedar
- **The problems:** if **all medusae are female**, how does the race survive? And if she looks at her dinner, it turns to stone. How does she ever eat anything but stone?
- **Ed's fix:** the **maedar**, a **male medusa who can turn stone back into flesh** (*Dragon* #106, February 1986). Now a medusa couple can actually eat.
- **The trick:** petrify a large meat animal, push it off a drop so it shatters, store the stone pieces, and turn small chunks back to flesh as needed. Just enough for whoever's coming to dinner.

### Filling the gaps
- The original Monster Manual pictures often **didn't show enough**: did it have wings, bat-like or feathered? How many legs?
- In a game where players **cut open and search everything they kill**, those gaps couldn't stand.
- Ed wrote **recipes** (baked stirge, dragon soup) and long lists of **alchemical uses** for parts of creatures like the **carrion crawler**.

## What he learned about monsters and the world
- **Humans and orcs are the fast-breeding, dominant races** whose numbers and reach exploded. Most monsters, meaning creatures those races fear, therefore either:
  - **learned to live with humans**, like **halflings and gnomes**, who are found in strength in almost every human city in the Realms; or
  - were **pushed into the wildest terrain** and became rarer.
- **Dragons must sleep most of the time**, for years at a stretch. Otherwise they'd have **emptied the world** long ago and would be fighting each other to the death over scraps or captive herds. The exception is dragons **guarding gates or portals** that bring them fresh food: explorers or blundering creatures from other planes.
- **Brainy monsters probably manipulate humans from behind the scenes.** Dragons, beholders, illithids and others likely use humans **like cattle, or for amusement, or both**. Perhaps they're even steering them toward some grand scheme, like an old science-fiction **human breeding program**.
- **There should be monster kingdoms**, just as there are human ones. Some would **ranch other species and tend crops**, both underground **mushroom farms** and tilled fields.
- **There must be creatures not yet in published lore**: the ones fearsome monsters eat, and the ones that fill other niches. **Swamps**, or the vast frozen wastes, can't be home to just remorhazes.
- **Creating new monsters** is one of the best ways to tailor a challenge to a specific place or moment in an adventure. Ed is still at it after 50 years.
- **His conclusion:** the most dangerous and fascinating monsters, the foes that challenge characters most, are **almost always humans**.

## Realms Speak: ixitxachitl
- The **sea-going, vampiric giant rays**. The emphasis falls on **"ZATCH"**. *(The captions garble the full pronunciation; check the video.)*
- *The Ecology of the Ixitxachitl* was one of Ed's early *Dragon* ecologies. He opened it by telling readers how to say the name, so they could actually use it at the table.

## How this fits the vault (Brodie decides)
- **Monsters steering humans from behind the scenes** describes the vault's mind flayers exactly:
  - House Cyl's **Iscahill** ([[Factions/House Cyl]]);
  - **Ixruu Thalarkul** and its Athkatla merchant network ([[NPCs/Volothamp Geddarm (Volo)]]).
- **"The most dangerous monsters are humans"** fits the campaign's political-intrigue spine.
- **Dragons sleeping for years**, or guarding portals for food, offers background logic for the campaign's many dragons.
- **The spell-ink mini-campaign** is a template for a **trade-secret intrigue**: thieves, merchants, rogue wizards, and **Zhentarim and War Wizard spies**. Neverwinter's powder is a similar trade secret (see [[Lore/The Powder]]).
- **Monster parts as trade goods** could feed into the vault's item-pricing notes (see [[Items/House Magic Item Prices]]).
- **Halflings and gnomes in strength in almost every human city** applies to Neverwinter's population.
- **No clashes** with the vault, and no movie lore.
