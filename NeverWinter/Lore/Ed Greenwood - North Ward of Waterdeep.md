# North Ward of Waterdeep (Ed Greenwood)

Ed Greenwood on Waterdeep's North Ward. Summarized in my own words. **Campaign connections are open threads.** See [[Locations/Waterdeep]].

## The ward
- **Prosperous, "not old noble money, but wealthy and grasping after prominence,"** though it has its poor, its struggling shop assistants, and some long-resident nobles in mansions.
- An image of **quiet respectability**; relatively safe. Clean streets, frequent Watch patrols (only Sea Ward is better served).
- Many buildings **reclad in light limestone**, with balconies, hooks for lamps and plants, sheltered porches, and tall ground floors.
- **One of the greenest wards:** deliberately planted window boxes and hanging plants, abundant **balcony gardens**, rooftop gardens starting to appear. Close enough to the sea for damp, far enough to keep the salt down. Cleaner air than anywhere but the upper slopes of Mount Waterdeep and the City of the Dead.
- **Neighbors keep to their own circles** and may know each other only by sight after years, but share a sense of community. An older name for it was **"Highclear"** (high and clear of the damp, reek and crowding of the Docks); the name's gone, the attitude remains.
- North Ward is home to **Trollskull Manor** (*Dragon Heist*).

## How a North Ward manor is built (Trollskull as baseline)
- **Fieldstone** ground and second floor; a stone-lined **cellar** with a level concrete floor, not connected to other buildings.
- Upper floors and attic **half-timbered** (box-frame truss, stucco between exposed beams).
- **Tiled roof** overhanging at least a foot, with downspouts; glazed tiles over lime-ash floors.
- **Indoor toilets** flushed by roof cisterns down interior pipes to the sewers; closets between rooms for sound privacy.
- Bedchambers upstairs; attics for cisterns, old furniture, sometimes caged pigeons for the table. **Servants sleep in the kitchens, pantries and entry room**, for security.
- Heavy wooden interior doors; a wood-framed open arch between dining room and main room.
- A main stair wide enough for furniture and a narrow (often spiral) servants' stair; a true mansion has wider, gentler stairs.
- **Diamond-paned blown-glass windows**, shutters inside and out; spare "overwindows" kept for broken panes. Balconies on grand houses. Sliding pocket doors are a newer luxury.
- **Hanging oil lamps or candle-lanterns** on fine-chain pulleys with frosted glass bowl shades.

## The Silent Nine
- Since shortly after **Ahghairon** died, **nine silver and gold dragons** have lived in North Ward, mostly in human shape.
- They long ago **modified the city's dragonwards** to ignore them, using enchanted tokens they later **embedded in their scales**. Laeral warns these aren't the only **pass-tokens** that let dragons bypass the wards, and some take **surprising forms**.
- **First noticed in the 1360s** by Blackstaff Tower apprentices, whom **the Blackstaff told to mind their own business**.
- They act mostly alone but like each other and cooperate when needed. They **watch criminals, Red Wizards, Dragon cultists and Arcane Brotherhood spies** (North Ward is where outland agents who aren't official envoys like to live).
- **Current human aliases** (changed now and then to hide their long lives, with invented family histories and real careers):
  - **Avalys Moonwindwynter** (Sashstar Street): quiet, bespectacled, raven-haired woman; sells maps and books.
  - **Haldrin Methland** (Horn Street): amiable, solid elderly man; restores and reforges blades and sheaths.
  - **Emtheira "Emberhair" Burlhaern** (Stallion Street): ankle-length copper-to-crimson hair; carves decorative door and window trim, and relief portraits of people for their own doors.
  - **Maulraus Marunelion, "the Lion of Lion Street":** weapons trainer, sells used armor; ex-mercenary captain and adventurer; red-skinned, long black hair, beak nose, odd yellow eyes, quick smile.
- They **don't like dropping their disguises**; forcing one to is dangerous. They know the city well, have **arsenals of magic items**, **trapped homes**, **backup properties** and **spare human personas**.
- Believed behind several **disappearances in the 1470s–1480s**, including **Irvild Murltam**, a Calishite assassin who stayed to sell murders to merchants. After two clients, he vanished. One of the Nine: *"I spat out his boots."*

## Local oddities
- **The Clutching Skeleton:** a prank by mid-level wizard **Aldamund Stonecloak** (1300s) after a rival said he was no good at necromancy. It teleported around North Ward grabbing people, harmlessly. **Someone else seized control of it**, made it deadly, and used it as a **night assassin**. Stonecloak vanished, probably killed by that someone. The first two times it was destroyed it **exploded**; the third just ended it. Copycats followed until **the Lords passed a specific law with heavy fines**; one offender lost his home over it. The prank lost its appeal. "For now."
- **Treasure tales:** most are generic and retold everywhere. One is truly North Ward's:
  - **Dorlen Spikeshield, the Sculptor of Immar Street:** a white-bearded, bald dwarf who **never named his clan** ("Spikeshield" was invented for a tax collector). Lived alone, worked constantly, sold statuettes, doorstops and bowls.
  - He **vanished in late summer 1486 DR**. Then a broken statuette turned out to hold **60+ bloodstones**. **Every statue hid cut, polished gems** (bowls and doorstops held nothing).
  - Someone had already **broken in and taken scores of statues**, not far given their weight. The Watch guarded the rest until the **Watchful Order** spell-sealed the shop; a tenday later the rest were gone, **taken from below through existing hidden ways** into one small sewer section that drains about 120 buildings. Trail ends there.
  - Open: why he hid gems, where they came from, where they are now.

## Locals to beware
- **"Lady Monster," Maerthra Dunvelgeld** (Tower March): tall, smiling, mature woman, claims to be a retired Chessentan adventurer. **Breeds and sells monsters** and sends hired bands across the Sword Coast North for live or dead monsters and parts. Her parlor is full of trophies for sale. **One gold piece** gets you an introduction to a suitable adventuring band; everything else is negotiated. Rumors: works with **doppelgangers**, gets night visits from **drow and mind flayers**, a **grell** guards her bedchamber. **Very friendly with certain Masked Lords and noble house heads.**
- **"The Mighty Manspider," Orbrel Harhammath** (Nindabar Street): paunchy, nondescript, prim lens-grinder (monocles, spectacles, lorgnettes, and "starshields" that project patterns from lamplight). Superb work, about **50 gp a lens**. He **bottles up anger until he shapechanges into a giant spider** and hunts, sometimes killing (webbing victims to suffocate). Cowardly ambusher. Money trouble has turned him to **robbery**: only coin, trade-bars, loose gems, tools and weapons, nothing traceable. Avoids the Palace, the Castle and Dock Ward. Remembers every face, address and debt.
- **Vressa Iyrunhund, "Vressa's Sweep Begone"** (Saerdoun Street): ratcatcher for North and Sea Ward homes. Uses cages, capture-satchels, and **cantrips** that lure, sear with heat beams, grab with sticky tendrils, and blow tight gusts. **25 gp a sweep if she catches nothing, 50 gp if she catches anything.** Also recovers escaped pet snakes and dropped rings from pipes. Doesn't gossip but has seen a lot in 20 years. Lives alone by choice; her lovers are **half-orc caravan merchants**. Twice investigated for thievery and cleared. **Drinks with Harpers and visiting wizards**; nobody knows if she's a Harper. Defended by **four enchanted flying daggers** under her mental control (they keep following her last order if she's knocked out), which she can make trail smoke, glow, repeat messages, or pull cord. Nobles increasingly trust her.
