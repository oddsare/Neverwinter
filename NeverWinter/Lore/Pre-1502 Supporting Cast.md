# Pre-1502 Supporting Cast

> [!warning] Supplemental Material
> Lifted and paraphrased from a fan-made guide describing Neverwinter as it stood in the **early 1490s DR** — roughly **30+ years before the campaign's 1525 DR start.** Use as historical backdrop / DM resource. Every NPC below was alive and active in the 1490s; most are now aged, retired, deceased, or replaced by successors in 1525.

> [!info] Status by 1525 DR — guidance
> The "Status by 1525 DR" line on each entry is a suggested DM read, not vault canon. Treat each as a dial: alive-and-aged, retired, dead, or replaced by a successor. With Dagult Neverember **missing since 1502 DR**, anyone whose 1490s role depended on his patronage is especially likely to have lost their position or fled.

---

## The Moonstone Mask

### Liset Cheldar — *half-elf, proprietor of the Moonstone Mask* (pp. 9–10)
A friendly, flirtatious half-elf woman who became the Mask's proprietor in the wake of Neverember's "New Neverwinter" movement. The guide presents her as the **descendant of [[NPCs/Ophala Cheldarstorn]]**, the Mask's original founder. Unlike Ophala — a powerful mage of the Many-Starred Cloak — Liset displays no obvious arcane talent. She convinced Neverember she was Ophala's rightful heir and got him to fund the inn's restoration; the Mask in turn housed Neverember and his officers throughout the 1470s–1480s DR.

> [!note] Vault canon flip
> Vault canon may treat Liset as Ophala's descendant or as a stand-in / impostor. Either reading fits the guide's deliberately ambiguous framing of her claim.
>
> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Aged but possibly still alive — half-elves live long.* If she was ~30 in the 1490s, she's ~60 in 1525 (middle-aged for a half-elf). Plausibly still running the Mask, or recently retired in favor of a successor.

## House of Knowledge

### Grand Scribe Spivey Liethennson — *iron-fisted overseer* (p. 7)
Stern, short-tempered. Oversaw the post-Chasm reconstruction of the House of Knowledge with an iron fist. Acts as gatekeeper for anyone seeking access to sensitive records.

> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Likely aged and possibly deceased — successor likely.* A new Grand Scribe is the simplest read. The role itself persists.

### Loremaster Atlavast — *half-mad young Loremaster, then aged recluse* (p. 7)
A dedicated young Loremaster of Oghma who, during Mount Hotenow's 1451 eruption, **sealed himself inside the temple's inner sanctum** with much of the library's most precious contents. He survived alone for years — saving countless rare tomes, documents, and artifacts — but grew reclusive and a little mad from isolation. By the 1490s he obsessively re-catalogued and reorganized the collection and was deeply suspicious of anyone wanting access.

> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Almost certainly deceased.* He was already aged and unstable in the 1490s. His catalogue system (and possibly his ghost or a curse on the inner sanctum) may persist.

## The Beached Leviathan (Docks)

### Captain Harrag — *one-legged ex-pirate, proprietor* (pp. 11–13)
Burly, hairy, **one-legged** (lost the other to sahuagin). Once an infamous pirate captain of *The Leviathan*; after running his ship aground in northern Neverwinter post-Hotenow, he converted the wreck into a thriving inn and tavern. Outwardly gruff and fearsome; those close to him know he's compassionate and deeply protective of his establishment. Capable of being cutthroat when business demands it.

**Secret loyalty:** A true **Neverember loyalist** — Neverember personally expunged Harrag's piratical record to make the legitimate business possible. In return, Harrag serves as a discreet asset. Neverember even arranged for **Harrag's own shadow to be animated as a sentient creature**, used to spy on the tavern (p. 13).

> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Aged, possibly deceased; loyalty obsolete.* He'd be elderly even if alive. Without Neverember (missing since 1502), his political utility is gone. The sentient shadow is a great loose-thread hook — does it still spy? For whom?

### Beached Leviathan crew & regulars (pp. 12–13)
Becoming a regular earned a nautical nickname.

**Staff ("Crew"):**
- **Andrella** — "Mate." Middle-aged human woman, lead barmaid. Leads the staff when Harrag's absent. Harbors an unrequited secret love for the Captain. (Other barmaids: Jyseria, Sandrine, Willow.)
- **Feng** — Very large male half-orc. Bouncer.
- **Tabnab** — Oddball human male. Cook.

**Regulars:**
- **Len-jes** — "Harbormaster." Female scarred watersoul genasi; actual Harbormaster of Neverwinter; rents a room semi-regularly.
- **Umi** — "Helmswoman." Female watersoul genasi, Len-jes's cousin; backup muscle for Feng; highly skilled with the sword.
- **Bobrik Highmantle** — "Bosun." Stout male dwarf with a pet monkey. Fiery temper that periodically gets him kicked out for a few days.
- **Markul** — "Lookout." Young human man; eavesdropper extraordinaire. Sells secrets for the right price, but never anything that would harm the Leviathan.
- **Ettain** — "Caulker." Half-elf male handyman/carpenter. Knows every square inch of the ship, including its secrets.
- **Korin** — "Chanteyman." Middle-aged male dragonborn bard. Plays and sings; gets the whole bar going on sea shanties and folk songs. Family was slaughtered by cultists — occasionally surfaces in a sorrowful dirge.

> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Mixed.* Andrella, Tabnab, Markul, Korin would be elderly. Feng (half-orc) probably retired or dead. Bobrik (dwarf) likely still going strong. The genasi cousins (Len-jes and Umi) — uncertain lifespan, plausibly still active. Ettain (half-elf) likely middle-aged and still working. Treat the Leviathan as having a turned-over crew with maybe one or two holdovers and a new captain.

## The Driftwood Tavern (Docks)

### Madame Rosene — *proprietor; secret Graycloak leader* (p. 13)
Roughly **70 years old in the 1490s**. Runs the Driftwood — Neverwinter's living museum of a tavern. A fount of city history, happy to chat about the old days. In **1479 DR**, she secretly led the **Sons of Alagondar (the Graycloaks)** — the political/subterfuge wing of the resistance against Neverember. By the 1490s the Sons had largely made peace with his rule; many former members enlisted in the city army.

> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Almost certainly deceased — would be ~100.* Tavern likely persists under a successor; her relic collection alone made the Driftwood an institution.

## Neverember's Military

### General Sabine — *Neverember's top general* (p. 13)
Neverember's senior military commander. Personally intervened to save the Beached Leviathan during the 1479 attack tied to the false-crown plot. Also lost one of her top advisors to an Ashmadai infiltration of the Leviathan that same year — part of the Elden Vargas affair.

> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Unknown — successor likely.* When Neverember vanished in 1502, his command structure either collapsed, defected, or splintered during the Dance of the Revolving Lords. Sabine, if alive, may be retired, in exile, or running her own faction. Good hook material.

## Tower District

### Vagdru One-Ear — *orc proprietor of the Fallen Tower* (p. 17)
Single-eared orc of the **Many-Arrows** tribe. Restored the abandoned Fallen Tower as a working tavern after the phantasmic show terrified the original looters. Under a personal truce between Neverember and the Many-Arrows leadership, the tavern operates as **neutral ground** between orcs and other races. Runs a tight house; troublemakers face the wrath of the regulars. Cheap ale, raw meat of unspecified origin cooked over fire.

> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Possibly aged but plausibly still alive — orcs in a tavern role aren't dying of old age fast.* The Neverember-Many-Arrows truce itself is the bigger question by 1525.

### Llomnauvel "Firehands" Oloadhin — *historical wizard, source of the Fallen Tower spectacle* (p. 16)
Powerful wizard who owned the tower **decades before the Spellplague**. The [[Factions/Arcane Brotherhood]] raided him for his magical collection. His tower was rigged with elaborate traps (including spells that transformed intruders into lowly sea creatures), but the assault overwhelmed him. Rather than surrender his goods, the **unhinged** Oloadhin triggered a massive destruction-spell that levelled the tower and killed everyone inside, himself included. The **nightly phantasmic re-enactment** at the Fallen Tower replays his and the Brotherhood's final moments.

> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Long dead.* The phantasms persist; the spectacle continues.

## The Vargas Affair (1479 DR)

### Elden Vargas — *Cormyrean War Wizard* (p. 13, expanded p. 28)
A powerful Cormyrean War Wizard. The **Ashmadai cultists** kidnapped his wife **Karis** and committed her to **Helm's Hold**, exploiting Vargas's desperation. Vargas turned to **Asmodeus**'s power to break her free, and in the process — using his substantial psychic magic — **drove much of Neverwinter's populace into madness**. The conflict spilled into the Beached Leviathan when Ashmadai cultists and demons killed one of General Sabine's top advisors there. Vargas eventually left Neverwinter for Helm's Hold, where adventurers ultimately captured or killed him; he never returned. He sealed his Bluelake townhouse with abjuration magic before leaving, and it remains shut to this day. A note pinned in his bedroom reads, in essence: *"My dearest Karis — if you're reading this, know that I'm truly sorry. — Elden."* (p. 30)

> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Confirmed dead or captured pre-1490.* The sealed townhouse and its hazards are still a good DM hook — see the "Abandoned Townhouse" quick adventure (p. 28).

## The Shard of the Moon Cleansing (1479 DR)

### Lady Jasmine & the Selûnite priestesses (p. 15)
In **1479 DR**, Selûne (see [[NPCs/Selune and Shar - Sister Goddesses]]) intervened directly in mortal affairs by sending shards to Toril. **Lady Jasmine** led a group of Selûnite priestesses who — with adventurer assistance — purified the corrupted **Shard of Night** into the **Shard of the Moon**, restoring the floating tower to alabaster white. The priestesses now control access to the tower.

> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Lady Jasmine: possibly aged or deceased — successor priestess likely.* The Selûnite custody of the Shard of the Moon almost certainly persists; the priesthood is institutional, not personal.

## The False-Crown Plot (1479 DR)

### Seldra Tylmarande — *fake-crown pretender* (p. 20)
In **1479 DR**, Seldra Tylmarande was given a **replica of the Crown of Neverwinter**. Disguising herself as a man, she used the fake crown to claim the throne and depose Neverember. The plot was defeated. Neverember himself simultaneously claimed to be a legitimate heir (via Nasher Alagondar's bastard son) but could never produce the real crown to prove it.

> **Status by 1525 DR:** *Defeated in 1479 — fate uncertain.* Either dead, imprisoned, exiled, or running a quieter game somewhere. The **real Crown of Alagondar** never being found is a juicy ongoing thread.
