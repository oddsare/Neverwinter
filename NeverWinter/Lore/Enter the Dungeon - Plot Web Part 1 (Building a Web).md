# Enter the Dungeon: Plot Web Part 1 (Building a Web)

How to tie the players' backstories and the main story into one **plot web**, so the campaign hooks the players with a story that feels natural, **without railroading them**. Summarized from the transcript, 2026-10-25.

**This is how Brodie builds this campaign** (2026-10-25). See also:
- [[Lore/Enter the Dungeon - How I Organize a Campaign]] (the session notes and folders)
- [[Lore/Enter the Dungeon - Plot Web Part 2 (Changing a Web)]] (swapping a PC, changing the villain, using the web in prep)

> [!note] About the example
> The host teaches with three real backstories from three different campaigns he ran, plus a made-up center (Orcus). **The example is his, not this campaign's.** Names are from auto-captions, so spellings may be off.

---

## The steps at a glance
1. **Design the center** (can be done before any backstories arrive).
2. **Break each backstory into pieces** (nodes).
3. **Put every piece on the board** and **connect them**, to each other and to the center.
4. **Draw the web** and tidy it into clusters.
5. **Read the web**: which nodes matter most, how the PCs link.
6. **Use the web to guess the arcs** and the route the party will likely take.

---

## Step 1: Design the center
The center has three parts. He chose all of them **before he even knew which backstories he'd get**.
- **Main villain.** *Example:* **Orcus**, demon lord of undeath. Simple, fits almost any campaign, lots of lore.
- **Central tension.** *Example:* a **cult of Orcus** is trying to summon him and needs **three powerful artifacts** to do it.
- **Theme.** *Example:* **family over power**. Always choose family, and the campaign keeps returning to it: not just the family you're given, but the family you choose.

---

## Step 2: Break each backstory into pieces
Read each backstory line by line and pull out everything you can build with:
- **Proper nouns:** places, people, groups.
- **Facts about people:** who they are, what they do, what happened to them.
- **Events.**
- **What the player says matters to them.** This is the player telling you what's important.

### His worked example: Ace
| Line in the backstory | Piece he pulled out |
|---|---|
| "Ace lived in a village named Lion Rock... in the mountains" | **Lion Rock**: Ace's home village, in the mountains |
| "His father James was the best bounty hunter there is" | **James**: Ace's father, a bounty hunter |
| James taught him to track, find people in crowds, identify and capture them, and read the land | Ace's skills |
| "We hunted almost anything that had a bounty on its head" | They hunted many bounties: "this is going to let us connect to a lot of things later" |
| "The whole village had bounty hunters in it" | **Lion Rock is a bounty-hunter village**: "a really cool piece of worldbuilding the player handed us" |
| "A group of bounty hunters named Red Sun..." | **The Red Sun**: a rival bounty-hunter group |
| They attacked; the village's hunters all fell; James told him to run | **The Red Sun attack**, its own node: they killed the whole village |
| He came back months later: village destroyed, father dead; he buried him | James is **dead** (added to James's node) |
| "Now I'm in search of the Red Sun, to find out why they attacked" | **What the player wants answered.** Build around this. |

### The other two backstories (summarized)
- **Caden Bryce:** an aasimar who has **lost his emotions**. Raised by his single mother **Miriam**; his father, essentially a god (**Onan Grimm**), was never there. His sister **Lillian** resented that their mother gave Caden all the attention, so she made an alliance with a **foreign government**. Its agents ambushed Caden, used a **mysterious tome to steal his emotions**, and kicked him off a cliff. Caden is tracking down his emotions and his sister, and **doesn't know she betrayed him**.
- **Tsutsui ("Cloud"):** a **changeling**. Their mother **Glimmer**, down on her luck, **swapped her own child for a rich young prince** named Tsutsui so her child could have a better life. Since then they've lived as a prince in one of the nation's grand families, the **Usaburo family**, hiding that they're a changeling. They're married to **Megahime**, their best friend, with no attraction between them. They love theater and work with a theater company (**Ozeki Productions**). They're on a **mission for glory**, because once they return home they'll have to settle down and start a family with Megahime, and they don't want to.

## Step 3: Connect the pieces
Put every piece from every backstory, plus the center, "on the board," then link them one by one. **How he does it:** say something, even something dumb, and keep working at it until you find the right answer.

### Filling gaps the players left
- **James has no last name** in the backstory. So: he's **James Bryce**, Miriam's **ex-husband**. He dropped the surname because he felt his ex-wife stole everything: the name, the kids.
- That makes Caden and Ace possible half-brothers... or instead:
- **Ace is the real Tsutsui ("Tsutsui Prime").** Glimmer left the real prince with James, a skilled bounty hunter who was broken after his wife left and took their two-year-old daughter (Lillian) and her unborn child (Caden, fathered by a demigod). Glimmer **memory-wiped** the boy's earliest years. That boy became Ace.
- **Ask the player** before adding to their backstory, or drop hints (James "never went by a last name") and let it surface.

### Tying a backstory to the center
- The Lion Rock bounty hunters took **contracts against demons and monsters**, which means they were working **against Orcus**.
- The **Usaburo family is in league with Orcus.** The bounty hunters slowly realized they were up against this huge family.
- The Usaburo **hired the Red Sun** to cover their tracks. The attack was really about **killing the family's rivals**.
- **James realized at the end of his life** that the Usaburo were working with Orcus and the Abyss, and **left a trail of clues only Ace could find.**
- The player who plays Tsutsui doesn't like the Usaburo family in their backstory, so the host is **free to paint them negatively.** They become a secondary antagonist, along with the Red Sun, Lillian and the foreign government.

### The three artifacts
- **Artifact 1, a weapon:** held by **Onan Grimm**. The party wants to keep it safe; the cult wants it at all costs.
- **Artifact 3, a tome:** the book holding **Caden's emotions**, and some of Onan's power. It's kept **inside the Red Sun's base, in the foreign country**: across the border, in the base of the people who killed Ace's father.
- **Artifact 2, a mask:** worked out from Tsutsui's love of theater.
  - **Megahime's family owns it**: a rare wedding mask (or death mask), a prized heirloom used on special occasions.
  - **The arranged marriage** between Tsutsui and Megahime exists so the **cult can get close to the mask.**
  - So Tsutsui's **mission for glory is delaying the cult's plan.**
  - **Secret plot:** the cult plans to **crash the wedding** (a "Red Wedding"). It can sit under the surface the whole campaign.
- **Miriam has joined Ozeki Productions**: she's there to watch for the mask and get close to Megahime.

### Giving an antagonist nuance: Lillian
- The foreign agents are **the cult**. The cult is **using Lillian to get to Onan.**
- **The ambush** on Caden was really about putting **some of Onan's power into artifact 3**. The cult is after Onan.
- After the betrayal, **Lillian tried to go home**: Lion Rock destroyed, her father dead. She **eventually felt regret** for what she did to Caden.
- **A path to redemption:** she has family in **Caden** (her half-brother) and **Ace** (the son her father wanted). She might be redeemed, or go fully dark.
- The player behind Caden saw Lillian as a **tragic character** (like Catra in *She-Ra*); they agreed on that together.
- **Keep some villains pure** (Orcus, the Usaburo, the Red Sun are stock enemies). **Nuance goes in characters like Lillian.**

### A villain you can't just kill
- The **foreign government is puppeted by agents of Orcus**, and the **main villain is its head of state.**
- You can't just walk into the palace and kill a head of state. If the party did, it would mean **total war between two nations, and the cult probably wins.**
- So an **antagonistic force doesn't have to be a final boss.** The party still has plenty to fight: the Red Sun, demons, undead.

### Rules he follows while connecting
- **Not everything needs to be connected.** Big connections, yes, but not every node.
- **You don't need it all figured out at the start.** Leave something underneath and fill it in over the weeks and months.
- **It can all change**, as long as no event in a session has depended on it yet.
- Look for the side that's **under-connected** (here, Caden and Tsutsui barely talked to each other) and work on it.

## Step 4: Draw the web
- Lay out all the nodes, connect them, then **rearrange them into clusters** so the web is readable.
- He used Photoshop for the video; at home he uses a **notebook**.

---

## Step 5: Read the web
- **The most-connected nodes matter most.** In his example: **James Bryce** (links to Miriam, Caden's story, Glimmer, Tsutsui and Ace) and the **Usaburo family**.
- **Linchpins need extra care.** **Lillian** holds up Caden's whole story: if he handles her badly, Caden's story doesn't work. The same goes for Tsutsui's **mission for glory** (it links to an artifact) and Ace's **Red Sun**.
- **Paths between PCs** show which players will care about each other's stories. Caden reaches Ace through the web; Ace and Tsutsui link directly through "Tsutsui Prime," so Tsutsui will probably get invested in Ace's story.
- **Every PC connects back to the center.** No matter what, every player has a reason to care about the main story.
- **The center can change.** (That's Part 2.)

## Step 6: Guess the arcs and the route
Educated guesses only. They can be wrong. It gets easier the longer you play with the same players.

### His rough arc plan
1. **Low-level undead.** The party comes together, low stakes, general fun. **For a new group, wait before the heavy story**: if the group won't stick, or just wants beer and pretzels, change course. Ends with them **learning about the cult of Orcus** (Orcus is the god of undeath, so the undead incidents lead there).
2. **The cult and the Red Sun.** One link apart on the web, so it's not a stretch (history, documents found). This gets into **Ace's backstory** and his revenge, and probably reveals that **the Usaburo ordered the attack**, which answers the question the player asked at the very start.
3. **The Usaburo family.** Tsutsui's history, Megahime, the wedding, and probably **Miriam** at Ozeki Productions. **Lillian** is likely a main antagonist here, heading toward redemption. *(She can also appear as a low-key recurring background villain in arc 1, to tease the story to come.)* Likely ends with the **cult making its move on the mask** at the wedding, maybe with a choice that hits the theme: save Megahime or Lillian, or keep the mask.
4. **The last artifact, with Onan Grimm.** A finale if the party beats the cult.
5. **The summoning of Orcus** (levels 16+), only if the cult gets all three artifacts. The party stops it, fights a partly summoned Orcus, or dies trying.

### Predicting the route
- Start from the center and see **what it connects to directly.**
- Ask what the party will **realistically reach first** from where they are. Low-level parties fighting undead won't suddenly care about a foreign government. They'll follow Ace's clues to **the Red Sun and James Bryce**, which also leads to artifact 3.
- Then take the **simplest next connection**, one node away: the **Usaburo family**, then Tsutsui's nodes (the mission for glory, Megahime, Ozeki Productions, the mask).
- Whatever's left (most of Caden's story, Onan Grimm) comes later.
- **Many routes, not one.** As players uncover parts of the web, it feels like they're uncovering the world, and the picture gets clearer.
