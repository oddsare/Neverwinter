# Ed Greenwood — The Seven Stars of Selûne

> Source: **Realms Lore** (YouTube), with **Ed Greenwood**. Transcript added 2026-10-19.
> Summarized from the video's auto-captions. **Star names are garbled** and marked *(uncertain)*; check them against the video.

## The holy symbol
- Selûne's holy symbol shows **seven stars around the goddess's eyes**.
- Today they're usually drawn **to look identical**, and arranged in different patterns from temple to temple and tapestry to tapestry.
- Long ago each star was drawn **differently**, in one particular arrangement. Those days are gone, but **the real stars in the sky are the same**.
- These seven are the **holiest lights of the goddess**, with both **navigational and magical** power.

## The seven stars

### 1. Tavon, "the Moon Chariot" *(uncertain)*: the holiest
- **The star Selûne rides.** Her clergy **pray to it to speak to her and to regain their spells**.
- **Seen in the northeastern sky.** A very large, bright star, brilliant pearly white with a wide ring of deep, rich blue.
- On some clear nights it **seems to reflect off the Moon** and casts a **straight road of white light for miles** across Faerûn. The road touches places the goddess wants her faithful to **visit, notice, or find magic she has left** for them.
- **"Walking the Moonlight"** means traveling that glowing road. **Every Selûnite tries to do it at least once in their life.**
- **Healing:** a person who knows and speaks the right charm (a prayer to the goddess) while in its glow can call on it to **purge diseases, poisons, venoms and lycanthropy**.

### 2. Daschant, "the Guiding Star" *(uncertain)*
- **A crimson star ringed with deep purple** that pulses slowly and rhythmically, flaring amber.
- It **sits so low on the northern horizon** that it's usually hidden from Faerûn. It **marks true north**.
- **Sailors off the Sword Coast** invoke Selûne by name to clear clouds, spray and storm wrack so they can see it.
- **A clergy secret:** a **luck blade** stood upright in the altar stone of the outdoor hilltop shrine to Selûne on an island *(name garbled)* at sunset, bathed in Daschant's light while the moon is in the sky, will by the goddess's grace **recharge all of its expended wishes**.

### 3. Clavin, "the Red Lantern" *(uncertain)*
- **A large scarlet star** that Sea of Swords sailors see low in the **southwestern sky**, surrounded by a drift of much smaller white stars. A navigation aid every night.
- Selûne's clerics use it when **casting "blind"**: unleashing a spell in a set direction without looking at the target.
- **The bound red dragon:**
  - Centuries ago Selûne defeated an **ancient red dragon** *(name garbled)*. He had attacked her to try to win enough divine power for immortality.
  - Rather than slay him, she **bound him to the star**, whose energies **heal and invigorate him forever**. He was very grateful.
  - He soon found eternal life boring. He's kept the boredom off by **sponsoring mortal adventurers in Faerûn to work against anyone who threatens dragonkind**. That includes **Szass Tam**, who sees certain dragon princes *(where garbled)* as his next foes.
  - **He and Selûne are working together on spells her clergy can cast to thwart dragons without harming them**, using what he knows of draconic nature.
  - **Be warned:** he will use his adventurers to **destroy anyone who gains such a spell and is not sworn to Selûne**.

### 4. Sormog, "the Ghost Star" *(uncertain)*
- **The shattered shell of a star that exploded in 1216 DR.** It's now a hollow sphere of faintly glowing gas studded with tiny twinkling stars, like a pale, shadowy cloak wrapped around dark nothingness.
- **Necromancy:** necromancers and clergy of other gods learned what Selûne's priests already knew. Magic like theirs worked while Sormog is visible overhead **lasts longer and hits harder**, though the boost rises and falls unpredictably.
- **Undead:**
  - **All mortals can clearly see undead** under its light, even ones that are normally invisible.
  - **Spells that command and control undead**, cast by anyone, are more likely to succeed. They take effect **as if cast 1d4+3 levels higher** than the caster really is.
  - The captions also say all saving throws are made **with advantage** while Sormog is visible to the caster; whose saves isn't clear.

### 5. Iland, "the Were Star" *(uncertain)*
- **An odd-looking star:** almost black in its light, with a vivid **amethyst halo**. It looks very tall and thin, because its vertical rays are four times as long as its others.
- It rides the **equatorial sky**, so depending on mountains, season and time, it's sometimes visible from a given place and sometimes not.
- **Shapeshifting:** when a caster can see it, **all spells that govern shapeshifting** have **twice their usual duration and twice their force**.
  - Selûne's clergy most often use it to **force lycanthropes into a particular shape and hold them there**.
  - Spellcasting shapeshifters can use it on themselves.
  - It even seems to affect body changes made with **magic items**.

### 6. Gef Rasper, "the Seeing Star" *(uncertain)*
- **Bright white with a dark center**, so it looks like a hollow ring or a staring black eye, hence the nickname.
- It hangs **high in the sky due west** for most of Faerûn, so it's valued for **dead-reckoning navigation**.
- **Reveals magic:** under its light, **all magic glows a bright ruby-copper**, contrasting vividly with its surroundings. That includes items, active spells, **ancient and fading wards**, and **the echoes and scars left by mighty magic**.
  - The glow is **precise**, with fine detail and clarity: exact boundaries and **even magical writing can be seen**.

### 7. Marharo, "the War Star" *(uncertain)*
- **A large pinkish-red star high in the southern sky** that trembles as its surface heaves. It constantly throws off sparks that wink out moments later, and it's ringed by leaping rays like short-lived tongues of flame.
- Its **pink light is easy to tell apart** from other light, and wherever it falls, its boons apply:
  - It **automatically and continuously recharges any ring of shooting stars** worn under it. The wearer doesn't need to do anything.
  - **All weapons wielded under its light act as paladins' sacred weapons.** Weapons that already are gain an **additional +4 to attack**.
- **The moonbeam sacrifice:**
  - Any **paladin, or any cleric of Selûne**, can **sacrifice a weapon** under its light. The weapon fades away forever and grants a ***moonbeam*** effect that the sacrificer controls.
  - They move it as an action in the normal way, and it **needs no concentration**.
  - It lasts as long as it stays in the War Star's light and **the Moon is brighter than the Sun** in the sky.
  - If the sacrificer leaves, dies or stops wielding it, the moonbeam **stays where it is at full power** until the moon wanes or sets, or the Sun outshines the Moon.

## Secret rituals
- Senior clergy of Selûne know **sacred secret rituals for each star**, used for **divination** and temple altar rites.
- Rituals that use **several priests and more than one star at once** can **boost standard clerical spells to truly mighty levels**. Only high-ranking priests of the moon goddess learn them.
- **The most often seen:** when a **temple of Selûne is attacked**, multiple ***flame strikes* rain down repeatedly around it like a defensive wall**.

## How this fits the vault (Brodie decides)
- **Neverwinter's Selûnite site:** the **Shard of the Moon** (taken from Shar in 1479) is the obvious place for star rituals and the flame-strike temple defense. See [[NPCs/Selune and Shar - Sister Goddesses]].
- **Sailors and navigation:** Selûne is a goddess of sailors and navigators, and the [[Quests/The Shadowy Force|Shadowy Force]] involves ships vanishing on moonless nights.
- **Sormog and undead:** relevant to Thay and the [[Locations/Neverwinter/Districts/Neverdeath District|Neverdeath Graveyard]].
- **Gef Rasper and wards:** it reveals old wards and the scars of great magic. That's relevant to [[Locations/Neverwinter/The Neverneath|the Neverneath's ward]], [[Lore/The Lost Crown and Hidden Alagondar Vaults|the Alagondar vaults]] and the [[Locations/Neverwinter/Districts/Chasm District|Chasm's seal]].
- **The bound red dragon** sponsors adventurers against threats to dragonkind. Whether he matters to the campaign's dragons is open.
- **No clashes** with the vault.
