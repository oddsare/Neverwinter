# Neverwinter Sub-Locations (Interior Detail)

> [!warning] Supplemental Material
> Lifted and paraphrased from a fan-made guide describing Neverwinter as it stood in the **early 1490s DR** — roughly **30+ years before the campaign's 1525 DR start.** Treat as historical backdrop / DM resource. Many buildings will have changed hands, fallen further into ruin, or been rebuilt by 1525. Anything the guide presents as "current" is historical here.

> [!info] Campaign Year Note
> - Dagult Neverember is **missing since 1502 DR** in vault canon. Any "Neverember-controlled" buildings below have, by 1525, passed to other hands, fallen into limbo, or been claimed during the Dance of the Revolving Lords (1502–1520).
> - [[NPCs/Renaer Neverember]] ascended in 1520 DR and now sits over much of what his father built.
> - Use the descriptions below for **architecture, layout, and atmospheric detail** — adjust ownership and condition to taste.

## Castle Never (Interior)

A millennium-old structure perched on cliffs at the western end of the city, where the Neverwinter River meets the Sea of Swords. Three bridges radiate from it: the **Sleeping Dragon**, **Winged Wyvern**, and **Dolphin** bridges (p. 5). After the 1451 cataclysm it was largely ruined and is haunted by the spirits of those killed; the entire structure remains dangerous to enter (pp. 5–6).

### Notable Interior Areas (p. 6)
- **The Atrium.** Boarded over to keep sunlight out and rot-heat in. Eerie display of exotic bird skeletons in gilded cages. Now infested by territorial **myconids** that feast on intruders.
- **The Hall of Mirrors.** Once used by nobility to practice posture and gait. Any mortal who lingers triggers a magical re-enactment of the hall's destruction — flying shards of mirror-glass and fiery debris are flung at trespassers.
- **The Neverneath** (also known as **"the Endless Maze"**). The magically protected catacombs beneath Castle Never.
  - **Original purpose:** built to (1) **maintain the structural integrity of the castle itself** and (2) **protect the buried corpse of Lord Halueth Never** (the sun elf founder of Eigersstor / Neverwinter, c. -10 DR).
  - **What changed:** after the **Spellplague of 1385 DR**, wild magic altered the protective ward, granting it **malevolent sentience** and the ability to **reshape the corridors at will**.
  - **Behavior:** the ward **bides its time** — visitors may proceed apparently undisturbed at first — then traps them inside an **infinite magical corridor loop** full of **deadly traps** and **animated statues** that attack anything that moves.
  - **Halueth Never's corpse is still buried down there**, undisturbed, protected by the ward it predated.
  - **Campaign load-bearing:** if the ward is destroyed, the castle's structural integrity is compromised — the maze isn't just defense, it's also a foundation. The ward and the corpse it protects are tied together. Disturbing one risks both.
  - *Earlier framing (Bob World Builder transcript): "corrupted spirit of Halueth Never himself" — treat as a mistake. The antagonist is the **ward**, not Halueth's ghost.*
- **The Vault of the Nine.** Tomb of the nine bodyguards of Neverwinter's royal lineage. An outer chamber lit by everburning torches (with undead lurking in the shadows) gates an inner chamber sealed behind a great stone door bearing Neverwinter's sigil. The inner chamber holds nine marble coffins around a central throne. Visitors who long-rest within may wake to find the spirits of the Nine standing vigil over them.
- **The Holes.** Restored dungeons beneath the castle, used by Lord Neverember as Neverwinter's prison. **Executions were held daily at noon**, typically two days after a prisoner's arrival (p. 6).

> [!note] Status by 1525 DR
> With Neverember gone since 1502, "The Holes" likely fell out of regular use as an execution site during the Revolving Lords years. [[NPCs/Renaer Neverember]] may or may not have resumed restoration work. The supernatural hazards (myconids, Neverneath, Hall of Mirrors, Vault of the Nine) almost certainly persist — none are tied to mortal politics.

## Hall of Justice (Tyr Temple) (p. 7)

Primary temple to Tyr in Neverwinter. Grand stone walls, towering pillars, high-domed roof — interior large enough to accommodate giants and dragons. Historically served as:
- Temple to Tyr.
- City court for **low justice** (cases not involving nobles, non-citizens, or murder).
- Drill and training ground for devout temple-guards.

The Hall's functions lapsed when Tyr "died" in the late 1300s DR. Locals refused to rededicate it to another deity. Neverember briefly installed a Torm priesthood and used the building as his personal residence and operational base — both moves unpopular with citizens. Following Tyr's resurrection, the temple was rededicated and Neverember moved to a private villa; his restoration of the Hall greatly improved his standing.

## House of Knowledge (Oghma Temple/Library) (p. 7)

Both library and primary temple to Oghma in the city. Architecturally one of the finest buildings in Neverwinter — abundant windows, an arched roof, vast libraries containing centuries of records and (because Neverwinter lacks a formal seat of government) much current government documentation.

Devastated by Mount Hotenow in 1451 DR. **Loremaster Atlavast** — a dedicated young priest — saved much of the collection by sealing himself inside the inner sanctum during the cataclysm. He survived in isolation, but grew reclusive and a little mad, obsessively re-cataloguing the collection. The outer sections were taken over by squatters including **Ashmadai cultists**.

After the Chasm's sealing (1486 DR), the faithful of Oghma returned. By the 1490s the exterior was largely restored; interior reconstruction continued under the iron-fisted **Grand Scribe Spivey Liethennson**. Atlavast remained extremely protective of the collection.

> [!note] Status by 1525 DR
> Spivey would now be 30+ years older; Atlavast (already aged and unstable in the 1490s) is likely deceased or in deeper decline. A successor Grand Scribe is plausible. The library itself should still exist — likely with new gaps and new acquisitions reflecting the chaotic Revolving Lords era.

## The Beached Leviathan (Docks) (pp. 11–13)

A grounded pirate vessel — the former flagship of Captain Harrag — that ran aground in northern Neverwinter after Mount Hotenow. Rather than abandon the wreck, Harrag converted it into an inn and tavern as the rebuilt docks rose around it.

**Three levels for guests, plus the captain's quarters:**
- **The Hold** (ground floor, dock-entry). Kitchen and tavern. Tables sized for parties large and small.
- **The Quarters** (second floor). Inn with 12 rooms of varying size.
- **The Deck** (third floor). Private parlour rooms and tables, rentable at a premium.
- **Captain's Cabin.** A small upper cabin atop the deck — Harrag's private quarters.

All levels are connected by a central spiral staircase. A pulley-operated cargo lift runs through a shaft spanning every floor. The interior is finished in oak and cherry, decorated with nautical trophies from Harrag's piratical past, but worn from years of patrons (p. 11).

Clientele runs the gamut: sailors, dockworkers, smugglers, thieves, **press gangs**. Brawling is common (and tends to get a patron tossed). Nautical slang dominates speech ("ballast" for a drunk, "privateer" for a newcomer). Neverember's people quietly monitor the place as a window into the city's underworld and rarely intervene (p. 11).

> See [[Lore/Pre-1502 Supporting Cast]] for [[NPCs/Captain Harrag|Harrag]] and the full crew — they were the regulars circa 1490s. Status by 1525 is anyone's guess.

## The Driftwood Tavern (Docks) (p. 13)

An upscale, expensive tavern in the northern docks — closer to a museum than a watering hole. Operating since **before the Spellplague (1385 DR)**, it served as a refugee shelter and **makeshift city hall** after the 1451 cataclysm. Crammed with relics and bric-a-brac, viewed by many as a living tribute to "Old Neverwinter." Drinks and lodging are expensive but the historical atmosphere draws travellers.

Run in the 1490s by **Madame Rosene** (then ~70). Secretly she had been leader of the **Sons of Alagondar / Graycloaks** during the 1479 events. By the 1490s the faction had largely made peace with Neverember; many former Graycloaks enlisted in the city army, preferring local soldiers to Mintarn mercenaries.

> [!note] Status by 1525 DR
> Madame Rosene would be ~100 years old — almost certainly deceased. The tavern likely persists under a successor; the relic collection alone makes it a Neverwinter institution.

## Tarmalune Trade House (Docks) (p. 13)

A trade cartel from the distant city of **Tarmalune** holding a large warehouse complex on Neverwinter's southern docks. Outside the warehouses, a swath of stalls sells goods. The Tarmalune merchants are trying to establish a solid local presence to counter rival traders from **Lylorn**, who have set up in nearby [[Locations/Luskan]].

Useful for hard-to-acquire goods (broad trade network), but expect high prices and long lead times.

## Shard of the Moon (Tower District) (pp. 14–15)

A floating tower in the Tower District — once **Moonshadow Hall**, a temple in the distant city of Yhaunn. The Shadovar (Netherese of Shadowfell) tore one of its towers free in 1379 DR and learned they could manifest it anywhere in the mortal realm. During the Spellplague (1385) they positioned it floating above Neverwinter, using an internal portal to shuttle between **Xinlenal** and the city. Renamed the **Shard of Night**, it appeared black and cast no shadow by day — magic absorbed shadow to fuel its teleportation. Anyone who entered did not return.

In 1479 DR, adventurers raided the tower and allowed priestesses of [[NPCs/Selune and Shar - Sister Goddesses|Selûne]] — led by **Lady Jasmine** — to purify it back to alabaster white. At night, when three magical shards are placed within or below the tower, **a stairway of solid moonlight** descends from its entrance. The Selûnite priestesses admit only trusted visitors; a long rest inside grants an additional healing surge until the next long rest (p. 15).

## The Cloak Tower (Tower District) (p. 15)

A wizard tower, citadel of the **Order of the Many-Starred Cloak**.

**Vanishing Fortifications (1385 DR).** The tower and all its wizards disappeared during the Spellplague. It returned about six months later — in a new location, with no trace of the wizards. The cloaks themselves still hung on their pegs. Thieves who looted the tower were **smothered to death by the animated cloaks** — only one survivor remained to tell the tale.

**Halavar's Universal Pantograph.** A rumored magical device, recovered from a Netherese ruin, supposedly held inside the tower. It can allegedly duplicate any metal object up to roughly throne-sized — including **coins**. Thieves never found it.

**Orc Occupation.** The **Many-Arrows** tribe seized the tower and, against all expectations, held it without resistance. Neverember tolerated this because the tower was one of the most fortified positions in the city. The orcs eventually left of their own accord to assist their kin against the dwarves. The Many-Starred Cloak presumably reclaimed custody by the late 1480s, with senior members working with Neverember.

> [!note] Status by 1525 DR
> The Many-Starred Cloak's standing in the post-Neverember city is unclear — depends on which faction held Neverwinter during the Revolving Lords. The Pantograph rumor is too juicy to retire; treat as a hook still in play.

## The Fallen Tower (Tower District) (pp. 16–17)

A tavern built into the ruined base of a wizard's tower, famous for a **nightly phantasmic re-enactment** of its destruction.

**The History.** Decades before the Spellplague, the tower belonged to the wizard **Llomnauvel "Firehands" Oloadhin**. The [[Factions/Arcane Brotherhood]] coveted his collection and stormed the tower. Oloadhin had rigged it with traps — including spells that transformed intruders into lowly sea creatures — but they were ultimately overcome. Rather than yield, the unhinged Oloadhin triggered a single massive destruction-spell that destroyed the tower with everyone (himself included) inside.

**The Nightly Show.** Each night, at the exact moment of the explosion, silent phantasmic projections re-enact the final moments — Brotherhood mages on fire falling to their deaths, the Brotherhood's Overwizard falling with his limbs transforming into eels. Entrepreneurs turned the ruin into a tavern, railing off the spectacle and ringing it with tables. The novelty kept it popular for over a century before mediocre overpriced drinks and Neverwinter's accumulated catastrophes drove it to abandonment.

**Under New Management.** When the Many-Arrows occupied the Tower District, an enterprising one-eared orc — **Vagdru One-Ear** — restored the tavern. Under a truce between Neverember and the Many-Arrows leadership, the Fallen Tower now operates as **neutral ground** between orcs and other races. Anyone is welcome who starts no trouble; troublemakers are dealt with by the regulars on the spot. The food is humble — cheap ale, raw meat of unspecified origin cooked over open fire.

## House of a Thousand Faces (Bluelake) (p. 18)

A comfortable tavern in [[Locations/Neverwinter/Districts/Bluelake District|Bluelake]]. The building was once a boutique clothiers' shop catering to local nobility, and remains full of **mirrors and mannequins** displaying antiquated fashions — the reflections give the building its name. Lush, comfortable couches; happy chatter.

**The basement** is the secret meeting place of the Neverwinter chapter of [[Factions/The Harpers]]. The local Harpers' reputation took serious damage in the 1470s when a high-ranking member was caught playing double-agent between Neverember and the Sons of Alagondar.

## Pirates' Skyhold (Floating Earthmote, NW of city) (p. 10)

A floating earthmote off the Sword Coast northwest of Neverwinter. Soon after the Spellplague, a band of pirates with a skyship claimed it and built modest wooden fortifications. **A mysterious tragedy then killed or disappeared all of them.** The skyship still sits docked at the earthmote — abandoned.

Rumors swirl: that a dragon took it; that **Netherese** occupy it now; that ghosts haunt it; that the pirates' hoard is still up there waiting. Reaching the Skyhold requires aerial or magical transport.

> The 4e source explicitly left this open for DMs to define — it's a sandbox hook. Suggested seeds: a young dragon's lair; an undead infestation (Myrkul-touched); a dracolich or ghost dragon presiding over zombie pirates.

## Fisher's Float (Floating Earthmote, SW of city) (p. 10)

An earthmote just southwest of Neverwinter. A close-knit guild of fishermen lives in austere wooden shanties and warehouses on the mote, supplying a major share of the city's seafood. They keep to themselves — and rumors persist that they hold some manner of **allegiance to evil sea gods or sea monsters**.
