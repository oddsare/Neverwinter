# Secrets of Baldur's Gate (Ed Greenwood)

Canon reference, summarized in my own words. Names come from auto-captions; spellings may be off. **Campaign connections are open threads.** See also [[Locations/Baldur's Gate]].

## The city
- **The largest trading city on the Sword Coast** between Waterdeep and Amn. A major port where the **River Chionthar** meets the sea and the **Coast Way** caravan road crosses it.
- Started as a fishing village; named for the explorer **Balduran**, whose home port it was. Protected for ages by the **Flaming Fist**, one of the biggest mercenary companies; its members all live there, a standing deterrent.
- **Lords' Alliance** member; economically and so politically a major player.
- **Layout:** a **bowl-shaped amphitheater** on the river's north bank, crammed with narrow, steep streets (some with steps). Up top on the tableland, **warehouses** keep spreading and pushing livestock paddocks farther out. The rich live up on **the Arm**.
- **Mildew everywhere:** where the warmer river meets the colder sea, **sea fogs** form and damp air sits in the Bowl. The lower city smells of mildew and dead fish; up top it's dung and sea air. The wealthy hose their cobbles and use spells and perfume.
- **How locals see it:** among the best places to live, free of tyrant kings, rural poverty and monster-roamed wilds. Expensive and dangerous, but hard work and luck pay. Grumbles: too damp, and "strange folk" flocking in with odd gods and lawlessness.
- **How outsiders see it:** too big, chaotic, a cesspool of cutthroat merchants where coin is king, **lacking Waterdeep's strong guilds and traditions**. Just bribes and corruption.
- **Diversity:** very diverse, like all big ports (Waterdeep, Calimport, Neverwinter), but the steep slopes and short sight lines mean you rarely notice.
- **What it makes:** mostly trade. The busiest crafts are **casks, kegs, crates and custom coffers** for repacking goods into mule-sized lots, and **portable food you can eat while walking**. Brewing and distilling come from elsewhere. Value-adding crafts thrive: **dyeing textiles, cutting gems, casting new alloys**, often in sweatshops in the new suburbs. **Sages are scarce**; they can only make a living if they know a trade (dyes, gems, metallurgy).
- **The mood:** everyone feels this is where it's all happening; the latest news is on every lip, and every second citizen thinks they're an expert.

## Politics: a "golden age"
- The Watch and officials were long **bribable and corrupt**, but not lately.
- **1476 DR:** the **Council of Four** (Grand Dukes) was restored, aided by a **54-member Parliament of Peers** that elects the dukes, debates everything, and drafts laws for the council to pass or send back.
- **Five deputies** run the city for the council: **Harbor Master**, **High Constable and Master of Walls**, **Master of Drains and Underways**, **Master of Cobbles**, and **Purse Master**.
- **The Bailiff of the Wide** (under the Purse Master) schedules and records every merchant and vendor; the official merchants see most.
  - In the 1470s, **Jedren Hiller** held it and became openly corrupt.
  - **1491 DR:** replaced by **Morathra Dworn**, a gravel-voiced, surly, so-far **incorruptible priestess of Helm**, devoted to fairness and honesty. She prays while bathing and while eating, and spends every other waking hour **patrolling the streets in person**, popping up at merchants' elbows.
  - Grumbling at first; now traders **trust** her. A level playing field, less smuggling and fewer muggings, and redress through her, so **trade is booming**.
- **Explorers:** Balduran (sailed the Sea of Swords, mapped islands) and **Dabron Sashenstar** (overland). Ed's view: the Gate is isolated, at a river mouth, a seaport and on a major road, so making money means travel; their success made everyone else follow.

## The dark secret: Trezorr
- For years the **Iron Throne**, the Merchants' League, the **Zhentarim** and **priests of Bhaal** (gathering Bhaalspawn) fought over the city's iron and arms trade. Few noticed why the Zhents never took over after the Iron Throne collapsed and the Merchants' League was weakened, even with the opening left by **Grand Duke Valarken's** failed coup (backed by lycanthropes).
- **The Zhents noticed:** they were **foiled by dragons**, and by humans, dragonborn and **kenku** working for those dragons.
- **Why would dragons care?** Local legends of **Balduran's buried treasure** under the city have grown wild (titans in stasis, chimeras fed condemned criminals, golem armies, a sleeping tarrasque). **Dragons first told those tales**, and they were about **Trezorr**, not treasure.
- **Trezorr** is a **primordial**, an **ooze archomental** that can turn rock into sludge it can see and swim through, letting it harden behind it. Awake under the **Arm and the Bowl**, it would cause **collapses, toppled buildings and huge loss of life**.
- **Six dragons bound it** long ago, during the dragons' rebellion against the primordials, deep under where the **Arm meets the Bowl** on the north bank. Their spells stripped its rock-melting power and dulled its mind (like *feeblemind*), leaving it merged with the rock in stasis.
- When bound, it was seeking magical masses of rock to **drain their magic**. Freed, it would likely resume, heading for the nearest such nodes: under **Cormyr near High Horn** and under **Evereska**.
- **Through the 1400s DR it has been stirring**, dreaming of the surface and **sending urges into the dreams** of dragons, wyverns and drakes.

## Dragons watching the Gate
- **The Braer Brood:** a family of **spellcasting green dragons** in the **Forest of Wyrms**: matriarch **Braeralalaghra** *(spelling uncertain)*, consort **Truindragarr**, and six children under strict discipline. Long invested in Baldur's Gate, watching it through hired and coerced **kenku and human spies**. Uninterested in fighting adventurers, they built a **false lair** in ruined caverns at the forest edge, stocked with hungry monsters kept penned by **Tavarra's Talons**, a veteran band of evil hired adventurers. **Trezorr's dreams tell them** rich treasure lies deep under the Gate, reachable by tunneling west and down from the deepest mines under **Duskhawk Hill**.
- **A copper dragon** *(Haervaerithavnavalar, spelling uncertain)* has lived **in human form for decades** as the moneylender and investor **Handor Vraunth**, watching trade and politics and **feeding information to the Harpers** (who don't know he's a dragon) about Zhentarim plots, Red Wizard spying, and attempts to revive the Iron Throne's covert arms role.
- **A black dragon** *(Mamaraukhlaggar, spelling uncertain)* who recently killed other wyrms and took their lairs in the **Troll Mountains** wants to **ruin Baldur's Gate, kill most of its people, withdraw, let it rebuild, and do it again**. But he wants it **bigger and richer first**, and is having great fun **exploring the city in human form** with newly mastered long-lasting shapeshifting. A willful child with a new toy, in no hurry.
- Temples and shrines have had **altar visions of dragons**, and talk has spread, so **Cult of the Dragon** agents are now nosing around, looking for hidden dragons to recruit.

## The Lurking Lords (the traitor duke)
- After his failed coup, **Grand Duke Valarken** and the **Band of the Red Moon** (lycanthropes) escaped. Valarken, after years of lycanthrope lovers, is now **a lycanthrope himself**.
- Much older now, they've given up on open rule. They want **power, wealth, good food and drink, and luxury**, through **control of wealthy merchant families**.
- **Targets:**
  - **House Draevren:** holdings in Amn; livestock breeding, metal mining and smelting, weapons making and selling.
  - **House Terethmaster:** the Gate's sword- and arrow-makers; invests in shipyards and ropemakers.
  - **House Baraelen:** hairy, hot-tempered, brawling men (called "toads" by foes); landlords to many, plus glassmaking, metal casting, alloy smithing and bulk seed sales.
- Calling themselves **the Lurking Lords**, under false names and in human form, they **invest in these families' ventures, make friends, and drift into positions as trusted advisors**. **Their help genuinely works**, fixing mistakes and extending reach, so the families embrace them more and more. **Each family thinks it's the only one** getting such help.
- A patient long game, with nothing public or violent. One family head, **Alavrond Terethmaster**, is beginning to dream of becoming a **Grand Duke**, and with the hidden backing, he might.

## Sarphoon's Sea Spell
- **Sarphoon:** a pompous, short, fat, middle-aged mage with a huge honey-blond beard and a high opinion of himself, newly rich and famous thanks to one spell.
- **The spell:**
  - Takes **a day** to cast and leaves him **exhausted**.
  - Needs many cheap but **oddly specific components** (certain birds' feathers, sea-smoothed pebbles of particular kinds, leaves and roots of particular wild plants, certain seabirds' blood) that take time to gather and are **used up** as catalysts.
  - **Consumes seawater and seaweed** to turn **one fish into three** of the same kind, equally edible. **The two new fish are compelled to rush ashore** and beach themselves.
- **The operation:** gangs of street kids with **slings** kill the swooping gulls (which are sold as fertilizer), so masses of fish are gathered **without ships, nets or skilled fishers**.
- **Consequences:** **some fishers want him dead**; he's dealing with at least two rival makers of **smoked fish kegged in oil**, and **at least five whole caravans** have gone east to feed landlocked cities in Amn and Tethyr.
- Others are coming to learn the spell, by persuasion or **kidnapping**, so he's **gone into hiding**.
- **Someone, perhaps a dragon's agent** looking for a way down to the "treasure," is helping him hide by spreading **a dozen false Sarphoons** and **setting traps for the Sarphoon-hunters**, so they can **capture other wizards** and force them to work magic for them.
- **The Council of Four and the Parliament** are debating whether to **hire adventurers** to sort it out.
- Ed's view on components: since they act as catalysts, a skilled mage could probably replace them with an **arcane focus**. Sarphoon isn't good enough, and is too busy making money, to experiment. Ed released a free **5e write-up of the spell** on his Patreon.

## RealmsSpeak
- **Prestidigitation** isn't a Realms word: it's a real-world term for sleight of hand that came into D&D as a word for minor magic.
- Aside at the end: the folk of **the Arm** are as rich and exclusive as ever, but **can't dominate** the many newcomers in the outlying districts the way they dominated the Bowl.

## Campaign connections (open threads)
- **Trezorr mirrors Maegera:** a primordial bound under a city, catastrophic if it wakes. Both fit Alaundo's **"bound one"** prophecy ([[Lore/Ed Greenwood - Alaundo's Prophecies]]). A bound primordial **sending dreams to dragons** is a pattern Maegera could follow.
- **Dragons in human form:** the copper moneylender and the black dragon, like **Gnawbone** and **Zundaerazylym**. The copper dragon quietly feeding the **Harpers** echoes Zundaerazylym and the Harpers ([[Factions/The Harpers]]).
- **A dragon who wants a city richer before destroying it:** Neverwinter's Dawnfire boom makes it exactly that kind of target.
- **The Lurking Lords** are a model for your **four noble houses**: outsiders becoming trusted advisors and steering several houses from inside.
- **A Helm priestess cleaning up corruption** is a counterpoint to Renaer's pushback in Neverwinter.
- **Sarphoon** is a model for a **Dawnfire crafter** with a secret process who goes into hiding, with fakes used as bait.
- **Cult of the Dragon** is active here too, as in the Mere ([[NPCs/Voaraghamanthar - Two Black Brothers]]).
