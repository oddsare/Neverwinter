# World-Building Lessons

A world-builder's nine biggest lessons from ten years on one passion project, from a YouTube video. Summarized in my own words; transcript added 2026-10-24. This is writing advice, not Realms canon. The sponsor segment (World Anvil) is left out. It looks like the same channel as [[Lore/Nine Steps Before Session One]], going by the code and sign-off.

## The nine lessons
1. **Practice in small doses.** Some of the host's best ideas came while watching a film or show, when a scene raised questions about their own world or showed them a different take. Even 15 minutes of notes at a time adds up to big changes.
2. **Every idea can become a "nugget."** Write everything down, even ideas that sound dumb at first, because something in them will spark later. A screenwriting teacher's rule: if you had the idea, there's something good in it, and you just have to refine it. Going back through old notes turns up useful things. **Your brain isn't a wiki:** you will forget the god's name, the date the empire fell and the mountain range that started a war.
3. **Make it fun.** "Have fun" isn't actionable advice, so *make* it fun. Gamify it and reward yourself so you keep coming back. Do it consistently for a few weeks and it becomes routine, and then you don't need the game anymore.
4. **Write what you know.** Trying to detail things you don't understand (a spaceship engine when you can't change your own oil) leads to burnout. You can still have spaceships. Just focus on the small stories rather than trying to sound like an expert.
5. **Be willing to kill off characters and ideas.** Don't get married to any one idea. The host was obsessed with one character and their backstory. Swapping in a completely different character forced a rewrite from the ground up. Building around the new character, instead of forcing the old one in, broke through years of being stuck. If you're blocked, try the story from a different angle, character or idea.
6. **Being different just to be different is bad writing.** Making dragons pink because you're tired of normal colors isn't creative. Making them pink because their diet is full of carotenoids, the pigment that turns flamingos pink, is. Something unique should have a reason.
7. **Borrowing is fine.** Every professional writer admits to inspiration from somewhere. The catch is to make it yours, fitted to your own voice and world.
8. **Real history has some of the best stories.** Read history and soak it up. Anything older than about a century feels strange to us, which makes it rich material. The host's example: a documentary about young children once serving in the British Army, which they plan to use for its human element and conflict.
9. **Human stories make a world stand out.** For the host, building from the bottom up works better than starting top-down. However amazing or unique an idea is, it fails if the reader can't relate to it. People find themselves, and their place in the universe, in those stories.

The host's closing point: these worked for their style, so find your own method.
