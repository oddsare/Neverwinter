# The 3 Faction Rule

> Source: **Mystic Arts** (YouTube), host **Di**. Transcript captured 2026-05-17.
> Video premise: every political situation can be presented with three factions — **good, bad, and ugly** — and complexity comes from nesting, layering, fracturing, and twisting them.

## The Rule in Brief

Three factions: **Good**, **Bad**, and **Ugly**. The **Ugly** faction is the **tipping point** — the neutral third side whose alignment decides the conflict. The **Good** side starts on the **back foot** so the party has a reason to exist. Complexity is built afterward by nesting more good/bad/ugly trios inside each faction (and above them).

## The Six-Step Build

1. **Conflict** — keep it simple. ("Hobgoblins are attacking civilization.") No originality required; a monster manual entry is enough.
2. **Factions** — three of them. The third is neutral/tipping. Give them evocative names.
3. **Characters** — players interact with NPCs, not factions. Cast each faction with a face. The **Good** faction should have *incompetent or absent* leadership so the party matters; if the Good side already has epic heroes, the PCs are extras.
4. **Ideology** — what each faction believes. Broad strokes are fine — no need for real-world political theory.
5. **Methods** — how each faction acts on its ideology (deliberation/treaties vs. war/autocracy vs. ritual/summoning, etc.).
6. **Twist** — recurse the rule. Each faction has three sub-factions: a good, a bad, and an ugly. The Duke's three children. The army's three commanders. The advisors' three camps. Keep nesting until the players feel lost in the political forest.

## Why the Good Side Must Be Losing

> "If the factions are even there is no adventure. If you start the adventure with two major sides evenly matched, you're sort of communicating that the adventure hasn't started yet."

The Good faction needs the players because its leadership is asleep, weak, or divided. That's why the players matter.

## Why the Ugly Faction Is the Whole Engine

The Ugly faction is the **swing vote**. Whichever side wins them, wins the war.

- The **Good** faction is usually *too uncomfortable* to court the Ugly faction (they're weird, scary, principled, or amoral).
- The **Bad** faction has no such hesitation and will ally with anyone.
- The party's job is often to broker the deal the Good faction can't bring itself to make — or to stop the Bad faction from making it first.

## Nesting / Fractals

- Every faction can contain three sub-factions (good/bad/ugly).
- Sub-factions can contain further sub-sub-factions.
- Above the three factions can sit **uber-factions** — bigger international powers the three local factions are subordinates of.
- Players' actions can **fracture** a faction (split it into its sub-factions), **remove** a faction (creating a power vacuum a new one fills), or **grow/shrink** factions over time.

## Grimdark Variants

Players need hope. Don't strip every faction of redeeming sub-elements at once.

- **All three factions bad** — each has a good sub-faction inside that could replace its leadership. That's the hope.
- **All three ugly** — with good and bad sub-factions inside each.
- **All three good** — with individual bad actors making them look bad from the outside.

## Worked Example from the Video

| Faction | Alignment | Leader | Ideology | Methods |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Duchy of Riverfell** | Good | Duke Ronan Brine (80s, ineffective) | Laws, trade, peace | Deliberation, treaties, slow public policy |
| **The Pale Fist** (hobgoblins) | Bad | General Varzuk Khal | Might makes right; conquest is virtuous | War, autocracy, direct violence |
| **The Eldritch Order** (wizards) | Ugly | Mouthless (sewn-shut wizard) | Wisdom of the unspeaking god; the grave fog will end all things | Rituals, summoning, bargains with higher powers |

**Tipping mechanic:** the Pale Fist will win unless Riverfell allies with the Eldritch Order — but Riverfell finds the Order too weird to negotiate with. The Pale Fist has no such hesitation.

**Nested example inside Riverfell:**

- The Duke's children — a good/bad/ugly trio (Duke = ugly, son/heir = bad, daughter = good).
- The army has three commanders mapped the same way.
- The advisors have three camps mapped the same way.

Recursion all the way down.

## Application to *This* Campaign (Neverwinter, 1525 DR)

The vault already has three named factions in `Factions/`. They map cleanly:

| Faction | Likely alignment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **City Guard** | **Good** | Defending the city, on the back foot per the Shadowy Force quest, leadership stretched thin |
| **Arcane Brotherhood** | **Bad** | Luskan-based schemers, openly hostile, will deal with anyone |
| **The Harpers** | **Ugly** | Secretive do-gooders, principled but won't act openly; the swing vote |

**The tipping logic for the Shadowy Force investigation:** the Guard can't crack the case alone. The Brotherhood will protect itself and is probably involved (or being framed). The Harpers know more than they're saying — and the party will need them, but the Guard is too proud / too suspicious to court them directly.

### Suggested Nesting for Tier 1

- **Inside the City Guard:** Captain Soren Ironwake (good — genuinely wants to solve it), a politically-motivated captain who'd rather bury the case for stability (bad), Lord Renaer Neverember pressuring for cosmetic results to protect trade (ugly).
- **Inside the Arcane Brotherhood:** a faction quietly aiding the Chosen of Kurtulmak (bad), a faction watching the Shadowy Force to learn from it (ugly), a defector who wants to leak intel to the Harpers (good).
- **Inside the Harpers:** a faction that wants to expose Old Gnawbone now (bad — too soon, tier-mismatched), a faction that wants to play the long game (ugly), a Cymril-loyalist faction trying to rebuild trust with the Sons of Alagondar (good).

### Uber-factions Above the Three

- **Claugiyliamatar** and her son's Cult of the Dragon (tier-3 capstone).
- **The Two Black Brothers** in the Mere — ugly outside faction with their own agenda.
- **The Sharran cult** — hidden bad uber-faction with fingers in multiple subs.
- **Kurtulmak's Chosen** — an entirely parallel three-faction system underground that runs on its own clock.

## DM Notes

- **Don't tell players this is the format.** The host explicitly says his players have never noticed in ten years.
- **The Ugly faction is the entire game.** Without it, the party has nothing to negotiate. With it, every session has stakes.
- **Recurse only as deep as you can keep track of.** Three layers (faction → sub-faction → sub-sub) is usually enough.
- **Romance and complex NPC relationships** are extra twists the host calls out at the end — use them sparingly inside the existing structure.
- **Players removing or fracturing a faction** is a major campaign event. Plan for the power vacuum, and have the next faction ready to fill it.
