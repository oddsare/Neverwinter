# Ed Greenwood — The Masked Lords of Waterdeep

> Source: **Realms Lore** (YouTube), Ed Greenwood with host Ivan, a **two-part series**. Part one covers the rules; part two covers who the Lords really are, Mirt, and how to use them. Transcripts added 2026-10-19.
> Summarized in my own words. Ed's "present" is the **late 1490s DR**; the campaign is in 1505.
> Short version in [[Locations/Waterdeep]]; the Open Lord herself is in [[NPCs/Laeral Silverhand]].

## Part one: how the Masked Lords work

### Who they are
- Also called the **Hidden Lords of Waterdeep**.
- **Numbers vary** over time, including after disasters like the deaths in Ed's novel *Death Masks*. The aim is to keep the roster **above 16, and usually above 40**.
- **Ahghairon's intent**, from the start, was that they'd **represent Waterdhavians from all walks of life** (all genders, ages and races too), for wider experience and better government.
  - They were meant to be **dominated by neither the nobility nor the guilds**, and to **counterbalance both**.

### The Open Lord
- **The one public Lord.** The whole city, and many envoys, rulers and visitors, know who it is.
- **Duties:**
  - speaks for the city;
  - receives envoys, and delegations from citizens and guilds;
  - **"leads"** almost all formal meetings of the Masked Lords. (Waterdhavians never say "chairs.")
- **Lives in the Palace of Waterdeep**, still called "Piergeiron's Palace" after the long-serving, popular Open Lord Piergeiron the Paladinson, and usually works from offices there. It's also where the Lords traditionally meet with the Open Lord and each other.
  - *(Part two adds that the current Open Lord lives in Mirt's mansion instead; see below.)*
- **The Masked Lords can dismiss the Open Lord by majority vote.** Under the current system they first did so formally **in 1489 DR, ousting Dagult Neverember**.

### What they do, and may not do
- **They debate civic policy and pass decrees** (laws) **by majority vote**.
- **They wear masks** so no one can threaten, blackmail, bribe or otherwise pressure them.
- **Who knows their identities:**
  - **The Open Lord knows** all of them.
  - A Lord may tell anyone, but **customarily doesn't**, except close friends who are fellow Lords.
- **They judge only fellow Lords**, no one else.
- **By law they may not directly command civic workers or officials, or run city departments or projects.**
- **No perks:** the office comes with **no salary, no residence and no property**, and is meant to be "distant from opportunities for personal or family enrichment." Any wealth, businesses or property within the walls a Lord has are their own.

### Lords vs. nobles
- **The heads of Waterdeep's noble families** (around 70; the number has varied over six centuries, and held at 76 for the first half of the 1300s) are colloquially called **"the Lords of Waterdeep."** Don't confuse them with the Masked Lords.
- **Masked Lords aren't nobles by definition**, and **no noble is automatically a lawmaking Lord** by birth or title.
- **A noble can be a Masked Lord, but rarely is.** Masked Lords traditionally see themselves as **defending the city against the haughty, corrupt abuses of born nobles**.
- **Some Masked Lords are paupers**, or have lowly jobs and status when unmasked.
- **To qualify** you must be a **citizen**: a property owner, or a registered renter and taxpayer. Living in the city is expected but not required.

### How Lords are chosen
- **Elected before they know it:**
  - The existing Lords **debate behind closed doors** and draw up a secret shortlist, formally called **"Our Chosen"** (not to be confused with the gods' Chosen).
  - The **Open Lord then approaches each candidate privately**, usually by inviting them to a social function at the palace, taking them into a private room, and asking.
  - **If they say yes, the Lords formally appoint them.**
- **Ed's comparison:** it works **like a closed members' club**, with blackballing and a preference for "folk like us."
- **Accepting is always voluntary**, though life sometimes applies heavy pressure to accept or to refuse.
- **If the Masked Lords were all wiped out**, a set of survivors would pick the replacements:
  - certain **guildmasters**, notably the head of the **Watchful Order**;
  - the heads of **12 specific noble families**;
  - the **Open Lord**;
  - the **Lord Mage (the Blackstaff)**.
  
  Their list is jokingly called the **"snort list"**, after the derisive snorts that greet unpopular names. **This procedure has never been used.**

### The masks
- **A full, helm-like head-covering mask** of a particular design with **minor magical powers**.
- **Worn whenever appearing as a Lord in public**, including open council meetings, and **usually at closed** (decision-making) meetings. **Not wearing it doesn't stop a Lord from speaking or voting.**

### Nobles' privileges
- **Trial by Lords:** a noble charged with a crime may demand **trial by a jury of Masked Lords** (or all available Lords) instead of a magister. In practice, though not by law, **almost every sentence becomes a fine** instead of the harsher penalties on the books.
- **Guild disputes:** nobles may appeal to the Masked Lords to judge disputes with guilds.
- **Access to the Open Lord:** nobles have the right to a **"prompt" private audience** with the Open Lord, except in war or dire emergency.

## Part two: who they really are

### Ordinary people
- **Many people gamers have heard of were secretly Masked Lords**, in the 1350s–60s and a few in the 1490s *Dragon Heist* era. They come across as everyday folk:
  - **shopkeepers**;
  - **craft workers** who make fine things;
  - **merchant traders and middlemen**, like the one who sells you ship space and slips some bad cargo in with your good.
- **Not** senior guild members, not guildmasters, and not nobles.
- **The occasional rebel young noble:** a third or fourth child at odds with their parents, recruited to **spy on what nobles say at their revels** and report back. Once they start acting like nobles ("we're the rightful rulers"), they're no longer useful, and they're **quietly told to leave**.
- **Most Lords aren't career politicians.** They tend to serve **a couple of years** to push one law through because they dislike where things are heading. Examples:
  - **Samoreza Sulfontis** *(spelling uncertain)*, a famous trader of the 1350s;
  - **Brian the Swordmaster**, in the 1300s.

### Mirt: the not-so-masked Lord
- **In the 1350s, Mirt was a Masked Lord and didn't care who knew.**
  - People assumed he was bluffing until they saw that when they told him what they wanted decided, **the Lords decided it at their next meeting**.
  - That made him **a conduit and safety valve**. People couldn't tell the Open Lord (Piergeiron, then) that the Lords were full of it, but they could tell Mirt. He'd report back: "Out in the streets they're saying you eat babies. Want to keep that reputation?"
- **Then he was caught in the blue fire** (the Spellplague), and **a century passed** before he came back out.
  - **As far as he's concerned he's still a Masked Lord.** No one ever stripped him of it.
- **His mansion became the Open Lord's home.**
  - Presuming him dead, the city had made his mansion **the Open Lord's residence**, so the Open Lord doesn't live in the palace.
  - That has a convenient side effect: the Lords can **meet without the Open Lord present** when they want to discuss her.
  - Ed tells the scene of Mirt coming home, stripping for a bath, and finding Laeral's things where his favorite robe should be.
- **Now:** the current Lords are of two minds about this crazy old man they don't know. But **Laeral likes him** and works hand in glove with him, using him as an **overweight, wheezing, elderly James Bond**.

### Why the Lords chose Laeral, and what they got
- **After they ousted Dagult**, there was **potential war with Neverwinter**, and they wanted to avoid it.
- So they begged **Laeral** to take the post: **everyone knows she's a Chosen of Mystra**, and she's striking, so people would treat the Open Lord with respect.
- **Then she made Elminster her head of secret police and security**, and **Mirt her secret agent**.
  - The Lords realized they'd **invited a dragon into their midst**: an iron fist in a velvet glove. So they **treat Mirt with care**.
- **Mirt as the bull in the china shop:**
  - He can walk into their meeting, call them dolts, and tell them to do the unpleasant thing first and get it done tonight. They don't like it, but they're **starting to see how useful it is**.
  - **If you want the china shop shattered, you send in the bull.** He's a lot of fun for a DM to run.

### Keep their identities secret
- **Ed's design advice:**
  - Two or three known Lords are useful.
  - But **always leave a voting minority, maybe a majority, of Masked Lords unknown and up to the DM**.
- **The reason:** if players know who the Lords are, they can **bully, kill or kidnap them** to swing votes, and that ruins the fun, like solving a murder mystery by reading minds. Kept mysterious, players have to **roleplay and tread carefully**.
- **They can be any age, gender or race**, but they favor "folk like us": wealthy, long-established Waterdhavians. **Good luck getting a mind flayer or a drow elected.**

### They fight like cats and dogs
- **Don't mistake one voice for agreement.** Because they speak through the Open Lord, outsiders think the Lords are of one mind. Behind closed doors **they fight like cats and dogs**.
- **The fighting keeps nepotism down.** "Your niece? Not unless two of mine get in too." They **bring in friends, but rarely close relatives**. If a Lord is accused behind closed doors of bringing in a sixteenth cousin, the best defense is to claim they can't keep track of who's related to whom.
- **That's what Ahghairon wanted:** the **street sweeper and the dung carter** seated next to the wealthy would-be noble, arguing over policy like a squabbling royal council.
- **They never come to open blows.** That's another reason they chose Laeral: if a closed meeting ever turned into a brawl, she could **freeze them in their chairs** until they agreed not to draw steel.

### A roleplaying hook
- **Masked Lords who fear assassination hire the player characters as stand-ins**, magically disguised to look like them, to **attend meetings for a tenday and vote as a bloc**.
- Ed adds that roleplaying these debates is practice for real life: town councils, city halls, boardrooms.

### Do they favor the rich?
- **Yes, they tend to favor the status quo**, and therefore whoever is on top now: the noble, the wealthy, or both.
- **The closed-door fights are the counterweight.** In Ed's home-campaign Waterdeep, three camps clash, and none of them is united:
  - **the nobles**, whose feuds among themselves are the bitterest of all;
  - **the guildmasters**, who quarrel over whose trade belongs to which guild;
  - **would-be nobles and merchant princes**, who don't want the guilds blocking them. Ed's real-world comparison: tycoons who invest in everything and try to change the rules of whole industries, up against entrenched unions.
- **Ahghairon's rule:** make sure the table includes a **dung carter, a beggar and a street prostitute**. They will look across at a Lord with rings on every finger and **block his scheme to enrich himself further**, because it's time the mean streets got a square meal.
- **These fights never stop. It's one reason Laeral looks older by the day.**

## Secret plots: the metaplot
- **From Ahghairon to the present**, the Masked Lords have repeatedly worked **behind the scenes, without anyone seeing their hand**, to:
  - **pick guildmasters**;
  - **dismiss or disgrace guildmasters** until they lose power in their guild;
  - **do the same to the heads of noble houses** whose personal habits made them a danger to their fellow citizens, or who were simply **rapacious**: the kind who love to bankrupt someone and seize their property.
- **How they do it:** **never openly**. They manipulate events so the target's plans go wrong, cost a fortune, and **backfire every time** the target tries to destroy someone.
- **Their favorite tool:** they **dupe, manipulate or hire outside adventurers** (the player characters, say) to get in the way of a guildmaster, a house head, or a **misbehaving black-sheep noble**, wittingly or not.
- **Ed calls this "meat and potatoes" material** you can build a whole campaign around.
- **The local saying:** it's time for someone "to get their **codpiece caught in the dung cart**." It comes from a portly noble who tried to leap a dung cart to escape pursuers, fell in, and hung there **stuck by his codpiece**.

## Realms Speak: Sschindylryn
**Sschindylryn** is a famous **drow city in a pyramid-shaped cavern**. Ed says the name exactly as spelled.

## How this fits the vault (Brodie decides)
- **An intrigue toolkit:** the Masked Lords' methods could be copied anywhere, Neverwinter included:
  - secret selection;
  - closed-door fights over status quo vs. the streets;
  - quietly breaking corrupt guildmasters and noble heads;
  - using adventurers as deniable tools.
- **War with Neverwinter:** Ed says ousting Dagult raised the threat of **war between Waterdeep and Neverwinter**. That fits the vault's 1489–1493 years, when Dagult "barely held onto" Neverwinter (see [[NPCs/Dagult Neverember]], [[TIMELINE]]).
- **Ed differs from the vault's Waterdeep note:** [[Locations/Waterdeep]] says the Masked Lords **know each other's identities**. Ed says the **Open Lord** knows them, and Lords **usually share only with close friends** who are fellow Lords.
- **The ouster date:** this video says the Lords ousted Dagult in **1489**, matching the vault. Ed's Laeral video says **late 1488** (see [[NPCs/Laeral Silverhand]]).
- **Manshoon** was blackmailing two Masked Lords in 1492 (see [[NPCs/Manshoon]]). That's exactly the pressure the masks are meant to prevent.
- **"Good luck electing a mind flayer"**: House Cyl's flayer controls Neverwinter nobles, not Waterdeep Lords (see [[Factions/House Cyl]]).
- **Mirt** also toured Ormpur with Volo (see [[NPCs/Volothamp Geddarm (Volo)]]).
- **No clashes** beyond the identity point, and no movie lore.
