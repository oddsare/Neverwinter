# Lolth and the Drow (MrRhexx, part 1)

How Lolth's faith shaped the drow. Summarized in my own words. Part 2 (her stats and realm): [[Lore/MrRhexx - Lolth's Power and the Demonweb]]. **Campaign connections are open threads.**

## Who she is
- **Cruel and capricious**, thought insane by many because she **pits her own worshippers against each other**. Malicious in her dealings, coldly vicious in a fight. She covets the power of the surface races' gods.
- She can be kind to those she fancies, but thrives on death, destruction and torture, including of her own followers who displease her.
- **Even her most devout clerics hate and fear her**, worshipping her only for the power she grants.
- **Lolth and the drow made each other:** she is what she is because of them, and they are what they are because of her.

## Only Lolth
- The drow have a pantheon (the **Dark Seldarine**), but they **don't treat those gods as true gods**, only as **Lolth's servants**, or her **divine children**. **Lolth is the only one** they fear and respect.
- **Comparison with other races:**
  - **Orcs (Gruumsh)** and **goblins (Maglubiyet)** are dominated by their gods but still have pantheons with differing views.
  - **Kobolds** are closest: their whole life revolves around **Kurtulmak**, a lesser god. The difference: **kobolds venerate their god; drow fear theirs.**
- **The god's personality becomes the culture of the race.**

## How the faith works
- **Worship isn't voluntary.** The priestesses **rule drow society** and enforce Lolth's worship, often making those who skip the rites the **next sacrifice**. They rarely need to: most drow take part willingly, from **reverence mixed with terror**.
- **Living sacrifice** is central to all but the smallest rites. Lolth watches, Lolth tests, Lolth consumes.
- **Her preferences:** sentient over non-sentient, humanoid over not, **drow over elves**, powerful over weak, and **her own priestesses above all**. Sacrificing her most powerful, devout servant would delight her.
- So the church protects itself: priestesses rarely sacrifice each other (though infighting happens), and instead **sacrifice rule-breakers**, using Lolth's appetite as a way to **control the community**. Ordinary rites use **slaves**.
- **Never ask Lolth for help.** It's weakness and an insult; you're more likely to get a **disease** than aid. **Every prayer is a request to be tested.** Drow scouts meeting enemies say "test me, Lolth" in Elvish.
- **All prayers are in Elvish.** Devotions must not use other races' words. Undercommon is fine for everyday talk, but drow are proud elves.

## Lolth's tests
- Unremarkable drow may never get one; those in power get **one or two in a lifetime**; priestesses and matriarchs are tested **regularly**.
- **Failing usually means death**; a significant minority are instead **turned into driders**.
- **Test of Loyalty:** she tells your friend, family member or lover that you betrayed her and must be ambushed and killed. As they strike, she sends you a vision: it's a test, and you must **survive the ambush and kill them**.
- **Test of Lies:** convince someone she chooses of particular falsehoods, possibly under **torture or threats**.
- **Test of Mettle:** she summons **venomous spiders** in and around your body. Survive the bites and get them off **without harming a single spider**.
- Tests come **at random** for anyone in power. Passing brings **prestige, power or her personal blessing**.
- **The point:** keep the drow afraid and keep her grip iron-tight. The church doesn't counsel anyone through crises; its only jobs are to **make drow perform the rites** and **enforce her rules**.

## Her presence
- **Unusually active** for a god. Curse her within a believer's hearing or near a temple and she'll likely **strike you down on the spot**. Her **avatars attend** major sacrifices (of a high priestess, say). High-level drow **interact with her directly**.
- **Forms:**
  - a giant **black widow** with crimson eyes
  - an exquisitely beautiful human-sized **drow woman**, often clothed in clinging spiders or artful drow chainmail
  - a giant spider with a beautiful drow woman's head, found in the Abyss and thought to be her **true form**
- **Mostly she watches** from the shadows, enjoying the struggle, rather than getting into the thick of things.
- **Signs she's watching:**
  - **smirking lips** appear on a nearby spider, which glows with **flickering purple faerie fire**
  - with no spider present, a **giant smiling spider-shaped shadow**
- **Purple fire:** a unique flame she sometimes grants clerics in dire need, but more often uses to **punish and humiliate** priestesses who fail her or botch a ritual. It spreads from the ritual and **burns away their clothes and magic items** without hurting them, shaming them in front of others. That's part of why major rites are performed **naked**, and why drow fashion runs extremely revealing.
- **Faerie fire** being innate to all drow comes partly from their link to Lolth.

## Matriarchy
- **Men can't be her clerics.** Lolth considers males inferior, so **women run drow society**; a man's best route to power is being a powerful woman's lover.
- **Lolth's Meld** (6th-level clerical spell): a priestess **binds a male drow like a familiar**: she sees through his eyes, reads his thoughts, speaks telepathically, **controls his body** at any distance, and **casts spells through him**. His life is worth less than hers, so she'll take him into battle.
- (Rhexx notes drow culture's wild orgies, including with summoned demons, and leaves the rest for a future video.)

## Yochlols
- Lolth's **handmaiden demons**, made and controlled by her in her Abyssal realm. The word of one is treated as **her word**.
- Summoned through a big **brazier of burning oils** made of black stone (onyx or obsidian). The flames become a **gate to her layer**, and the yochlol stays **only while the fire burns**.
- Shapechangers who usually take **drow women's** form on the Material Plane.
- **The church teaches that priestesses become yochlols when they die**, the drow's equivalent of angels.

## Her dogma (paraphrased)
- **Fear is as strong as steel; love and respect are soft and useless.**
- Convert or destroy any drow who don't worship her. Weed out the weak and rebellious. Kill those who abandon the faith.
- Males serve. **Priests of other races** who act outside her will are to be **sacrificed**. Eliminate the faithful whose loyalty is weak.
- Raise children as her worshippers; **each family should produce at least one priestess** who serves better than her parents.
- **Revere spiders. Anyone who harms one must die.**

**In practice:**
- Most of what she rewards or punishes is **left unsaid**. Priests who follow commandments blindly are on a slippery path to a quick death.
- **Ambition over loyalty:** a **successful betrayal** shines brightest in her eyes, as does setting yourself hard tests and passing them. One of her most trusted servants left her to serve another demon lord, **betrayed that lord for Lolth's benefit**, and was promoted. But get too cute and she'll smite you.
- **Sins:** questioning her motives or wisdom; **helping non-drow against drow** (a great sin); ignoring her commands.
- She often tests favor by **ordering the sacrifice of a favorite consort**.
- **Drow who lose her favor get one chance to redeem themselves**, usually a dangerous mission. Sometimes she sets no task and just watches. Willful failure means destruction; failure by bad luck or poor planning usually means becoming a **drider**.
- She plays favorites, but **favorites fall without warning**.

## A drow city's laws (Menzoberranzan's seven decrees, paraphrased)
1. **No god but Lolth.** Anyone following another power's dictates is killed, preferably as her sacrifice.
2. **Worship of any other entity is forbidden** in the city. Non-drow offenders are fined and expelled; repeat offenders, and any drow, are killed.
3. **Slaves have no rights.** No limit on their duties or punishments; refusing any order from their owning house is a capital offense.
4. **A commoner or student who disobeys a priestess** can be punished as she sees fit, even with death. If the offender belongs to another house and its noble objects, the two agree on a punishment, usually flogging.
5. **Wearing another house's colors, or disguising yourself as a different station, is death.**
6. **A non-drow impersonating a particular drow**, a noble, or a member of another house is killed.
7. **If two or more houses attack another house** and it's proven, **all the attackers are destroyed** by the rest.

## Can drow be good? (canon)
- Rhexx's blunt take: **the vast majority of drow are evil**. The drow we meet on the surface, mostly player characters, behave differently.
- **Canon (*Drow of the Underdark*, p. 15) gives two views, and never settles it:**
  - **Majority:** after Lolth's influence and countless generations of violence, drow attitudes are now **innate**. A drow raised by kind humans or elves would still grow up a calculating, violent manipulator.
  - **Minority:** drow are like **abused children** perpetuating a cycle. Raised away from Lolth and their culture, they **could be very different**. Unlikely to be tested soon.
- **A typical drow city** (canon description, paraphrased): treachery, murder and debauchery are common; the populace are capricious sadists who rise through others' suffering. Every drow sorts everyone into three groups: **those above** (to be appeased), **those useful** (to be exploited), and **the weak** (worthless except as leverage or fodder). Every day is a chance to rise or remove a rival.
- **Drow psychology (canon):**
  - A drow's sense of worth comes from her **status**. Without power she's a failure in her own eyes, and that need is **close to instinct**.
  - Drow are primal; they came from spirits born of a god of chaos and change. **Pride and anger come more easily** than to other races. Pride seems malleable; **anger seems innate**. That doesn't force violence, but they feel anger more.
  - **A drow's brain struggles to treat anyone as an equal.** Every interaction is dominant vs. submissive, and unless shown otherwise she assumes she's dominant; disrespect brings pride and anger. Drow who deal with other races learn others don't think that way, but **never lose the urge to establish dominance**.
  - Their cruelty is often **thoughtless and effortless**, because they value subordinates only by usefulness.
- **But:** someone who feels superior and is prone to anger can still live a happy, successful, even good life. And it's **canon that many drow reject Lolth's evil** and **choose death rather than pass her tests**, especially tests that demand killing a spouse or friend. Those who won't adapt simply die, so more would resist if they weren't killed.

## Eilistraee
- **The one good god of the drow** (Rhexx's favorite). She fought Lolth when Lolth betrayed Corellon. When the dark elves were banished, she **chose exile with them** even though Corellon wanted her to stay, believing the drow would need her light.
- She guides drow who reject Lolth toward a better life on the surface; they hear her music and see her light leading them to the moonlight.
- Where Lolth demands sacrifice, betrayal and obedience, **Eilistraee asks the drow to dance freely in the moonlight**, setting aside armor and weapons.
