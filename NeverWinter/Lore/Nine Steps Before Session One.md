# Nine Steps Before Session One

A DM's prep order for a new campaign, from a YouTube video, summarized in my own words. Transcript added 2026-10-23. This is DM advice, not Realms canon. The sponsor segment (a scene-description tool) is left out.

## The nine steps, in order
1. **Establish the setting, and keep it local.** Know the wider world, but a whole campaign can fit in about a 50-mile radius. A detailed local area feels more real than dragging players through biome after biome. People talk about their neighbors and the farm down the road, not a kingdom two countries over.
2. **Build characters with the players.** Some players give a lot of backstory and some give nothing. Both are fine, because blank space is room to connect everyone to one story. Ask what they want included, and what they *don't* want to know (for example, whether a lost loved one is alive). List what you know so you can see what's still unfilled.
3. **Figure out the end.** Decide where you'd like it to lead, knowing it will change. Optionally tie one or two backstories to a villain: a minor one for early hooks, or the main villain. This is also when you pick the kind of villain (demons, devils, ghosts, eldritch beings). Keeping backstories separate from the main plot is fine too, but it means more sessions.
4. **Set up a flowchart of moments and beats.** It can be as simple as "left path, right path" or a web of threads, quests, people, side quests and planned encounters. The point is a system for knowing where the story is and where the emotional moments go. At minimum: where the players start, the end, and room in between.
5. **Set goals and challenges for each character.** Growth gets you from start to end, and growth comes through failure, loss and pain. Ask what each character **wants**, what they **need** (often in conflict, like an addict who wants the drug but needs to get clean) and what they **fear**. Then build challenges that push against those.
6. **Build the first milestones.** You don't need each session's ending, just the next point to reach. Direction matters more than prep or notes. A DM who only asks "what do you want to do?" will eventually get a silent table. Milestones double as emotional beats: by each one, a character, the world or the story has changed. Keep swinging between fun and danger, with each high more intense until the finale.
7. **Pick the tone and write the intro bullets.** Genre and feel (sci-fi, gritty, Western, high fantasy), drawing on shows and films. The video's author prefers bullets they'll cover in session one over read-aloud text, which they find disconnecting. Examples: the floating pyramid, cold winters, fog, huge evergreens, crumbling walls, an old city inside the original wall and a newer one outside it.
8. **Do the setup last.** Music, maps, notes, monster tokens. None of it happens until the story is known, sometimes the day before.
9. **Run a session zero.** The author calls it the most important step. Set expectations and content limits, whether with a formal safety system or a loose Q&A. Answer world and character questions. It also tests fit, especially with strangers from online groups. They often run a quick 1–2 hour one-shot with standalone characters, so everyone gets a feel for the world and each other's styles, and problems like phones or disruptive play show up early.
