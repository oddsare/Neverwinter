# Neverwinter session prep

Brodie runs a D&D 5e campaign in Neverwinter (Forgotten Realms, 1505 DR). The vault in `NeverWinter/` is his whole campaign binder; Claude keeps it organized. Read `CLAUDE.md` first: its rules always apply (address him as Brodie, don't invent content, don't connect threads unless told, no tier wording).

Method reference: `NeverWinter/Lore/Enter the Dungeon - Plot Webs and Session Notes.md`.

## The rules for session notes
- **Brodie reads these at the table.** Put in only what he'll use. No background, no options he didn't pick, nothing "just in case." Short, plain sentences.
- **Build each section with him, one at a time.** Ask, write his answer in his words, show it, move on. Don't fill sections ahead of him.
- **Ideas only when he asks for them.** Mark Claude's ideas 💡 until he picks one; once picked, drop the marker and the options he didn't pick.
- **Links only where they help him find something fast.**
- **Never put Claude's commentary, sourcing notes or "where this came from" in a session note.** If he asks where something came from, answer in chat.

## Prepping a session
1. Copy `NeverWinter/_Templates/Session Template.md` to `NeverWinter/Sessions/Session N.md`.
2. Go section by section in the template's order: Outline, Moments (start, each moment with its fight inside, stock cliffhanger), Character moments, Location details, People, Plotlines.
3. **Plotlines:** what each faction is doing right now, as if the party didn't exist. Pull from existing notes; one line each.
4. **Character moments:** come from the plot web and the PCs' backstories.
5. Before finishing, check the note against existing notes and tell Brodie about any conflict. Don't fix it silently.

## After a session
1. Brodie gives his notes in any form (rough bullets, voice-to-text, a paste).
2. Write the **Log** in that session's note: what happened, what the players did, what changed. His words, tidied, not embellished.
3. Write **Next session**: the threads left open.
4. Update the notes that changed (an NPC died, a place was found, a faction moved) and the plot web. List every change for him.
5. Record new decisions in `NeverWinter/Campaign Decisions.md` and the handoff.

## The plot web
- **Center:** the main villain, the central tension and the theme. Brodie decides these.
- **Nodes:** each PC backstory broken into pieces (names, places, groups, events), plus the campaign's factions and NPCs.
- **Connections:** only ones Brodie makes or approves. Leave gaps; fill them in over time.
- **Backstory secrets:** what a player wrote is what the character knows. What's really behind it is Brodie's call; keep secrets out of anything players see.
- **Reading it:** the most-connected nodes matter most; every PC should reach the center; the closest node to where the party is now is the likeliest next step.
- **Keep it lean:** about 6–7 nodes per PC. Concepts (ideologies, beliefs) make the best nodes; thematic "rhymes" count as connections; leave some nodes blank on purpose.
- **When a PC dies or leaves:** add nodes, don't remove the old ones. Ask Brodie how much the new PC should tie to the old.
- **Changing the villain or central tension:** only when Brodie decides. Go through gray areas (nodes the players touched but know little about), add as few nodes as possible, and keep several routes to the truth.
- **In prep:** check which important nodes need seeding or support, and suggest small touches for the central tension. Mark them 💡.
- After each session, update which nodes the party has uncovered.

## Arcs
- **Campaign arc:** the whole story. **Narrative arc:** about 9–10 sessions. **Immediate arc:** what's happening now.
- Start low-stakes while a new group gels.
